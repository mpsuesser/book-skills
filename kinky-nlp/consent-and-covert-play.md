# Consent with NLP, and consensual covert hypnosis

## The obvious: "Don't Be an Asshole"

The two marks of being an asshole are **lying** (misleading or omitting, including hiding information to get what you want or having ulterior motives) and **coercion** (applying pressure to get someone to act as you want, either directly or indirectly through social norms or the sense that they will let you or themselves down). You might be an asshole if you are:

- coercing someone with NLP ideas, wordplay or techniques without consent or disclosure;
- inducing trance or altered states in someone without consent or disclosure;
- going against someone's desires or consent.

## The nuanced

Hypnosis, NLP, kink and consent are not black and white. Ethics offer options and ways to think; they can't prescribe one correct way. Sexual-ethics models aren't one-size-fits-all. People's needs for structure and comfort vary widely. Tangible activities (sex acts, impact) are easier to negotiate than talk-and-listen play. The models themselves keep evolving: "enthusiastic consent" is now being challenged so that it doesn't invalidate consent that is valid but less than enthusiastic. Practical skill in making consent conversations easy to engage in, and easy to disengage from, matters more than theory. NLP is a way to communicate in a more controlled way, which is exactly what this needs.

## Before negotiation: flirtation and escalation

Negotiation runs from explicit **opt-in** (agree on what is acceptable) to **opt-out** (focus on limits), and most people blend the two. Partners should have a meta conversation about what they need, including what they need from negotiation itself. Before any of that comes the **gray zone**: the flirting that leads to those conversations, where formal questions would be strange ("Can I flirt with you?"). It runs on nonverbal and indirect communication. Forward, direct advances read as off-putting or creepy early on; a no-pressure environment and gentle, subtle interest build connection.

**Escalation** is any move to make an interaction more intimate or intense: compliments, sustained eye contact, playful coyness, open body language, "Would you like to play?" **De-escalation** leans away from intimacy or intensity. The governing question: "How do we flirt and escalate in ways that allow a person to disengage or deescalate easily, and without pressure?"

**Askers and guessers.** This is a non-scientific pop-psychology distinction. An "asker" requests directly ("Could you give me a ride home after the party?") and tends to be comfortable giving and receiving "no". A "guesser" invites indirectly ("Oh, I don't have a ride home after the party, I could Uber or something...") and only raises it when a yes seems likely. Most people are both at different times. Kink culture pushes toward asking, which helps normalize "no". Relying on it entirely ignores how hard direct questions are when the request is personal, intimate, and loaded with circumstantial power imbalance. The point isn't to type people. It is that guess-style, indirect speech can make kinky communication *easier*.

## Indirect check-ins

Direct check-ins ("Are you OK with this?") pressure the person asked, because it is easier to avoid the confrontation of saying "no" even while discomfort grows. The goal: **"The person should need to take more action to say 'yes,' rather than taking more action to say 'no.'"** Use this before, during and after play. Before each check-in, ask yourself two questions:

1. **"Is my message an escalation, or de-escalation?"** "Would you like to do bimbofication?" looks neutral but is an escalation: it brings in something new and bigger.
2. **"What kind of responsibility am I putting on my partner?"** An escalating question makes them responsible for either flowing along with yes or producing a difficult no. Offer de-escalating questions, where yes means slowing or stopping: "Would you like to slow down a little bit?"

**Statements instead of questions.** Give your own position and let them opt in.

- At a play party, after some positive flirting, say "I don't have any plans to play with anyone tonight." It allows any response and leaves the next move to them. Without the prior flirting it can read as passive aggressive; the form is similar, but the intention and purpose differ.
- "We can stop here if you'd like" is an easier out than "Would you like to stop here?", because declining the stop takes more action than accepting it.
- "I've really been thinking a lot about doing doll play" is easier to engage or ignore than "I'm interested in doing some doll play."
- Offer outs as statements ("I'm OK if we slow down", "We can do something else if you'd prefer"). Stating them shows the idea came from you and sits in your comfort zone, so they needn't feel bad about taking it.

**Double not-binds.** Borrow the double bind, but make sure one option de-escalates. Instead of "Do you want to do a small scene?", say "We could do a little play, or we could just chat for a bit." "A bind is a bind", so include an easy option, as in "Would you like a hug, or a handshake?" Better still, include a complete exit: "We could do a little play, or we could chat a bit more, or we could each go off on our own. It's all good." Other outs: take a break, revisit this later, wind down, go mingle. **Asking for a color** in the stoplight safeword system is the same structure. Its unspoken question is "Are you OK, or do we need to re-evaluate?", which is preferable to a bare "Are you OK?" You can pose that form even without the stoplight system.

## Discerning wants and needs

No step-by-step method guarantees good negotiation. Apply map/territory: meet the partner at their map, and give them room to understand yours. Even in short-term or pick-up play, learn a little about who they are and be forthcoming about your own interests. Ask *why* they want to play with you, and where the desire comes from. If they want you to control them, find out what they imagine when they think "control". Words like "control" are ambiguous and rich, and are defined by personal history. Being "informed" isn't about objective reality either: "amnesia", for example, is a nebulous concept that two partners may picture very differently, so clarify it.

**Indirect meta model questioning.** Don't aggressively clarify. Hold a real conversation and listen for the deep structure, for what has been generalized, deleted or distorted. "I really find the idea of mindlessness hot" raises questions: What does mindlessness entail for them? How do they know it is hot, and why? Where did the interest come from? Make it give and take, so it doesn't become an interview or an interrogation. Offering your own view also models how to talk about desire: "I also really love the idea of mindlessness; there's something about the fantasy of someone just really unable to think for themselves. I think it's about power?" That invites them in, and a "What about you?" is optional. You clarify their model without explicit meta-model questions.

## Covert hypnosis, consensually

Everything here assumes covert play has been established as consensual beforehand; nonconsensual covert hypnosis is squarely asshole territory. There are three structures for consent:

- **Bounded consent**: covert play is allowed in a defined situation only. That can be a signal item they choose to wear (clothing, jewelry), a time frame ("for the rest of the night", "on our date next weekend"), or a place ("in this room at the play party", "if I'm sitting on that couch", "in this chatroom").
- **Blanket consent**: covert play is allowed by the nature of the relationship, without per-instance negotiation. It is safer for partners with enough history to read each other's responses and signals. A safeword is usually good practice.
- **Amnesia**: consent is given, then the memory of the negotiation is obscured, by direct suggestion or by waiting until memory naturally fades. Avoid leaving them genuinely feeling unsafe. Suggest that even if they don't remember giving consent, they know on some level that they are OK, and will remember the consent later.

**The goal shapes the method.** The covert fantasy, with its unsuspecting and none-the-wiser subject, is built for an observer or voyeur. A subject kept fully in the dark from start to finish never gets the "appreciation" moment, and may even end up disappointed, waiting for a scene that already happened. So covert play is a juxtaposition. It is always overt on some level (consent was given). It has to stay covert long enough to fly under the radar. And it has to be revealed eventually to have impact. This is complicated by the subject's reflexive check: "Oh, are they covertly messing with me right now?"

**Roleplaying covert play** is simpler. Both partners pretend it is covert and enjoy the fantasy as a fantasy, so both are observers. Here, make the patter *conspicuous*: switch tones deliberately, and use presuppositions and other patterns obviously, so the sneakiness can be appreciated.

**Hypnosis versus suggestions versus change.** Find out which one excites the two of you, because each is done differently:

- an induced state nobody noticed (the focus is the sinking itself);
- suggested responses that arrive spontaneously;
- changes in personality or behavior, which can happen with or without hypnosis.

Trance is not what makes suggestions work. "Responsiveness is much more tied to expectation, role-enactment, and other psychological principles", so covert play doesn't need a covert induction to produce effects.

### Methods

**Stop signaling.** Experienced subjects recognize play from changes in their own state, and from the hypnotist's flags:

- tone of voice and manner of speech (more or less direct, certain phrases);
- microexpressions and body language;
- the way you look at them;
- a setting where play is expected (a date, an event, an agreed time).

Flagging is useful, and becomes part of a couple's relationship grammar, but covert play needs less of it. Study how your behavior changes when you hypnotize. If you play long-distance, record a session and review it. Then practice with no signal at all: your normal speaking voice, casual body language, none of the intense eye contact hypnosis leads people to expect. NLP's indirect suggestion is not the same as real conversational speech. "If it doesn't sound like something you'd normally say, it's not going to sound natural." A conversation loaded with presuppositions, tag questions and Milton patterns sounds like a caricature of a sneaky hypnotist. Use them sparingly, in your natural voice, and misdirect from them by putting the tonal emphasis on another part of the sentence. Many people slip into almost a different dialect to hypnotize. Find where your ordinary speech is already suggestive.

**Inducing without "hypnosis."** Even indirect patter flags itself. "Maybe there's a little part of you that's already noticing the way you feel as you relax" carries "relax" and "noticing", words already bound to hypnosis. Lean instead on spontaneous trance states: metaphor, future pacing, anchoring, inward looking and imagining, without invoking hypnosis.

- **Recall.** "Oh man, do you remember when we went to the beach last month? You were so burned out..." Recall is naturally hypnotic and brings up sensory and emotional memory. Let them talk ("I need another vacation"), then steer: "I remember that we had really amazing sex when we got home. You were so into it." With their brain already in the memory, arousal can come up vivid.
- **Memories as anchors.** Having someone reminisce about a past trance is a known induction. Memories work as anchors, an association between the memory's "hallucination" and the feeling. The same holds for metaphors and imagined futures: any time someone constructs something in their mind, they are hallucinating an experience. "All states of processing are hypnotic or hypnotically useful in some way."

**Covert suggestions.** "Trance is all suggestion": the induction/suggestion distinction is false, except that some suggestions change a state and others elicit a response. Responding to suggestion is about how someone is *focusing*. Direct their focus to stir up associations they already have.

- **Dumbness**: talk confusingly, ask questions they can't answer, and use the memories, words, bodily actions and emotions they already tie to feeling dumb.
- **Pleasure**: ask yourself where their focus goes when they feel pleasure without hypnosis. On parts of their body? An exciting fantasy or memory? Intensely on what you're doing in the moment? What does their body do: tense or relax, move or go still, touch themselves or you? Follow the patterns they already run.

Covertness comes from riding the focus shifts that occur naturally in conversation. Practice by noticing your own shifts while you talk.

**Nonverbal methods.** Touch is subtler than words when done mindfully.

- **Anchoring through touch** (pairing, as in classical conditioning): during or just before a desired response, touch them in a particular way or place. Stroke down their arm or squeeze their hand while they are very turned on, or use a more intimate touch. Repeat it, and the touch will bring back at least the memory of that feeling.
- **Symbolic touch**: "If I want my partner to go into trance, how can I represent that through touch?" Use downward motion, rocking the body, controlling the head. Catalepsy comes easily: holding an arm in position signals it to stay, often without their awareness.
- **Mirroring and matching** posture or breathing pace builds comfort, rapport and focus. Keep it to small movements, because obvious mirroring distracts.

**Keep them from noticing trance.** Experienced subjects know their signs. Work in states that don't feel like erotic hypnosis, staying within what is familiar from their ordinary processing. Learn what your partner associates with hypnosis so you can avoid it:

- your cues;
- environmental cues (a negotiated scene, being in bed together);
- internal cues (body heaviness or lightness, eye fluttering, a slack jaw, thoughts slowing or changing).

Since hypnosis is associated with relaxation, aim for trance that is engaged but not lethargic. Point their focus outside themselves and away from their body and mental responses. Go gradually. Speed is thrilling, but slow shifts are harder to catch when they compare how they feel now with a few moments ago, especially where trance is expected (a play party, bed, after negotiation). Over time you can train a partner to be blind to trance, questioning their state less and growing unsure what trance even feels like. That is an exciting play of power, but it may call for more convincers and reassurance when trance does arrive.

**Covert change** (of behaviors, thought patterns, personality) needn't rely on trance or on anything recognizable as hypnosis; simple conditioning works too. Go slowly. Slow change is usually visible only in retrospect, like acquiring a taste for new music. Watch for **milestone** moments (the first time they engage with the new thing, the first time they seek it out unprompted), which are hard to fully suppress. To manage them:

- Loosen their hold on the original opinion: "Didn't you always kind of like that, in a way?"
- Blur memory generally: "I find it so hard to remember what I was like a year ago..."
- Suppress the milestones by suggesting the changes just feel natural: "You actually have a lot of qualities of someone who just goes with the flow..."

### The payoff

The **payoff** is the moment a person realizes they have been manipulated. It is "arguably the most important part of covert hypnosis", because it carries the scene's full eroticism. Mark it and emphasize it: "Do you realize you've been in trance for the last ten minutes? Isn't that just amazing?" They will abruptly re-evaluate their state against how they felt before, so use that to make the trance or the change concrete: "You can feel it, right? You can feel it all catching up to you?"

Success doesn't require that they never noticed, or that you are the one to reveal it. Experienced partners will sometimes catch it. The potency is in the surprise, and in having had no chance to react: "Oh, did you just notice? And there really wasn't anything you could do about it, was there?" So don't suppress their reaction to noticing; it is reinforcement. There can be a series of payoffs. With covert change in particular, each time they notice their progress it feeds the change and proves it is real. Behavioral shifts are felt only when the person runs into them, so play up each encounter to keep the excitement alive.
