# Kinky NLP

sleepingirl's book takes neuro-linguistic programming (NLP), which was built to make therapy work and was later bent toward sales and seduction, and translates it for the erotic hypnotist/subject partnership. NLP is "a kind of philosophy towards human communication": it treats language as the window into how a person processes the world, and treats language as the lever for changing that processing. The book keeps the parts that hold up, names the parts that don't, and re-aims everything from helping or persuading to mutually enjoyable hypnokink: "We aren't looking to 'unlock the unconscious' to solve problems, we are looking to discover the depths of our partners to fulfil desires."

The book's stance on evidence: "It's OK to label NLP as pseudoscience, but the key is that that doesn't invalidate it." Hypnosis can't be fully tested, so the hypnotist works from logic and from a thorough understanding of how their partner's brain works in and around trance and language. Every model here is a **model**: a representation with boundaries, useful and therefore limiting. NLP set out to be a "model of models" (hence *meta* model); something that tries to unify frameworks just ends up as one more distinct model.

## The core lens

- **The map is not the territory** (Korzybski). Nobody experiences the world as it is. Each person builds a **map**, a model of the world filtered through their history and perceptions, and no word, description or mental image *is* the thing it represents. Partners begin from impoverished, different maps. The hypnotist's work is to understand someone's model of the world, and then to expand it or meet it. A response shows you part of the map: tell a subject to act like a bimbo, and if they babble about shopping, you have learned what "bimbo" means to them, and you can use it.
- **Language as representation.** People show their maps through the language they use, so how someone talks always reveals how they process.
- **Generalization, deletion, distortion** are the three ways experience becomes incomplete. *Generalization* treats one experience as standing for a whole class. *Deletion* leaves part of the experience out. *Distortion* changes something's quality: misremembering a detail, or assuming a causality (the sense that hypnosis is sexual). None of the three is good or bad in itself; all three are normal processing and all three are hypnotic opportunities.
- **Deep structure / surface structure** (borrowed loosely from Chomsky). The surface structure is what is said; the deep structure is the fuller meaning underneath it. "I really want more brainwashing" hides a deep structure about who, when, how, and what "brainwashing" means to this person. A **transformation** is the change between the two, in the speaker's experience or in the listener's mind.
- **Two models, one goal.** The **meta model** clarifies incomplete language: it goes *top-down*, bringing things into focus. The **Milton model**, built from Erickson, deliberately creates generalization, deletion and distortion to induce trance: it goes *bottom-up*, creating fuzziness. Both aim to bring someone into deeper contact with parts of themselves they aren't aware of: resources, memories, experiences.
- **The hypnotist's loop.** "We hypnotize others by 1) understanding how someone is processing, and 2) using that knowledge to influence where they go." Every response you observe is new information, and information is options. The governing question is: *What choices are available to you?* A trance is a trip from point A to point B along a line of ideas shared with the subject, so you need links between what is being said now and what you want to say next.

## Pacing, leading and ambiguity

These are the engine under every technique in the book.

- **Pacing.** "A hypnotist has successfully paced a client verbally when the hypnotist's verbalizations are accepted by the client as an accurate description of the client's ongoing experience" (Bandler and Grinder). Throughout a trance, the subject is checking, often by gut: *Is this working? Am I in trance? Am I following correctly? Do I feel what they are telling me to feel?* Pacing makes those checks come up less often, or come back "yes". At its simplest, pacing is narrating what you observe ("your shoulders are starting to relax"). Over text or audio, infer what they are doing ("watching my words appear, reading almost automatically"). Pacing also works nonverbally: subtle matching of tone, posture and breathing. Bandler and Grinder call the hypnotist "a sophisticated bio-feedback mechanism."
- **Utilization** (Ericksonian). You observe all of the subject's messages and nuances, and you acknowledge and use them to keep the scene flowing. This ranges from an explicit suggestive remark ("I can tell that you're focusing deeply on me, which is perfect...") to a simple "Oh yes, just like that" at an eye flutter. It also takes in their whole map: their history, likes and hobbies, and what they've told you (an eye-contact lover, a music lover's musical metaphors). Information that matters to the subject gives your suggestions the same weight. Utilization shows attention, and feeling "known" or even "caught" shifts focus toward trance.
- **Yes sets, broadened.** The classic yes set (a run of questions with known "yes" answers) is of questionable efficacy, though fine as an induction. The principle worth keeping is that *agreement begets agreement*, and the yes need not be spoken. Following Georg Barkas's view of a rope scene as an "interview", every suggestion is a question and every response is an answer: a sigh, slumped shoulders, a gasp, stiffening, soft cursing, widened eyes, trance signs. "Every trance, from beginning to end, can be seen as a yes set."
- **Leading.** Leading is shifting from statements that are observably true to suggestions you *want* to be true. "Three truths and one suggestion" is the textbook form. No number of yeses tells you when to switch; read trance signs and participation, and watch how interspersed leads land. Pacing and leading run continuously through a scene. There is no hard line between "induction" and "suggestions": "everything you do is suggestive and hypnotic, and the state, whatever it may be, is fluid."
- **Ambiguity** is the best way to "cheat" at pacing and leading. Make statements that are hard to contradict: broaden them until they carry little concrete content, so the subject supplies the meaning. "You feel your eyes getting heavier and starting to close" invites a pass/fail check. "You can notice that feeling in your eyes, the way they are starting to shift" does not. "You're experiencing something interesting now, aren't you?" is always true. Bandler and Grinder define ambiguity as one surface structure with many interpretations.
- **Transderivational search** (TDS). In kink circles the term usually means the "surprise" moment of a pattern interrupt. Originally, and more accurately, it means the process of going inward to work out what something means: deriving deep structure from surface structure. Metaphors, ambiguity and presuppositions all trigger a TDS. Give it a beat of space.
- **Mind reading with ambiguity.** When pacing lands, the subject feels you are inside their head: "How can they possibly know what I'm experiencing, and yet, they seem to?" That feeling raises rapport and makes you seem more in control. Acknowledge a response without specifying it ("Oh, you felt that, huh?", "That's right"). Escalate toward "I know how much you are loving this." You can name an observation at once, or allude to it indirectly for a while before naming it outright, which leads them and leaves them feeling "caught".
- **Collapsing ambiguity.** Constant vagueness reads as guessing, gets boring, and gives the subject nothing to grip. At a big "yes", collapse into direct, concrete suggestion, which is "a fabulous proof of control." Wrong guesses rarely cost much, because subjects often think "Oh my gosh, they're totally right, I just realized...". A **false direct** statement is direct in form and ambiguous in content ("You feel like your mind is collapsing"), so it gives punch without a pass/fail check.

## Working principles

- **No magic words.** Words are ingredients whose meaning shifts with tone, timing, relationship and history. "Perhaps" isn't "a get-out-of-the-yes-set-free card": a subject may hear it as "I should", and feel they are failing. Put intent first and words second.
- **Use your own voice.** Many people who use "NLP" do it badly because they sound like a caricature of Bandler or Erickson. Bandler and Grinder themselves say not to copy Erickson. "The most effective method is simply to have your hypnosis sound more like your normal speaking." Learn where your ordinary speech already presupposes, hedges and tags, and build from that.
- **Overt is allowed, often better.** Hypnokink subjects recognize hypnotic language, and the recognition is part of the fantasy. A knowing "...yet" at the end of a tease, or pointing out the "fun language" you're using, often lands harder than hiding it.
- **The subject's mind is not a blank slate.** People generate spontaneous detail and associations. Leave room for that and tell them it's worthwhile. Treat nonverbal responses as real answers ("That's right" lets a response "count"), and keep that permissive tone going throughout.
- **Push or follow.** Faced with a partner's generalization, deletion or distortion, ask: "Would it be more fun to push against and clarify, or go in the same direction as their thoughts?"

## What the book corrects

- **Primary modality** and the "preferred representational system" have been refuted by research. **Eye-accessing cues** turned out not to work as Bandler and Grinder first claimed. Sensory predicates are still useful evidence of what someone is processing *right now*.
- **The conscious/unconscious split** is a metaphor, useful when it suits, but the mind is not literally two parts. It is also a "delusional verbal split" in meta-model terms. Fromm: "There is no such thing as the unconscious; there are only experiences of which we are aware, and others of which we are not aware." Do not rely on any "hidden observer" (Hilgard's idea, which he likened to a guardian angel) or unconscious guardian to keep a partner safe. MrDream: "You know what protects you? Boots."
- **Trance is not what makes suggestions work.** "Responsiveness is much more tied to expectation, role-enactment, and other psychological principles." Waking suggestions are an inherent part of NLP and of hypnosis generally.
- **Subjects are not always unaware.** Plenty of trance is conscious, and some parts feel automatic while others feel voluntary. Tell partners this, so that an expectation of unawareness doesn't block them.
- **Memory is unreliable,** which is part of why "resetting" someone to clear triggers or influence doesn't help.

## "Don't Be an Asshole"

"Don't Be an Asshole" is the book's simplest model of consent for sex, kink and hypnosis. Since "everything is NLP", the rule is not simply "Don't do NLP without someone's consent". It is "don't purposefully mess with someone without their consent". Active listening is fine; using hypnotic language patterns on someone who hasn't said it's OK is not. Being an asshole has two marks: **lying** (misleading or omitting) and **coercion** (pressure, direct or through social norms). Kink consent differs from therapy's: a therapist doesn't walk a client through the techniques being used, while in kink understanding the process helps both partners consent. Consent needs differ by person and have to be discovered in real, engaged conversation. Newer kinksters may need extra explanation of what is happening and what the risks are.

## Applying the book

1. **Name the situation**, and open the matching file from the table below. A request often spans several files: writing a brainwashing script, for example, can draw on Milton patterns, metaphor, future pacing and submodalities.
2. **Gather the map.** List what you know about the partner: their words (their sensory predicates, nominalizations, equivalences), their history, their likes, and their responses so far. These are the utilization ingredients. Done when each suggestion you plan to write ties to at least one of them, or is marked as a deliberate guess to test.
3. **Set the direction.** Name point A (how they are processing now) and point B (where the play is going: awake to trance, smart to dumb, doll to toy to pet). Find the links between them by chunking, metaphor or reframing.
4. **Draft the patter as pacing, then leading.** Pace what is observably true, lead with ambiguous suggestion, and collapse into direct suggestion at the peaks. Leave pauses for responses. Done when every leading line is preceded by pacing it can ride on, and every stretch of ambiguity has a planned collapse point.
5. **Annotate in the book's terms** when you critique or explain: name each pattern (presupposition of time, comparative deletion, conjunction reframe, false direct), so the user learns the move along with the wording.

## Lineage, for reading other sources

The meta model came out of a group at UC Santa Cruz in the early 1970s. Richard Bandler (who had a knack for "modeling" people) and the linguist John Grinder are the credited founders, and Frank Pucelik is the generally unacknowledged third cofounder. The group modeled three "therapeutic wizards": Fritz Perls (Gestalt), Virginia Satir (family therapy) and Milton Erickson (hypnosis). Many hypnokink staples come from NLP without the community knowing it: modalities, yes sets, double binds, anchoring, pacing and leading. The founding texts are *The Structure of Magic I* (1975) and *II* (1976), and *Patterns of the Hypnotic Techniques of Milton H. Erickson, M.D.* (1975, 1977), which became the basis of the Milton model. Gregory Bateson arranged the meeting with Erickson and originated the double bind and logical levels. Robert Dilts brought work on belief systems and adapted logical levels into neuro-logical levels; the book's reframing material leans on his *Sleight of Mouth* (1999). L. Michael Hall's *Communication Magic* (2001) updated the meta model from Korzybski's general semantics. NLP's recurring pattern is a model, then a contradiction of it, then an "update" asserted as definitive. Its habit is to borrow a term and distort it, so check what any given source means by a term.
