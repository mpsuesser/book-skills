# The evidence against the classical view

Use this when judging a claim that an emotion has a universal face, a bodily signature, a brain location, or a machine-readable signal. Apply the fingerprint test from SKILL.md: a fingerprint must be specific to one emotion and have no alternative explanation. Barrett's conclusion across faces, bodies and brains: "*variation is the norm.* Emotion fingerprints are a myth." "A theory of emotion must explain all the evidence... One must not point to fifty thousand black dogs as proof that all dogs are black."

## Faces

- **The basic emotion method.** In the 1960s, Silvan Tomkins, Carroll Izard and Paul Ekman built posed photographs of six "basic emotions": anger, fear, disgust, surprise, sadness and happiness. Subjects matched each face to a word, or to a story, from a list. Results ran around 85% agreement in Western-style countries and around 72% in less US-like ones. The poses were *stipulated*, inspired by Darwin, not observed in people feeling emotion.
- **The word list was a cheat sheet.** Each step that removes concept knowledge lowers agreement:
  - **free labeling**, where subjects supply their own word: 58% in an early study, lower later
  - wordless matching ("Do these people feel the same emotion?"): 42%
  - **semantic satiation**, where repeating "anger" until it becomes meaningless ("anggurr") temporarily disables the concept: 36%
  - patients with **semantic dementia** sorted 36 photos (6 actors × 6 poses) only into positive, negative and neutral piles
  - children aged two and three use sad, mad and scared interchangeably until they acquire differentiated concepts
  
  "Emotion concepts are the secret ingredient behind the success of the basic emotion method." Removing words mattered "every single time."
- **Remote cultures.** The **Himba** of Namibia sorted posed faces into a smiling pile, a wide-eyed pile and a mixed remainder. They labeled them "laughing" (*ondjora*) and "looking" (*tarera*): behaviors, not mental states. A vocalization study that seemed to show universality had an unreported step: participants explained each intended emotion in their own words and were corrected until they learned the English concept. The **Hadza** of Tanzania do not recognize posed fear faces. In the 1960s, of seven remote samples, the four tested with the basic emotion method supported universality. The three tested with free labeling did not, and those were published only as book chapters.
- **Measured faces.** Facial EMG shows that faces in emotion do not reliably move as in the posed photos. At best it distinguishes pleasant from unpleasant. The face has 42 small muscles on each side.
  - Infants restrained by the arm (anger) and shown a growling gorilla toy (fear) made indistinguishable facial movements. Adults judged their emotion from the context, even with the faces blanked out.
  - Newborns do not show adult-like differentiated expressions.
  - Actors' faces alone, without the scenario, were mostly read as some other emotion than the one intended. Martin Landau's shooting scenario was read as fear 66% of the time with the scenario, but his face alone as fear only 38% and surprise 56%.
  - A scowl grafted onto a body holding a dirty diaper reads as disgust.
  - Serena Williams's "terrified" face is her winning the 2008 U.S. Open.
- **Smiles.** The Romans did not smile spontaneously in the modern sense, and Latin had no word for "smile". Smiling was an invention of the Middle Ages, and broad, toothy smiles became popular only in the eighteenth century, as dentistry became more accessible and affordable.
- **What faces are.** Faces are instruments of social communication. The wide-eyed "fear face" is a cultural stereotype that preschools teach. Projects that catalog universal expressions with the basic emotion method are "helping to create the universality that they believe they are discovering." Emotion-detection technology and apps assume this universality.
- **Better question.** Replace "How accurately can you recognize fear?" with the study of the variety of movements people actually make in fear.

## Bodies

- In 1983, Ekman, Levenson and Friesen coached subjects to pose faces and measured their autonomic nervous system. Results:
  - Anger poses gave warmer fingertips; scowls, fear poses and sad poses gave faster heart rates; skin conductance and arm tension did not distinguish emotions.
  - The changes fit an alternative explanation: Western subjects understood what the poses meant.
  - The Minangkabau of West Sumatra did not show the same changes.
- Four meta-analyses of autonomic physiology found no consistent, specific fingerprints; the largest covered over 220 studies and about 22,000 subjects. "The body's orchestra of internal organs can play many different symphonies."
- In anger, people fume, cry, go quiet and cunning, or withdraw. Each behavior comes with different physiology, and changing posture changes the response. "The same emotion category involves different bodily responses."
- In twenty years of creating anger in Barrett's lab, no subject ever killed anybody. People swear, threaten, pound the table, leave, cry, resolve the conflict, or smile while wishing ill.

## Brains

- **The amygdala.**
  - Klüver and Bucy's monkeys in the 1930s lost their fear when the amygdala was removed.
  - **SM**, who has Urbach-Wiethe disease and no amygdalae, showed no fear of horror films, snakes or a haunted house. Yet she perceives fear in postures and voices, and panicked when she breathed CO₂-loaded air.
  - Of twins with the same damage, **BG** had deficits, and **AM** had normal fear because other networks compensated.
  - The amygdala is "neither necessary nor sufficient for emotion." Its activity increases for *any* face, fearful or neutral, as long as it is novel, which explains its response to the rarely seen posed fear faces. It also increases for pain, learning, meeting new people and making decisions.
- **Meta-analysis.** Barrett's lab analyzed about 100 neuroimaging studies with about 1,300 subjects, which later grew toward 400 studies. The amygdala increased in activity in only about a quarter of fear-experience studies and about 40% of fear-perception studies. It also increased for anger, disgust, sadness and happiness. No brain region contained the fingerprint for any single emotion, and neither did networks, electrical stimulation, or animal studies.
- **Pattern classification ("neural mind-reading").** A classifier can diagnose instances of anger at better than chance, but its pattern is an abstract statistical summary that "need not exist in any instance of the category." Treating it as anger's brain state is "mistaking a mathematical average for the norm"; no individual voxel appeared in all the anger scans.
- **The triune brain** (reptilian core, limbic system, rational cortex "like icing on a cake") is a myth, though popularized by Carl Sagan and Daniel Goleman's *Emotional Intelligence*. Barbara Finlay calls it "just plain silly... All brain divisions are present in all vertebrates." The "limbic system" is a fiction. Broca's area, still held up as the clearest case of localized function, is neither necessary nor sufficient for language.
- **"Emotional" brain regions.** "Every brain region that's claimed to be a home of emotion in humans is a body-budgeting region within the interoceptive network." "The idea of an emotional brain region is an illusion caused by the outdated belief in a reactive brain." Cortisol is not a "stress hormone"; it is released whenever you need a surge of energy.

## Why essentialism persists

- **Essentialism** is the belief that a category has an essence, a true underlying nature shared by its members. The classical view comes in several variants:
  - Panksepp: emotions as subcortical circuits
  - Pinker: emotions as mental organs built by genes
  - Cosmides and Ekman: emotions as "programs"
  - **basic emotion theory**: six emotions triggered automatically
  - **classical appraisal theory**: the brain first judges or appraises the situation, then triggers the emotion
  
  "Essentialism is the culprit."
- **It is intuitive.** It promises simple, single-cause explanations.
- **It is hard to disprove.** "*Essentialism inoculates itself against counterevidence*": the reply is always "we haven't looked everywhere yet."
- **Words breed it.** Words are a Faustian bargain. They invite a concept, but also the belief in a *reason* for the sameness among its instances. William James: "Whenever we have made a word... to denote a certain group of phenomena, we are prone to suppose a substantive entity existing beyond the phenomena, of which the word shall be the name."
- **The cortex feeds it.** Its compressed summaries are "like a little imaginary essence."

## History and Darwin

- In *Origin of Species* Darwin freed biology from essentialism through population thinking. Thirteen years later, *The Expression of the Emotions* (1872) reversed this. Darwin called expressions vestigial and useless, like a tailbone, more than a dozen times. Floyd Allport in 1924 recast them as functional, wrapping the claim in "Darwin's magical cloak."
- William James wrote that each *instance* of emotion comes from a unique bodily state, not each category: fear of getting wet is not the same fear as fear of a bear. John Dewey's grafting of Darwin onto James produced the "James-Lange theory". Damasio's **somatic marker** idea descends from that merger.
- The war of views is old. On the essence side: Plato's charioteer and two winged horses, Descartes, phrenology, Freud's id, ego and superego, and Kahneman's System 1 and System 2. On the construction side: Heraclitus ("No man ever steps in the same river twice"), Buddhist dharmas, Hume, Kant, James and Wundt. Barrett recasts System 1 as times when predictions are less corrected by prediction error, and System 2 as times when they are more corrected.
- Behaviorism reduced emotions to behaviors: the "four F's" of fighting, fleeing, feeding and mating. The cognitive revolution then anointed basic emotion theory and classical appraisal theory. The **Lost Chorus** of construction-minded research, over 100 publications across 50 years, went unheard.
- Construction comes in three flavors, and the theory of constructed emotion takes from each:
  - **social construction**: culture and concepts
  - **psychological construction**: core systems, as in William James's "nothing but such processes variously combined", and Schachter and Singer's adrenaline experiment
  - **neuroconstruction**: experience wiring the brain. "Some of your synapses literally come into existence because other people talked to you."
- Thomas Kuhn: "To reject one paradigm without simultaneously substituting another is to reject science itself." The classical view still drives products, such as emotion analytics, and policy. The TSA's SPOT program, which trained officers to spot deception from faces and body language, cost taxpayers $900 million.
- **Stakes.** Women with chest pressure are more often told they have anxiety, while men are diagnosed with heart disease. The Gulf War was launched in part because Saddam Hussein's half-brother thought he could read the American negotiators' emotions, and told Saddam the United States wasn't serious about attacking; the war claimed 175,000 Iraqi lives and hundreds of coalition lives.
