# Emotion and illness

Barrett calls this her most speculative chapter; present it at that confidence. Her thesis: illnesses are "human-made ways of carving up the same highly variable biological pie." Some major "mental" illnesses may be rooted in a chronically unbalanced body budget and unbridled inflammation, and categorized as different disorders by context. Necessary causes are not sufficient: of 100 people with a cold virus in their noses, only 25 to 40% get sick.

## The body budget and inflammation

1. **Fluctuation is normal.** The stomach borrows from the muscles, and short-term imbalances are fine if paid back by eating and sleeping.
2. **Prolonged imbalance brings in the debt collectors.** The immune system releases **proinflammatory cytokines**, which make you feel fatigued and unpleasant.
3. **Cortisol stops braking.** Under chronic imbalance, from a gunfire neighborhood to daily social rejection (a lunch clique, unanswered texts), excess cortisol stops suppressing inflammation and inflammation flares.
4. **The cycle closes.** Fatigue means less movement, poorer eating and sleep, and weight gain (fat cells produce cytokines). People avoid others, which feeds the fatigue.
5. **Inflammation reaches the brain.** Cytokines reshape the interoceptive network and move body-budgeting regions from "mostly deaf" toward "completely deaf." They can kill neurons and lower IQ performance.

"A chronically imbalanced body budget acts like fertilizer for disease": diabetes, obesity, heart disease, depression, insomnia, memory problems and dementia. Cancer tumors grow faster and metastasize.

Disorders' boundaries are evaporating. Disorders with different names share symptoms, atrophy the same regions, show low granularity and respond to the same medications. Their common ingredients include genetics, insomnia, and damage to the interoceptive network and its hubs.

## Stress

- **"Stress doesn't come from the outside world. You construct it."** "Stress" is a concept applied to an unbalanced body budget.
- **Three kinds.** Stress can be positive (learning a new subject), tolerable (a fight with your best friend) or toxic (poverty, abuse, loneliness).
- **Same mechanisms as emotion.** Stress, emotion and pain are built by the same mechanisms and differ in the final categorization. "Distinguishing between pain, stress, and emotion is a form of emotional granularity."
- **Chronic stress atrophies** the interoceptive and control networks.
- **Childhood adversity.** "All it takes are a couple of highly negative experiences for children to feel like they are living in a combat zone," shrinking their body-budgeting regions by adulthood. Harsh, chaotic families predict inflammation in adolescent girls. Bullied children carry low-grade inflammation into adulthood.
- **Labeling helps.** Cancer patients who categorize and label their emotions have lower cytokines. Men with prostate cancer who expressed a lot of affect they didn't label had the highest. Breast cancer survivors who labeled their emotions made fewer medical visits.

## Pain

- **Nociception is not pain.** **Nociception** is the detection of physical damage. Pain is an *experience* constructed from damage or *predicted* damage, using the concept "Pain". Nociception may be a form of interoception.
- **Prediction sets the volume.** Body-budgeting regions act as a volume control: unpleasant affect plus heat produces more pain. "This is not imaginary pain. It is real."
- **The placebo and nocebo effects** are prediction at work. Opioids are "your internal medicine cabinet." Barrett's daughter Sophia had 13 ear infections in 9 months, and began crying earlier at each visit: in the waiting room, then the lobby, then the parking garage, until she whimpered whenever they passed the street.
- **Chronic pain** includes fibromyalgia, migraine and chronic back pain. It affects over 1.5 billion people, 100 million of them in the US, and analgesics fail more than half the time.
  - Barrett's model: "errant predictions without correction". The brain didn't get the memo that the injury healed. It is a brain disease rooted in inflammation, a "misapplication of the concept 'Pain'", with the brain "predicting poorly *and* receiving misleading data".
  - What to tell a sufferer: "You're not crazy... Your predictive brain... is *generating authentic pain*," as with a phantom limb.
  - Early injury and stress raise the risk; infants before the 1980s were operated on without anesthesia.
  - The law treats chronic pain as "emotional", but it is "a difference of degree, not kind."

## Depression

- **The traditional story** is thinking failing to control feeling: "Don't worry, be happy; and if that doesn't work, try antidepressants." Of 27 million Americans on antidepressants, over 70% still have symptoms.
- **Barrett's model.** Depression is "a disorder of misbudgeting and prediction." Experience is "constructed from the past but *not corrected by the present*." The brain predicts metabolic needs that never come, metabolizing unnecessary glucose, which links depression to weight gain, diabetes, heart disease and cancer.
- **The causal story reversed.** "Your feelings right now drive your next thought." A depressed person is locked into uncorrected predictions from an adverse past. To cut spending, the brain stops moving and ignores the world, which produces fatigue.
- **Tipping points**: childhood abuse, heart disease, insomnia, genes, menstrual-cycle shifts in connectivity.
- **Treatment** breaks the misbudgeting. Antidepressants and cognitive behavioral therapy normalize a body-budgeting region. Prediction error has to be let in, for example by keeping a diary of positive experiences. Mayberg's deep brain stimulation shifts attention from inside toward the world. "Depression is a brain disease and not just a shortage of happy thoughts."

## Anxiety, and the spectrum

- **Anxiety is the opposite setting.** "An anxious brain, in a sense, is the opposite of a depressed brain. In depression, prediction is dialed way up and prediction error way down... In anxiety... too much prediction error from the world, and too many predictions are unsuccessful."
- **Uncertainty is the burden.** Uncertainty is more unpleasant than assured harm. Seriously ill patients with a good chance of recovery are less satisfied with life than those with a permanent condition.
- **Two strategies.** An anxious brain seeks a deposit, which brings unpleasant affect. A depressed brain cuts the need, through stillness and fatigue.
- **Two concepts, similar sensations.** "Anxiety" and "Depression" are concepts for similar sensations. Rating scales prime one or the other, which explains why Barrett's early subjects couldn't tell them apart.
- **Autism (speculative).** Possibly too little prediction, leaving constant prediction error. That would mean impaired statistical learning and disinterest in faces and language, and a need for stereotyped input. Temple Grandin hears "a dentist's drill." Naoki Higashida (*The Reason I Jump*) describes scanning memory.
- **The spectrum**: "too much prediction and not enough correction, you feel bad, and the flavor of badness depends on the concepts you use. In small amounts, you might feel angry or shameful. In extreme amounts, you get chronic pain or depression... too much sensory input and ineffective prediction yields anxiety... With no prediction at all..." you get something comparable to autism. There is no single cause and no golden cure.
- **Co-constructing a diagnosis.** Barrett's doctor once suggested "Maybe you're depressed and you don't know it." Her fatigue had other explanations: little sleep, a lab of 100 people, a book, a teenage daughter, menopause.

## The cultural recipe for misbudgeting

- **What young people are fed**: media violence, bullying played for laughs with a laugh track, social-media rejection, lost sleep and pseudo-food.
- **Opioids and sugar.** The brain's natural opioids regulate affect (not nociception), and opiate drugs mimic them; people likely self-medicate a chronically imbalanced body budget. From 1997 to 2011 the number of U.S. adults addicted to prescription drugs rose 900 percent. Sugar triggers the brain's opioids and may act as a mild analgesic, so junk food *feels* good.
- **For medicine**: add interoception, the body budget and emotion concepts to the common factors behind illness. "Distinguish physical reality from social reality, and never mistake one for the other, while still understanding that the two are irrevocably entwined."
