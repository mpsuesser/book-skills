# Front-end lead funnels: squeeze, survey, summit

Lead funnels sit on the free step of the ladder. The offer costs the visitor nothing but an email address, and the goal is a lead who enters your follow-up funnels with the Attractive Character.

The squeeze page was born when pop-up blockers killed the lead flow from pop-up ads: marketers made the pop-up the first page, a gateway in front of the site. The debate was whether people would give an email first; with a good lead magnet a large percentage did, and although fewer people saw the website, follow-up made far more money (100 people → squeeze → 30 people → website, with every opt-in getting follow-up emails).

## Lead "squeeze" funnel

Two pages: **squeeze page** (headline, lead magnet, email field, submit) → **thank-you page** (thank you, download the lead magnet, a Step #2 "Next Step" button to the next step of the ladder).

Model: Eben Pagan's Double Your Dating, $20 million a year and a list of more than a million, with all ads pointing at one squeeze page: "You're About To Learn Secrets That Most Men Will Never Know About Women..." with bullets ("The Kiss Test"; how men and women think about dating differently; "secret" body language; approaching without pick-up lines; free fun places to go), a first-name and email form, and "Free Instant Access!" The next page delivered the kiss test in a few paragraphs (touch the tips of her hair and comment on it; if she's receptive, it's a sure sign; if she pulls away, stop), then invited them to his e-book. Real value first, then the invitation up the ladder.

**Page 1, squeeze page.**
- *Offer*: the **lead magnet**, given in exchange for an email. It attracts dream customers like a magnet: design something the avatar will love and everyone else will ignore. Short and genuinely valuable is the key (the kiss test). Any form works (e-book, coupon, contest, video, membership site); "How or what you deliver matters less than creating something that your people will truly want." His: Marketing Secrets Blackbook; Marketing Secrets Binge Guide; Funnel Hacker's Cookbook ("Get My Top 22 Sales Funnels For FREE..." with a button that opens a "Where Should We Email Your Cookbook?" pop-up). Keep the page short and simple: you're only asking for an email.
- *Hook*: the headline. More curiosity, more opt-ins. Low conversion means the lead magnet isn't good enough or the headline lacks curiosity. Use the Curiosity-Based Headline scripts.
- *Story*: short, usually a subheadline (Blackbook: "Want to Know How We Took ClickFunnels from ZERO to over $100,000,000 in Sales in Just 3 Short Years...?"). A strong, simple offer needs little story. When the offer needs explaining, use a **reverse squeeze page**: a video in place of the image tells the story and asks for the opt-in. It converts at a lower rate but the leads are more qualified. Script: Who, What, Why, How. (His: "THE PERFECT WEBINAR / THIS WEBINAR SCRIPT MADE ME A MILLIONAIRE..." over a video, name and email, GET ACCESS NOW.)

**Page 2, thank-you page.** Thank them and deliver the lead magnet. In your first funnel there's no next funnel yet, and that's fine: the first Soap Opera Sequence starts the relationship, and later you add the link to the next funnel. His: "Thanks For Signing Up! The Download Links Are Ready..." Step #1 subscribe to the podcast, Step #2 download your gifts, Step #3 join the One Funnel Away Challenge.

## Survey funnel

Pages: **survey page** (a "Tell Me More..." video, a question, NEXT) → more questions → a **"Calculating..."** page → a **squeeze page per result bucket** → a **results page per bucket** (what "You Need," with pricing and add to cart).

Model: FuzzyYellowBalls.com's hidden quiz. "Can You Really Add 10–15 MPH To Your Tennis Serve Overnight With This Trick? (HINT: It All Has To Do With Eliminating Your #1 Serve Killer...)" → questions → calculating → "we've pinpointed the Serve Killer," enter name and email for a free video on fixing it → a sales page specific to that problem. Retaking the quiz showed the same program with a sales message tailored to each answer. Works best when many different types of people benefit from the offer.

**Page 1, survey page.** The offer: "Take this short quiz, and I will tell you ___." The purpose is to sort visitors into **buckets** so you can speak to each directly. ClickFunnels: "Take This Short Quiz To Find Out Which Funnel Will Work The Best For YOUR Specific Business..." with "Which Of These Best Describes Your Business/Service?" (Agency/Freelancer; Information Products; E-Commerce; Coaching/Consulting; Network Marketing; Local Small Business; B2B Lead Generation; Blogging/Affiliate; Non-Profits) and the promise of a free two-week trial for taking the 30-second quiz.

The questions keep people engaged, but only one really matters: the **bucket question** (Ryan Levesque, *Ask*), which decides the results page. ClickFunnels asks it first; it's often buried deeper. Other questions: Where did you hear about us? How long have you been in business (0–2, 2–5, 5–10, 10+ years)? Are you using sales funnels (a working funnel / have one but don't use it / don't have one yet / what's a funnel?)?

The general question order he funnel-hacked:
1. Self-identifying (gender, age).
2. Self-identifying on the subject (Do you have a funnel? Have you tried to lose weight?).
3. Self-declared skill level (money made; knowledge of the topic).
4. Self-declared biggest challenge (traffic; eating carbs).
5. Educate and clarify: "Those with [#2 response] typically suffer from one of three [symptoms]. Tell me which issue you suffer from the most," followed by the three choices.
6. A surprise, curiosity-based question ("Wait... Are you drinking water first thing in the morning? Because that could matter...").
One of these becomes the bucket question, each answer assigned its results page.

**Page 2, squeeze page.** After the last question a "calculating" pop-up runs (not essential, but it lifted conversions): "Your Case Study is Being Generated SPECIFICALLY FOR YOU!" with a progress bar and a checklist (searching the user database, filtering the selected industry, selecting top templates, generating an industry-specific case study). Then: "CASE STUDY FOUND! Where Should We Send This Case Study To?" with as-featured-in logos. Each bucket answer maps to a case study from someone in that industry (nine options, nine case studies), and the email enters a bucket-specific Soap Opera Sequence about their industry.

**Page 3, results page(s).** Show the case study (the story), then the offer. The delivery (video, custom PDF, text) matters less than the customization. Template: "Based on your answers, your #1 reason for [not succeeding/your self-declared pain] is likely because you don't have a [retail/coaching/author funnel—or other surprising result], which typically costs [subject] an average of [$/result] every [time], not to mention a lot of [other costs]." His: "Natalie Is Selling Information Products JUST LIKE YOU!" and "Garrett Is Building His Coaching Business, JUST LIKE YOU!", each with a testimonial video and Start Your FREE 14 Day Trial. The more you customize each bucket (results page and follow-up), the higher the conversions.

## Summit funnel

Pages: **registration page** (video, Register, speaker photos with blurbs, Register again) → **special offer page** (bullets, Add to Cart); then emails send everyone to **broadcast pages** for Day 1, Day 2, Day 3 (a few speakers each).

A lead funnel that uses other people's traffic and co-brands you with established names to build authority. Brunson built his early list and influencer relationships this way, and ClickFunnels runs a summit about twice a year to reach new types of people. Taught by Bailey Richert, who started with no list, no authority, no "as seen on" logos, testimonials or profit reports, no ad budget, and organic channels that were too slow. "A virtual summit is a free online conference": zero ad spend, good for beginners, and it brings targeted traffic from dozens of established businesses' lists into your funnel within weeks, builds a network of influencer colleagues, and lends you authority by appearing beside the speakers. The 30 Days summit (30 Two Comma Club winners on how they'd rebuild in 30 days if they lost everything) made more than $1 million in under two weeks.

How a summit works:
- **Invitations**: the host invites colleagues in the niche to be interviewed. The ideal speaker has their own email list of your ideal clients. A good-size summit has about 30 speakers, so approach more than that.
- **Interviews**: the host prerecords them and broadcasts them online for a limited time during the event.
- **Promotion**: motivated by affiliate commissions and prestige, the speakers promote the summit to their lists ahead of time ("This is the key!"). The host collects the email of everyone who registers and sells to them; a portion of each speaker's subscribers become yours.

**Page 1, registration page.** Get the email so they become a registered attendee who receives the viewing details. The top section is hook (a curiosity headline), story (video), offer (register). His: "You Suddenly LOSE EVERYTHING... What Would You Do From Day 1 To Day 30 To Save Yourself...?"; and "If you HAD to Feed your Family with Nothing but your Affiliate Commissions and you only had 100 DAYS To Turn This Hobby Into Your FULL-TIME CAREER / WHAT WOULD YOU DO EACH DAY..." Below that, long form: the summit name, topic and dates, then each speaker's name, headshot and basic info (website, interview topic, a benefit headline). Speakers expect to be featured, and their subscribers seeing a favorite influencer's face raises conversions.

**Page 2, special offer page.** Three parts: (1) registration confirmation (reassure them they're registered and an email is coming); (2) the **all-access pass** offer, a video of the host explaining it, then sales-page text and images; (3) the order form. The pass is lifetime access to every interview in a membership area, to watch at their own pace instead of in the limited window, with bonuses: extra interviews not in the broadcast, transcripts, compiled PDF notes, MP3s. Alternative: sell the next offer up the ladder with unrestricted replay access as a bonus (30 Days sold the One Funnel Away Challenge plus all 15 speakers' blueprints, more than 186 pages, free). Price low (about $100 or less); the traffic is mostly warm, but still make it an incredible offer at a no-brainer price.

**Page 3, broadcast pages.** Deliver the free event to every registrant, upgraded or not. These pages are not shown after opt-in; the links are emailed on the live dates (prerecorded interviews, but a limited-time event on specific days). Day 1: embed that day's interviews with speaker photos and details, plus a timer showing how long they stay free (24 hours per day works across time zones), with tabs for the days ahead shown locked. The call to action: get full access to the recordings. At the start of Day 2, take down Day 1's videos (the only way to see them now is to buy), post Day 2's, and email the link. Continue until every interview has aired; three to four days is typical, since engagement drops after that. Keep promoting the recordings during and after. If you buy ads, the sales should cover the ad cost; pay speakers commissions on the sales they generate.
