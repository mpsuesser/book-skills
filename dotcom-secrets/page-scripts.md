# Page scripts: headlines, Who What Why How, Star Story Solution, OTO

The pitch (the **sales copy**) is the biggest piece of getting someone to buy. A funnel takes a proven sales presentation, makes the pitch once, and lets it close sales around the clock; even if it converts below face-to-face selling, revenue rises dramatically. Brunson found the **funnel scripts** by funnel-hacking a few dozen similar funnels, transcribing their sales videos, upsells, webinars and phone scripts, reading them for patterns and doodling each one. Jim Edwards built them into the FunnelScripts.com software. A script is a framework, never a word-for-word text: weave your own stories, Attractive Character and message into it, because your personality is what keeps people coming back.

Fill every part of a script, in order.

## Curiosity-Based Headlines (Jim Edwards)

Headlines appear at every step: the Facebook ad, the squeeze page, the sales page (several), the upsell page. It's the most-used script in marketing: ads, landing pages, email subject lines, sales-page headlines and subheads, upsell, downsell and thank-you pages, blog posts, articles.

Most people write the headline last, as an afterthought, which is a huge mistake. A great product with great copy under a junk headline gets few sales; a headline that stops the ideal prospect and pre-frames them perfectly lets even weak copy sell more. (Jim's demonstration: covering the headlines on a newspaper's front page leaves no way to tell whether you're interested, and the paper's own full-page ad had no headline.)

"The number one purpose of a headline is to hook the right people to stop what they're doing, pre-frame them for your story or sales message, and get them to the next step in your sales copy." An ad headline gets them to stop, read and click; a page headline arouses curiosity and desire so they watch the video or read on; a subject line gets the open; a video's first sentence makes them curious to hear the next. "Curiosity is the key!"

**The math.** Doubling the clicks on an ad at least doubles revenue and halves the traffic cost per reader ($1,000 buying 500 readers is $2 each; at 1,000 readers it's $1). Selling a $1,000 product with $900 of ads per sale makes $100; double the clicks and it's $2,000 minus $900, $1,100: more than ten times the profit. You don't know the right headline until you've tested a bunch, so you need scripts that generate many fast: "Headline scripts are the flashlight in the gold mine."

**Match the temperature.** Hot traffic knows you and your products; warm knows a solution exists but not you; cold knows only that it has a problem. Never send all three to the same landing page; each needs a headline at its own temperature. (The temperature system is a starting point, not the Ten Commandments.)

Hot (name you and the product):
- Finally! Discover How (Product/Service Name) Gives You the Secret to (Big Result)... Without (Pain)... Today!
- (Product/Service Name) Is the Secret to Automatically (Big Result)!... Guaranteed!
- Watch This Free Video to Learn How (Product/Service Name) Gives You an Unfair Advantage with (Main Topic)... Guaranteed!
- It's True! You Too Can (Big Result) in (Specific Timeframe) with (Product/Service Name)... Guaranteed!
- (Big Result) with (Product/Service Name)... Guaranteed!

Warm (lead with the payoff they're after):
- See How Easily You Can (Big Result) Without (Pain)!
- For (Target Audience) Who Want to (Big Result) but Can't Get Started!
- The Secret to Automatically (Big Result) Without (Pain)!
- Here's a Quick Way to (Big Result)... Guaranteed!
- How (Target Audience) Just Like You (Big Result) in Just (Specific Time Frame)!
- The TRUTH About How to (Big Result) and Avoid (Pain)

Cold (lead with their problem):
- Don't Let (Pain) Stop You!
- You Can Laugh at (Main Topic) Problems If You Follow This Plan
- How (Target Audience) Can STOP (Pain)
- When Pro (Target Audience) Have (Main Topic) Problems, Here's What They Do
- How to Eliminate (Pain) from Your Life... Guaranteed!

Question headlines (ask about something they want or want to avoid):
- What If You Could (Desire) Without (Pain)?
- Worried About (Pain)?
- Isn't It Time You Stopped Struggling with (Pain)?
- Sick and Tired of Not Having Any (Desire)?
- Are You Getting Enough (Desire)?
- Do You Want to Be a Successful (Target Audience)?
- Isn't It Time You Got the (Desire) You Really Want?

Curiosity headlines:
- You Can Laugh at (Main Topic) Worries—If You Follow This Simple Plan
- Imagine (Desire) in Just 30 Minutes
- You May Be Sabotaging Your (Main Topic) Success... and You Don't Even Know It
- Don't Even Think about (Desire) Without Reading This!
- Here's What (Enemy) Doesn't Want You to Know about (Main Topic)

People buy to satisfy a desire or solve a problem, and "99 times out of 100, the headline is what starts the buying conversation." Turn up the emotional punch in three areas: a clear desire to satisfy, a pressing problem to solve, a nagging or intense pain to avoid or eliminate.

**Only the buyer's opinion counts.** Jim launched a CD with "How I Went from Trailer Trash to Piles of Cash," which only he liked ("every headline I fall in love with rarely works"); 300 visitors, no sales. He asked what a great copywriter would do (test a different headline, now), and a new headline brought a sale in three or four minutes. He then modeled a headline that had made him spend money ("If sales copy gets you to spend money, it's good copy" and worth studying): "How to Gain a Positively Unfair Advantage in the Search Engine Wars" became "How to Gain a Positively Unfair Advantage in Business and in Life!" With only the headline changed, he made five sales in five minutes. It worked because it was about them, not him, and the curiosity was personal to each reader. Make the headline about them, start from proven templates, and you can have a viable headline in under a minute.

## Who, What, Why, How

Used on squeeze pages, book and cart funnel videos, webinar registration and thank-you pages, product launch opt-ins, and almost daily for video and text ads on low-ticket and front-of-ladder offers. Four questions, then a fast call to action:

1. **Who are you?** At the front of the ladder, with colder traffic, they're probably seeing you for the first time, so a quick introduction ("Hi, I'm Russell Brunson, and I'm one of the cofounders of ClickFunnels.").
2. **What do you have?** Hook them and introduce the offer quickly (100,000 entrepreneurs have ditched their websites for funnels; here's a free two-week trial).
3. **Why do they need it?** **PAS: Problem, Agitate, Solve.** Raise the problem in their words, agitate it by twisting the knife, and present the offer as the solution. (Problem: your website isn't generating leads or sales. Agitate: people spend thousands on a glorified brochure that doesn't do anything. Solve: a free trial, with your funnel live before you spend a penny.)
4. **How can they get this offer?** Exactly what to do ("click on the link below, or go to ClickFunnels.com").

It works the same for a free teeth cleaning, a free report or a free webinar. Four amplifiers make it stronger:
- **The catch**: people always assume there is one, so say what it is, or that there isn't one (the trial is free because once your funnel is live, you'll never leave).
- **Urgency and scarcity**: real ones (10 lead-funnel templates if you sign up before Friday at midnight; after that, still the trial, but no templates).
- **Guarantee**: reverse the risk (cancel with no hassle, one click, no one to talk to).
- **Recap**: remind them what they get and why.

## Star, Story, Solution

The video sales letter script, from Vince James (more than $100 million in 23 months selling supplements). A **star** (the Attractive Character), a **story** that agitates a problem, a **solution** (the offer). It works for text and video sales letters; a video runs about 20 to 30 minutes, usually for low-ticket offers (up to about $100) that need a longer presentation or a false belief broken before the offer.

### Star

1. **Pattern interrupt (hook)**: pull them out of what they're doing and into the copy. Often modeled on a curiosity headline: a big promise of what they'll learn, a loop opened, then move on ("During this presentation, I'm going to show you my weird niche funnel... But first, I have a few questions for you...").
2. **Core-desire questions**: move their mind to the outcome they want and get them saying yes in their heads ("Have you ever wanted to work from home? Own your own business?").
3. **Agitate past failures**: this isn't their first try; twist the knife ("So, why hasn't it happened for you yet? Come on, admit it." "This isn't the first time you've been looking for a proven way to make money, is it?" (or lose weight) "When is it your turn?").
4. **Big promise, the One Thing**: the one thing that, solved, they'd pay anything for ("by the time you finish this video, that will change forever. I'm going to reveal to you [big promise]!").
5. **Introduce the star**: the Attractive Character (often the Reluctant Hero), as the transition into the story.

### Story

Close to the Soap Opera Sequence's shape.
1. **High drama**: start at the peak. (His: crawling out from under his desk to answer a call: in six hours, over thirty spam complaints from your IP address; you're a spammer. Then explaining it to his wife of six weeks.)
2. **Backstory (wall)**: how the character got there, until they hit a wall and were completely stuck, which is probably where the prospect is now. (Six hours earlier: the most important thing you can have is your own list; 1 percent of 1,000,000 people buying at $50 is $500,000; he had no idea how to get one.)
3. **Identify the problem**: why the character was stuck, as close to the prospect's problem as possible. "I realized the problem I had wasn't ___, but it was actually ___."
4. **Epiphany, or Declaration of Independence**: the idea, or the decision to change. "As I was sitting there trying to figure out what to do, I had an idea..." / "I knew the answer, but I didn't want to do it. But right then, I decided that I HAD to make a change..."
5. **Path to the ultimate solution**: what you tried first, like what they've tried. "The first thing I tried was ___, but the problem I found was ___. So then I tried ___, but nothing seemed to really work, until..."
6. **First sign of success**: "And that's when I finally tried ___. And guess what? This time it worked!"
7. **Conspiracy**: the cards were stacked against you from the start, as the prospect believes of themselves. "It wasn't my fault! All these years of failure, and it was actually because of ___."
8. **The big lie**: why it isn't their fault. "For years, they had been telling me ___, and when I figured out that it wasn't true, I was finally able to break out of their chains..."
9. **Common enemy**: who or what to blame for the lie. "The real problem is ___. They were the ones keeping me and you from ___."
10. **Rapid growth**: how fast things moved once you knew the truth. "Once I realized ___, that's when we started to ___ really FAST!"
11. **Case studies**: others with similar success. "But it wasn't just me. Take a look at what ___ has done for others."
12. **Hidden benefits**: the unexpected ones. "Not only does it ___, but it also ___ and ___."

### Solution

1. **Formal introduction**: "And that's why I created ___."
2. **Pain and cost**: what it took you to create it, so they can use it without going through the same pain ("took ___ to create, and cost me ___").
3. **Ease**: contrast your pain and cost with their ease, because you blazed the trail.
4. **Speed**: the time saved. "What used to take me ___, I can get done in ___."
5. **"So you can" benefits**: feature + "so you can" + benefit, because people buy the benefit. "Burns fat while you sleep so you can lose weight without exercising." "Builds your email list on autopilot so you can concentrate on running your business."
6. **Social proof**: "don't just take my word for it..."
7. **Make the offer and build value**: everything they get, presented with the Stack (each element added, then restacked).
8. **Price anchor**: the total value, at least ten times the price; if it isn't, add more to the offer.
9. **If all (emotional close)**: "If all this did was ___, would it be worth the full asking price?" Each yes is a subconscious agreement that it's worth the full price (toward pleasure: the house of your dreams; away from pain: firing your boss).
10. **Reveal the real price**: a price drop with a reason. I'm not going to charge you the $___ it's worth, or even the $___ you were probably thinking; because you're here today, just $___.
11. **Guarantee (logic close)**: remove the risk; it speaks to the logical mind (a full 30-day, 100 percent money-back guarantee).
12. **Inject scarcity (fear close)**: a legitimate reason to buy now. "But you must act now because ___."
13. **Future pacing**: "Just imagine what life will be like..."
14. **Call to action**: what to do and what happens next (the secure order form, then the members' area, downloadable even at 2:00 A.M.).
15. **Post-selling**: make them feel left behind ("For those who are already signing up, this is what's going to happen next...").
16. **Takeaway selling (warning)**: the decision is theirs, and it doesn't matter to you; you'll keep hitting your goals either way, while without your help they'll always work harder than they need to.
17. **Close with a reminder**: a summary for skimmers, restacking the offer.

## The OTO script

For any one-time offer page, right after the first purchase. The mistake was 20- to 30-minute Star, Story, Solution upsell videos, which converted terribly. Tim Erway called it "the cardinal upsell mistake. You tried to resell the prospect"; they're already sold and already said the first yes, so they just need a reason to say yes again. Reworking a dozen funnels this way took upsell conversions from almost nothing to double digits overnight.

Its job is to confirm the first purchase and quickly win a second yes for the next offer that helps them reach the result. Information products: the next product, solving the next problem (a funnel book leads to funnel software or a traffic course). Physical products: more of the same at a discount. The video runs three to five minutes and closes offers from $27 to $997 or more.

1. **Confirm the initial decision.** Lay buyer's remorse to rest and keep the loop open. "Congratulations, your order is complete" closed the loop; "Wait! Your order is not yet complete" kept it open, and conversions rose. ("Congratulations on purchasing ___! Don't close your browser yet because your order is not quite finished.")
2. **Smart → why.** "You've made a smart choice, and here's why..." "You ordered this because you wanted ___, and that's exactly what it's going to do for you."
3. **Question: next or more.** Information: "Let me ask you a question. Now that you have ___, many people ask me about ___." Physical: "How would you like to get more ___ at a huge discount?"
4. **Exclusive.** Not for everyone; offered because they proved they're an action-taker by buying ___; a special one-time offer only here, only now.
5. **Results, fast, speed.** It complements the first purchase with better results faster ("help you to ___ in a fraction of the time").
6. **The One Thing.** Present the one most valuable thing that gives the best results, because explaining everything kills the sale. (The Perfect Webinar upsell holds 24+ hours of video; he focuses on one, the video teaching the Stack.) "I don't have time to go over everything... but one of the strategies inside that will give you the results you're looking for FAST is ___... And that is just ONE of the things... I wish I had time to explain everything else!"
7. **Future pacing.** "Can you imagine what your life will be like when you have ___?"
8. **Call to action.** "Click the button below right now to add ___ to your order."
9. **Guarantee.** Reverse the risk.
10. **Value stacking.** Use the word FREE as many times as possible: each other item, "worth $___," free if they act now.
11. **Scarcity.** Truly one-time: it sells on the website for [a higher price], but right now this is their one chance, and when they leave this page it's gone forever.
12. **Second call to action.** Repeat it.
13. **Testimonial rush.** The more the better ("If you're still here trying to decide... I'll show you a few videos from others who were just like you...").
