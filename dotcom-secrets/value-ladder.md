# The Value Ladder and funnel stacking

## The ladder

A staircase with **value** rising on one axis and **price** on the other: each step delivers more value at a higher price. The first step is bait that costs little or nothing; customers who receive value naturally want more, and a percentage climb each step. "It's how we're wired as humans: to seek more value from the places that we've already received it from."

The dentist example: a postcard offers a free teeth cleaning (bait). In the chair: "Are you a smoker?" leads to whitening trays; shifting teeth lead to a retainer or Invisalign; more than $2,000 sold in under an hour; cosmetic dentistry ($10,000+) sits at the top. Booking the next cleaning six months out is **continuity**: an offer the customer keeps paying for weekly, monthly or yearly until they cancel. Dentist ladder: Teeth Cleaning → Whitening → Retainer/Invisalign → Cosmetic, with 6-Month Checkups as continuity beneath.

Tiers, named: **Free Offers → Front-End Offers → Middle of the Value Ladder Offers → Back-End Offers**. In funnel terms: bait → front end → middle → back end, with traffic poured in the top and money out the bottom.

### Value Ladder Mission Statement (VLMS)

From Stacey and Paul Martino: the ladder is the customer's yellow brick road to their Oz. Formula: **"We help (insert who) to (insert result they want to achieve) through (insert your new opportunity)."**
- **Who**: the dream customer.
- **Result**: what they have been unsuccessfully trying to accomplish that you can solve.
- **New opportunity**: an offer different from the things they have already tried.

Dentist: "We help families in our community to increase their confidence through creating a beautiful smile they are proud to share." ClickFunnels: "We help visionary entrepreneurs to grow their companies through sales funnels." Every offer on every tier (books, courses, events, software, coaching, masterminds) is a different way to fulfill the same VLMS.

### The ladder is the business plan

"Your value ladder is your actual business plan": it shows how you acquire customers, where you make money, and the result you help them achieve. Without one, "they don't really have a business; they are just selling a product." His $1 million done-for-you offer would never sell to a stranger on the street (no value given yet, and it seems expensive), but to 1,000 dream customers after days of value, more than 50 raised their hands. You can't sell your best and most expensive offer first, any more than you'd propose on a first date.

Example ladders:
- Author, speaker, coach, consultant: Book → Home Study Course → Live Event → Mastermind.
- The Martinos (relationships): podcast and free offers (lead funnels) → 14-Day Boost Challenge $47 (challenge funnel) → Quick Start Home Study $997 (webinar funnel) → Relationship Breakthrough Retreat, 3-day live, $1,997 → RelationshipU yearlong coaching $14,997 (application funnel).
- ClickFunnels: "Secrets" books → ClickFunnels $97/mo → Platinum $297/mo → Collective (live support, group coaching, events).
- Dr. Chad Woolner, chiropractor: he had only $50, 10-minute adjustments. Back end added: a $5,000+ wellness program with 10x the value per visit. Front end: "If you have a boring front-end offer, your business will always struggle." Adjustments aren't sexy (people wait until they hurt), so instead of a free exam he gave a free massage, supplements and a meditation CD for booking a first appointment; the clinic filled to capacity. Ladder: Free Information → Massage/Health → Adjustment → Wellness.

### Building out a partial ladder

Most companies have only part of a ladder. Information products have a proven ascension path; physical products and services need creativity.
- **Middle only?** Create bait. A custom suit maker whose cheapest offer was a $2,000 suit gave away free cufflinks online and got hundreds of qualified leads in days.
- **Front end only?** Bundle a back end: a coaching program, a live event, other results or value you could give.
- **Top capped too low?** At a $25,000 mastermind, his top offer was $5,000: "Russell, that's a $5,000 buyer lead—you need to sell them something else!" In that same room, 9 of 18 paid $30,000 to appear in a movie with Dan Kennedy. "There really is no end to your value ladder"; a percentage always want to pay a premium for more value, and the only limit is imagination.
- **No ladder at all** is a named failure: FitLife.tv was spending $116 in ads for every $97 sale, and its relationship with buyers ended when the funnel did, though people wanted to give more money. Making two to three times as much per sale would have let it buy more traffic and outbid its competitors.

### The ladder and the funnel

The funnel does two things: gives every customer value at the level of service they can afford, and stays profitable while identifying the dream clients who can afford the highest offer. Early online, $1 in ads brought $2 back; competition raised ad costs and buying resistance, so funnels had to go deeper. "The deeper your funnel is... the more each customer will be worth to you. And the more they are worth to you, the more you can spend to acquire them." Upsells right after purchase are one funnel type; **follow-up funnels** sell the other products. Every product has its own sales funnel.

## Funnel types per tier

Each tier has its funnel family (the tier table in SKILL.md): lead funnels get dream customers onto your list, unboxing funnels turn leads into buyers, presentation funnels sell higher-priced offers through a presentation, and phone funnels take people offline onto the phone. Choose by price first, then by audience and offer within the step. Prices flex: a $1,000 offer can be unboxed and sold through an unboxing funnel; a $100 product can sit on the thank-you or special-offer page of a lead funnel. For each step, ask: What is the goal for this step? Which funnels best achieve it? What offers plug in? What scripts go on each page? The funnels and scripts are a framework: plug in your offers and your Attractive Character's personality, and tweak to fit.

## Your first funnel

The failure mode: sketching the whole ladder, spending months or years building every offer, and never selling anything. The ladder does not need to be finished to launch.

Inner Circle rule: sketch the ladder, pick **one** level, and focus on that one offer and one funnel until it reaches the **Two Comma Club** ($1,000,000 gross in a single funnel; **Two Comma Club X** is $10 million). Pick a middle or back-end level (a presentation or phone funnel) because the price is high enough to cover ad costs and still profit.

Liz Benny built a webinar funnel selling a $1,000 course with the Perfect Webinar in 90 days, and was not allowed the next tier until it had made $1 million (1,000 sales of $1,000). She got there within a year; then her high-ticket coaching filled in days from one email to her customer list. A simple two-tier ladder is enough for many businesses.

ClickFunnels sold a $1,000 offer (a course plus ClickFunnels access): 2,500 the first year, 100 of whom joined the Inner Circle. After about 10,000 sales the market saturated and ads got harder, so they launched a book (a book funnel) to bring in a new audience who then ascend to the webinar.

The sequence: focus on one funnel; after it sells and customers press for more, open the back end; when ads for the core offer stop working, build new front-end offers.

## Funnel stacking

Brunson's top-level matches in wrestling were won with two or three fundamental moves, not the flashy camp moves. The same holds for funnels: people with thousands of variations (upsells, downsells, cross sales, crazy email sequences) are often making no money. Of the ten core funnels, build **one funnel for each step** of your ladder; about 95 percent of his revenue comes from three: book funnels, webinar funnels and high-ticket funnels.

**Funnel stacking** is moving a customer from one funnel into another funnel higher on the ladder, for example squeeze → book → webinar → application. The specific types and the order are not fixed. It happens on the funnel pages and through the follow-up funnels: each funnel has its own Soap Opera Sequence whose call to action leads up to the next funnel. Done right, it lets you outspend competitors, give clients more value and scale predictably.

### Ascending from one step to the next

Place the first step of the next funnel on the last page of the previous one. For example, the "Next Step" button on a squeeze funnel's thank-you page goes to a challenge funnel; at the end of the challenge, invite everyone to a graduation webinar that pitches the current offer. His live stacks:

- **Lead funnels → next funnel**
  - Squeeze → challenge: the Marketing Secrets Blackbook thank-you page lists Step #1 subscribe to the podcast, Step #2 download your gifts, Step #3 join the One Funnel Away Challenge.
  - Survey → unboxing: the ClickFunnels home-page survey's results page ("Mindy And Mandy Are Building Their Downline, JUST LIKE YOU!") offers a free 14-day trial.
  - Summit → challenge: the 30 Days summit's special-offer page sells the One Funnel Away Challenge for $100.
- **Unboxing funnels → next funnel**: buyers are much more likely to buy again if you give them the chance.
  - Book → webinar: the free-plus-shipping *Expert Secrets* thank-you page pushes to Funnel Builder Secrets (packages at $1,997, $2,997 and $5,997).
  - Cart → book: the Perfect Webinar Secrets thank-you page is an offer wall that includes the *DotCom Secrets* free book.
  - Challenge → webinar: after the challenge's final mission, a post in the Facebook group invites challengers to a "top secret next mission," a webinar selling the current offer.
- **Presentation funnels → next funnel**: thank-you pages push into another presentation funnel or an application funnel.
  - VSL → application: the 10X Secrets thank-you page has Step 2 "Need My Help...?" leading to the Inner Circle application.
  - Webinar → application: the CF Certified Partner webclass thank-you page has "What's Next?" leading to the partner application.
  - Product launch → application: the offer page has an "Apply To Work With Russell" button.

### Why stack

Each page builds the relationship and each funnel deepens it. Slow movers finish a funnel and stop, which is fine: the Attractive Character and the follow-up funnels carry the relationship until they reengage. Some people buy a book and nothing else for three or four years; others buy a book and pay more than $25,000 within a week. If you don't offer fast movers something now, they go and solve their problem somewhere else. More offers seen means more money per person, which lets you outspend every competitor.

The guardrail: launch your first funnel before you have the others. Build funnel two after funnel one succeeds, then add the push from one to two. Inner Circle members must make at least $1 million in a funnel (the Two Comma Club) before creating their second: after the webinar hits it, create the high-ticket coaching, promote it to past webinar subscribers and add it to the thank-you page; or sell 1,000 or 5,000 books, then create the webinar, promote it to book buyers and stack it on the end of the book funnel. Customers are ready to buy now, so start serving them with your first offer and your first funnel.
