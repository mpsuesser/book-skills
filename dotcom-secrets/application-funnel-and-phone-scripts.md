# Back-end phone funnels: the application funnel and the phone scripts

Above about $2,000, conversions rise when you change the selling environment from online to offline (phase 7). At the keyboard the prospect has full control: they can close the page or scroll past the ad, and all you can do is throw out better hooks and stories. Once rapport is built, move them somewhere you have more control (at a live event you have their full attention and can choreograph the room for selling). For offers from about $2,000 up to $1 million or more, get offline as fast as you can; the most consistently profitable way is the **application funnel**: they fill out an application, then you call and sell. Businesses that already sell everything by phone use it to get higher-quality leads who arrive warm.

## The application funnel

Pages: **"Free Case Study" or "Success Story" page** (video, Apply) → **application page** (video, questions, Apply) → **homework page** (video, bullets).

Origin: Matt Bacak mailed a free CD ($4.95 shipping) that taught his business model holding nothing back; days later his team called to offer an $8,000 workshop, and 1 in 10 signed up. Facing layoffs one Christmas, Brunson put his best product on a $4.95 DVD, sold more than 800 to a small list, waited until buyers had it, and called: "Did you watch the DVD yet?" (no: go watch it, I'll call back in an hour), then invited them to a $5,500 January workshop. A dozen noes the first day, the first yes on day four, and 11 had wired $5,500 by Christmas. Create the lead online, sell offline. Scaled to 60+ salespeople calling 6,000 DVD buyers a week, it cost about $1 million a month in overhead and he shut the call center. The fix: call only the people who were interested and applied. He emailed an application to his best customers asking why they'd be a good fit; dozens came in within hours, each selling him, and two salespeople picked whom they'd enjoy working with. He tested one, two, then three pages. In the end, 2 salespeople calling applications replaced 60, with the same gross revenue and almost ten times the net.

The phone scripts: the **Four-Question Close** when the expert takes the calls without salespeople; the **Setter and Closer scripts** when a two-person sales team does.

**Page 1, "Success Story" or "Free Case Study" page.** Show the big result you can get them and cast the vision. Three goals: (1) they watch the success story or case study and see what's possible; (2) they can apply if they want help getting the same result; (3) you get their email so you can follow up if they don't finish the application. Two versions:
- **Success story**: a past client who got what they'll want. His Inner Circle page: "WARNING: Inner Circle Is Limited To Only 100 People (33 People In Each Group) 97/100 Spots Already Gone..."; "I'm Looking For A Few More Of My Dream Clients... If That's You... I Will PERSONALLY Work With You One-On-One..."; Liz Benny's story video; a Tony Robbins testimonial; Apply Now. It works for any business: Dr. Woolner's chiropractic page ("GET NECK & BACK PAIN RELIEF NATURALLY WITHOUT DRUGS, SURGERY, OR OTHER HARMFUL PROCEDURES"), a success-story video, a limited-time offer of a free one-hour massage and home care kit with the initial exam, "Apply To Work With Dr. Woolner," and a countdown.
- **Free case study**: what you did for yourself or someone else. "FREE CASESTUDY: $89,230 TO A TINY LIST OF PEOPLE, IN LESS THAN 3 DAYS!", "THE BLACK BOX... A Simple 3 Step Funnel To Sell Almost Anything... Without Actually Selling Anything!", with an email opt-in for the infographic and an application for a strategy session. Show exactly *what* you did (the strategy) step by step, never *how* (the tactics): wanting the how makes them apply.

The next two pages pre-frame and presell the reasons to sign up.

**Page 2, application page.** Follow up by email until they complete the longer application. The goal is to get them selling you on why you should work with them, a **takeaway** sale in which they sell you, and that positioning carries into the phone call. Put a video on the page explaining what working with you looks like, then a **testimonial rush** (as many video testimonials back to back as you have) as social proof while they fill it in. His header: "You Are Just 1 Step Away! Please Fill Out This Short Application Now..." The questions that matter:
- **Revenue qualifiers**: make sure they are able to buy, not just willing, without asking outright whether they can afford it. What type of business do you own? How much do you spend on ads each month? How often do you go out to eat? How much do you set aside each month for investments? How much have you spent in the past trying to solve this problem?
- **What does success look like for you?** Someone expecting $10 million in 12 months with no product is misaligned, and you can't make them happy.
- **The Dan Sullivan Question**: if we were having this discussion three years from today and you looked back over those three years, what has to have happened in your life, personally and professionally, for you to feel happy with your progress?
- **Why would you be a good fit for the group?** His coaching is always in groups: they sell him on why they belong in the "cool kids' club," which protects the program.
- **How can you contribute to the group?** "Givers are always more successful than takers": attract givers, repel takers.

**Page 3, homework page.** His: "Congratulations! We Have Your Application. Here's What To Expect Next..." Step #1 watch the video ("This Is Why I'm SO PASSIONATE..."): the application is received; it's reviewed and pre-approved within 24 to 48 hours; if pre-approved, a coach calls; then an email arrives with week one's training. Step #2 "Jump To Front Of The Line - Call Us NOW!" (50 to 100 applications a day), with photos of the people they'll talk to. Three goals:
1. **Build connection with the Attractive Character.** Now they're deciding whether you're the one they want on the journey. A video of your story; people call in tears because it matches theirs.
2. **Tell them what to expect and give them homework**: videos or reading that prepare them. Pre-indoctrinated prospects convert higher.
3. **Get them to start the phone call.** A call they make is worth four times a call you make (you make a quarter as much when you call them). Still call every application, but calling in shows who is serious; look at their applications first.

## The Four-Question Close

For the expert making the calls on high-ticket offers ($2,000 to $8,000 and beyond); it ties back to the application questions. Built on Dan Sullivan's *The Dan Sullivan Question*, and learned from Perry Belcher and Greg Cassar. It is really four phases: four main questions with follow-ups that dig until you get complete answers. The doodle: (1) a person picturing the future, with external and internal desire circles; (2) the person hitting a wall; (3) the person on the wall asking "Resources?" and looking toward the goal; (4) the person over the wall asking "Help?" while you stand ready.

Before the call they apply (prequalification), then an assistant books a 30-minute call. **Open by taking control**, without small talk, which invites rambling: say who you are, that you'll ask four questions, and that depending on their answers and how well you get along, you'll both decide whether to move forward. "Sound fair enough?" They say yes or the call ends; that micro-commitment sets the ground rules.

1. **The future.** Imagine we start working together today and I do everything I can to get you results; a year from now, in a coffee shop, what has to have happened personally and professionally for you to feel happy with your progress and believe it was the best decision you ever made? You want their **external and internal** goals. If they can't answer, don't take them: you can never satisfy someone who can't say what they want. They'll start with external desires ($10,000 a month, a boat, a house in a better school district, weight lost, a better marriage). Follow up with *why* until the inner reasons appear (to prove to family they're a good provider; a charity; kids who aren't challenged; hope for a family that hasn't come yet). Everyone ultimately wants **respect, inclusion and purpose**; when an answer reveals one of the three ("I just want my parents or spouse to respect me," "I want my life to have a purpose," a legacy, being part of something bigger), move on.
2. **The obstacles.** You clearly know what you want, so why don't you have it yet? What's been holding you back? If they blame other people or outside circumstances ("My spouse doesn't support me," a program that "didn't work. That guy was useless."), you can't help them and don't want them as a client. You want responsibility, some version of "I don't know how," with the word *I* in it. If they talk about themselves and lack know-how you can supply, it will be a successful relationship.
3. **The resources.** What resources, talents, connections or skills do you have that you're not using to their full potential, that we could use to overcome the obstacles and reach the goals? Give them time; keep asking "What else?" until they run out. Then review: you want ___ because ___; you haven't got there because of ___ and ___, right?; and you have all these resources you aren't leveraging yet, right? Then ask how much more money they'd make (or weight they'd lose, or how much better the marriage would be) if they removed the obstacles and used those resources, and let them tell you.
4. **The help.** "So I only have one more question. Do you want me to help you to achieve your goals?" Then stop talking until they answer. Most say yes. Then: here's how it works; my fee is $___; for that you get ___; I can transfer you to my assistant to handle the financial details now; would you like that? Usually the only reason for a no here is money: help them find it or offer a payment plan. Done well, with prospects who can afford the fee, you close most of the people you talk to.

## The Setter and Closer scripts

For when the expert isn't on the phone; it's the script for growing and scaling a sales team, and it takes **takeaway selling** further. Two people interview the prospect to see if they're a fit. It works because a $2,000 to $100,000 program is much easier to sell on the phone (the extra step makes people comfortable paying more), and because higher-level programs mean working closely with people, so you screen them and don't accept anyone who isn't a good fit. Two salespeople following the script make sales consistent.

The **setter** gathers the basics, draws out emotions, and identifies the prospect's pain and goals, then gets off the phone. The **closer** calls back, magnifies the pain, gets the prospect to sell themselves on why they're a good fit, and provides the solution. The flow: Setter: Intro → Questions → Blast → Posture → Probe → Finding Credit → Goals → Commitments, then the hand-off, then Closer: Time → Decision-Making → Resources → Knowledge → What's Included → Close.

### The setter

1. **Introduction.** Low-key and natural. Learn where they are right now and *how they feel* about it.
2. **Questions.** Where do they want to be, and the real reason why. They want $100,000 a year; what would it let them do? Quit the job, stay home with the kids, sail around the world, buy their aging parents a home, show their ex? The *why* is where the emotion is: people buy on emotion first, then rationalize with logic. Hook the emotions with follow-up questions (the kids' names and ages; why homeschool; how it would feel) and help them paint the *feelings* behind the dream. Within five minutes the setter knows why they'll buy and what their hot buttons are. Then: "What's holding you back? Why haven't you achieved your dreams already?" Expect some version of *I don't know how*: "no time" means they don't know how to do it in five hours a week; "no money" means they don't know how to do it with other people's money. Then ask: if you knew how to build a business in just five hours a week, would you do it? Saying yes starts them selling themselves. The setter cannot move on until they know the hot buttons and the prospect has admitted, in some form, that they don't know how and need help.
3. **Blast** (a taste of what you offer, no great detail). It's impossible to work with everyone who applies; my job is to weed out those who aren't ready and find the right people; maybe we can help; let me explain what we do. Then the pivotal question: if you could work one-on-one with [the expert] or someone like them, do you think you'd be successful? ("Absolutely!") How come? Then shut up and let them talk: if *they* list the reasons, it's the truth to them. Repeat their reasons back and confirm: so with the chance to work with [the expert], you believe you'd succeed, you could get [what they want], you'd know how to get there? Then: why do you think you'd be a good candidate for this program?
4. **Posture.** Set up the closer as the expert: I'm not an expert at this myself; my job is to find qualified people; if I feel good about you, I'll turn you over to our program director, who decides who fits; first I need a short profile of where you are professionally and financially and where you want to be. "Is it all right if I ask you a few questions?" That permission covers almost anything.
5. **Probe** (financial information, filled into a form). Start with quick, unemotional questions (age, marital status, highest education). Always ask whether anyone else is involved in the business, a spouse or a financial partner; if so, get them on the phone right then, because presenting without every decision maker wastes the call.
6. **Finding credit.** How would you rate your credit, and why? If [the expert] wrote a check to pay off all your debt, how much would it be? How much of that is major credit card debt? What's the total combined credit extended to you? Combined credit minus total debt is what's available; look for more available than the program costs. Talking through balances shows you're trying to help pay them off, not add to them: they may need one step back to take ten forward. Also ask about savings, investments, owning or renting, and retirement accounts.
7. **Goals** (short term, still getting them to sell themselves). Where do you want the business in six months? How long have you been trying, and how successful have you been? In 12 months, where do you want to be, and what would make you feel good? Working one-on-one with [the expert], could you achieve these goals? Can you see it? How come?
8. **Commitments.** Getting them to declare themselves a good candidate, publicly stating they're someone who acts and finishes, makes the declaration very hard for their brain to reverse later. You look like a candidate I could recommend to my director; first there are four commitments: (1) a minimum time commitment of ___ per week; (2) being coachable and following the experts' advice (why are you coachable?); (3) being a quick decision maker who can start today (when is the best time to start? You want "right now." Then: if it's a fit for both of us, is anything holding you back from starting today?); (4) using OPM (other people's money): explain using the bank's money (credit cards) as a short-term lever to invest in growing the business or reaching their goals. Then: we have two levels, ___ [lower price] and ___ [higher price]; how much are you comfortable investing to start today, and why that amount?

**The hand-off.** The setter's job is done. Tell them the director (the closer, often titled something like Director of Coaching) will call to see if they're a good candidate; have them write down the director's name; say the director is an expert at ___ and makes sure the team gets the right people, that this isn't for everybody, and not to be offended if they don't get a spot. Give them homework while they wait: write down a six-month and a 12-month financial goal, and three things they want besides money. The setter then briefs the closer on everything before the second call.

### The closer

The closer reinforces the prospect's decisions by running the same ground again, worded differently, until they can picture exactly what life looks like after success with the program. Why are you serious about ___ right now? How long have you been thinking about it? What's the biggest thing that's held you back? What do you want in six months, and what would that do for you? In 12 months? In five years, what would your lifestyle be like? Then connect yourself to their dream: if you had the chance to work with someone like [the expert], how would it change your life? Anything else?

Then the four commitments again, framed as a takeaway: my job is to find only the very best people; this isn't for everybody; these are simple yes-or-no questions; are you willing? If an answer isn't the one you need, backtrack to find out why, or get off the phone, because they aren't a likely close.
1. **Time.** Can you commit to ___ hours per week? (Yes or no.)
2. **Decision-making.** Opportunities don't wait around; is there anything that would hold you back from deciding to work with [the expert] today? (Yes or no.)
3. **Resources.** Write this number down: ___. As long as you see the value and the program meets your goals, is there any reason you couldn't invest $___ today? (Yes or no.) If the earlier steps were done right, the price is no longer a big deal; everything before was set up so they don't object to money here.
4. **Knowledge (teachability).** My main concern is that students are teachable, willing to learn and to implement; are you that kind of person? (Yes or no.) How come? So if someone showed you how to do ___, you'd be successful? (Yes or no.)
5. **What's included.** We give you everything you need to succeed and avoid mistakes, and your coach helps you work at your own speed; then list exactly what they get.
6. **Close.** "This is probably the most important question: Why do you feel like you are a good candidate for this program?" They sell themselves again, and all that's left is taking the card. They're asking you to *let them* buy. That's takeaway selling: it makes the sale easier and finds the people who will actually be a good fit.
