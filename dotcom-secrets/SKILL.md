# DotCom Secrets

Traffic problems and conversion problems are rarely the real problem: "More often than not, it's a FUNNEL problem." Dan Kennedy: "Ultimately, the business that can spend the most to acquire a customer wins." Kennedy again: "A business beats its competition by making the same prospect worth more to his business than to that of his competition." Fix the funnel so each customer is worth more, and you can outspend every competitor for the same traffic. Does a business need a funnel? "Only if your company needs leads or sales." (Mike Filsaime's McDonald's math: the burger makes $0.18 over the ad cost; "Do you want fries and a Coke with that?" adds $1.32 profit, eight times the first.)

A website is a glorified brochure: so many buttons, menus and calls to action that it confuses people, and "a confused mind always says no." A **sales funnel** works like the best salesperson standing outside your store: get the visitor's name and contact details, ask what they want, walk them straight to it past every distraction, then offer what complements it. Each page asks for one thing. The layers: lead capture produces **leads**, the sales process produces **prospects**, the order and upsells produce **customers**, and ascension produces **clients**.

## The core system

**The Secret Formula.** Four questions under everything in the book: (1) Who is your dream customer? (2) Where are they already congregating? (3) What bait (hook, story, offer) will attract them? (4) What unique result can you create for them? "If you change your bait, you'll change your customer." Customers come for a result, not your product.

**Hook, Story, Offer.** Every piece of bait (ad, email, landing page, sales page, upsell, webinar, phone call) carries all three. The **hook** grabs attention just long enough to tell the story; the **story** builds the relationship and raises the perceived value of the offer; the **offer** is the one thing you ask for. "If something isn't working in your funnel, it's always either the hook, the story, or the offer." An offer is stacked until its total value is at least ten times the price; price resistance means the value isn't high enough yet. A **commodity** (something sold elsewhere too) gets restructured into an offer unique to you.

**The Value Ladder.** Offers in ascending value and price: bait → front end → middle → back end, plus continuity. Each rung is a different way to fulfill the **Value Ladder Mission Statement**: "We help (insert who) to (insert result they want to achieve) through (insert your new opportunity)." "Your value ladder is your actual business plan." Each tier has its funnel family:

| Tier | Price | Funnels |
|---|---|---|
| Front-end lead funnels | $0 | lead "squeeze," survey, summit |
| Unboxing funnels | $1–$100 | book, cart, challenge |
| Presentation funnels | $100–$2,000 | video sales letter, webinar (live or automated), product launch |
| Back-end phone funnels | $2,000+ | application |

"The deeper your funnel is... the more each customer will be worth to you. And the more they are worth to you, the more you can spend to acquire them." **Funnel stacking** moves a customer from one funnel to the next one up the ladder, through the last page of each funnel and through its follow-up emails.

**The Attractive Character.** The personality you build for your market: four elements (backstory, parables, character flaws, polarity), one identity (Leader, Adventurer, Reporter, Reluctant Hero) and six storylines. "They come in looking for a result, but they stay because of their relationship with you."

**Funnel hacking.** Before building any funnel, find others already succeeding with one in your market and model the framework (page order, layout, price points), never their words or art. Build only where someone is already succeeding with dream customers in that market.

**The seven phases of a funnel.** (1) traffic temperature, (2) the pre-frame bridge, (3) qualify subscribers, (4) qualify buyers, (5) identify hyperactive buyers, (6) age and ascend the relationship, (7) change the selling environment. A **pre-frame** is the state someone is in as they enter the next step, and each step pre-frames the next. Traffic is **hot** (knows you), **warm** (knows other solutions) or **cold** (knows only the problem); each gets its own bridge and headline. "If you're stuck in your business, it's probably because there's a problem somewhere inside of these seven phases."

**Traffic you control, earn and own.** Paid traffic is controlled; social, search and PR traffic is earned; your lists are owned. "Your one and only goal is to own all the traffic you can": send controlled traffic to a squeeze page, earned traffic to a funnel hub. A rule of thumb is about $1 per name per month.

**Follow-up funnels.** A **Soap Opera Sequence** (five emails, each opening a loop the next one closes) bonds new subscribers to the Attractive Character and pushes them through the funnel and up the ladder. **Daily Seinfeld Emails** (entertainment, each a hook, story and offer) go every day to everyone not in a sequence.

**Upsells.** Use an **order form bump** (a checkbox before Submit; about a third take it, and it often covers the ad cost) and **two upsells**; more hurts lifetime value in book funnels. The **OTO** rule: for information products, sell the next thing they need, never more of the same; for physical products, sell more of the same at a discount. Keep the buying loop open ("WAIT! Your order is not yet complete") instead of thanking them for the purchase.

**CPA and ACV.** **Cost Per Acquisition** is ad spend ÷ customers. **Average Cart Value** is funnel sales ÷ customers. "If CPA > ACV, your funnel is broken. If CPA < ACV, your funnel is working," and you spend as much as you can.

## Applying the book

The DotCom Secrets process, in order. Steps 1 to 4 are strategy; 5 to 12 build and grow. Start at the step the user's situation reaches, and fill every script and worksheet completely, every part answered in the user's own material.

1. **Dream customer.** Name exactly who they are (an avatar with a name, traits and a picture). Done when you could write every message to that one person.
2. **Congregations.** List where they already gather (sites, forums, Facebook groups, influencers, podcasts, newsletters, blogs, YouTube channels, keywords).
3. **Hooks and stories.** Find the hooks that capture their attention and the stories that raise the perceived value of the offer; stack the offer to 10x its price.
4. **Relationship and ladder.** Build the Attractive Character, and sketch the value ladder with its mission statement, so you can ascend dream customers and serve them at the highest level possible.
5. **Pick one tier and one funnel.** Usually the middle or back end first, where the price covers ad costs. Model it from at least half a dozen funnel-hacked examples.
6. **Build and plug in.** Build the funnel's pages in order, then put the headlines, videos and scripts on each page and write the Soap Opera Sequence. Done when every page has its hook, story and one offer.
7. **Launch the test.** Spend what you'd make if one customer bought everything in the funnel, and find where the hooks, stories and offers aren't converting.
8. **Split-test** new pages against the controls and repeat until ACV > CPA.
9. **Keep making new ads** to hold CPA down while you raise ACV. Repeat until the funnel reaches the **Two Comma Club** ($1 million in one funnel).
10. **Next funnel.** Only then build the next funnel on the ladder, email existing customers about it, and stack it onto the first funnel.
11. **Repeat steps 6 to 8** until it, too, hits the Two Comma Club.
12. **Pick the next funnel** on the ladder.

## Diagnostics

- **Hook, story or offer?** Every failing page, ad or email is failing on one of the three. Ask what the hook is and how to make it better, whether the story builds the perceived value of the offer, and how to make the offer sexier.
- **The numbers first.** Compute CPA and each page's contribution to ACV (price × conversion rate). Pages under the benchmarks are where you call audibles; ads with CPA above ACV get cut.
- **One thing per page.** Each step asks for one action; a second ask is a brick wall.
- **Simple wins.** Most winning funnels are three to five pages with one good Soap Opera Sequence. Complexity hides what's broken.
- **Temperature mismatch.** All traffic sent to one landing page, or cold traffic with no bridge page, means the message starts in the wrong place: "Start your copy wherever your prospect is."
- **No ladder.** If the relationship ends when the funnel ends, there's no back end to ascend to; a boring front end means the business will struggle.
- **Resold upsells.** A long upsell video or a sales pitch on the order form is the cardinal mistake: they already said yes, so confirm it and ask for the second yes.
- **Real urgency.** Every deadline and limit is a true one; fake urgency backfires and costs all credibility.
- **Your opinion.** The only vote that counts is the customer's credit card.
- **Expect the flop.** Most funnels fail on the first launch; it's the test that shows where to adjust. Three or four rounds is normal.
