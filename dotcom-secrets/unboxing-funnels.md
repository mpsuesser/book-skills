# Unboxing funnels: book, cart, challenge

An offer worth $100 to $2,000 would normally be sold with a presentation. The alternative, earlier in the ladder, is to **unbox** it: take the whole offer (say $997) apart and sell one piece on each page. The *Expert Secrets* bundle ($840) unboxed: Book $27 → order-form bump Audiobook $19 → Upsell #1 Expert Evolution Home Study Course $297 → Upsell #2 Traffic Secrets Home Study Course $497. More people become customers, and the average cart value rises high enough to spend a lot to acquire each one.

## Rules every unboxing funnel shares

**Free-plus-shipping and the 100 Visitor Test.** Sending 100 visitors to a $197 product at 1 percent: one buyer, $197. Sending 100 visitors to a free CD (they cover shipping) splintered off from one of the best parts of that same product: 8 percent took it with almost no copy, and 25 percent of those took the $197 upsell. That was twice the money and seven times the customers (8 buyers instead of 1). The first yes removes the friction: their card is already out and they've committed, and a stored card makes the upsells one-click. A digital giveaway works in lead funnels, but it loses the qualification that paying shipping gives and the one-click upsell. Selling the product with "free shipping" instead tested at almost no dip, often an increase. The principle: a low-ticket first offer gets more buyers committed with a first yes, then you show them the other pieces.

**The order form bump.** "Upsell hell" (dozens of upsells) makes short-term money and leaves customers who won't buy again, which stops ladder progression. The **sweet spot is two upsells**: more hurts lifetime value, fewer makes it hard to make money. The bump adds money without counting as an upsell: a small checkbox on the order form, after the card details and before Submit, offering a lower-ticket product; the digital version of the gum, Tic Tacs and tabloids at the grocery checkout. Example: Lead Funnels Swipe File $7, with a bump: "YES! Upgrade My Order NOW! ONE TIME OFFER - ONLY $37," training on each of the 106 lead funnels plus 30 pre-designed templates (a $257 value), for a $44 total. About 33 percent of buyers take the bump, with no drop in lifetime value. "In most funnels, my profit from the order form bump covers all my ad costs, leaving the other upsells in the funnel as pure profit!" Typical shape: free plus shipping $0 → bump $37 (33%+) → upsell $297 → upsell $497. Use a bump instead of a third upsell.

**Average cart value** is why unboxing works: a $20 CPA against a $100 ACV is $80 profit per customer, a slot machine that pays $100 for every $20 you put in. "It's not complicated, but it's the real secret."

The book funnel (authors, speakers, coaches, consultants, info products at the front) and the cart funnel (e-commerce, physical products) are built the same way with different strategies; the challenge funnel gets a quick result, delivers massive value and indoctrinates people into how you work with your tribe.

## Book funnel

Pages: **sales page** (order form, often with a bump) → **upsell 1** → **upsell 2** (or a downsell) → **thank-you page** (access the purchase, next step).

A book makes you seem more legit (the difference between you and Tony Robbins or Brendon Burchard, a friend told Dr. Woolner, is that they have a book). Brunson funnel-hacked dozens of book funnels, sold more than 100,000 copies, and built book funnels for Tony Robbins, Dave Asprey, Grant Cardone and Robert Kiyosaki; the framework works for almost any book. It works for any free-plus-shipping information product: an MP3 player preloaded with 257 podcast episodes ("Marketing In Your Car"), CDs, DVDs, reports, booklets, the one-page Perfect Webinar script with a DVD. Without a book yet, a CD or DVD is faster and works just as well.

**Page 1, sales page.** Two layouts:
- **Layout 1, free-plus-shipping.** Top: a Curiosity-Based Headline as the hook. Left: story and offer in a video (Who, What, Why, How, or Star, Story, Solution). Right: the order form. His *DotCom Secrets* page: "FREE Book!", copies-sold proof, a Tony Robbins quote, "We Pay For The Book, You Cover Shipping." Use a **two-step order form**: step one asks "Where should I ship this?" (address); step two takes the card for shipping and handling. Say on the first page that they'll be charged shipping and handling, or it's unethical and upsets customers. People fill in step one more readily because they see no card field, and once in they're committed, so they finish; step-one conversions often beat an email squeeze despite asking for a full address, because physical mail has higher perceived value than digital. Example: "Get Your FREE Copy Of Expert Secrets Shipped In Less Than 7 Days!" with steps 1 SHIPPING, 2 UPGRADE, the note that it retails for $19.95 and shipping is just $7.95 US or $14.95 international, and a bump for Story Selling Secrets at a 90 percent discount ($497 → $37, "Almost 63% Of Our Most Successful Members Choose This Upgrade"), $44.95 total.
- Below the top, three blocks: **Emotion** (the emotion-based headline, story video and order form; most buyers are emotional and buy here); **Logic** (a long list of bullets showing logically what they'll get); **Fear** (urgency and scarcity for those who buy only when the offer may be taken away).
- **Layout 2, value before price.** For a front-end digital product at $7 to $27 that needs value built first: Emotion, Logic, Fear, and the full order form at the bottom, so the price doesn't push them away before they've seen the value.

**The bump on a book funnel** must need no explaining: a few seconds, like the checkout aisle. "Would you like to add the audio book for an extra $27?" "...a special behind-the-scenes training where you'll learn ___ for an extra $37?" "...our templates that let you ___ for an additional $47?" The more it needs explaining, the worse it converts. Place it after the card details, before Submit; earlier usually hurts conversions, because the purchase must already have happened in their mind.

**Page 2, upsell pages.** The OTO comes immediately after purchase. Keep the buying loop open: "Thank you for your purchase" tells them they're done and conversions stop. Use "WAIT! Your order is not yet finished. Please customize your order now." (His page: a "do not press back" warning against double charges, "Order Customization - Step 2 Of 3," "WAIT! Your Order Is Not Yet Complete... Get The 'Expert Secrets' Audiobook And Get These 6 Other Courses For FREE!", video, $47 single payment, was $97, a countdown, a No Thanks link.) Then the OTO script confirms the first decision and earns the upgrade.
- **The OTO rule for information products: don't sell more of the same thing.** It's the biggest mistake. Someone who bought a six-pack-abs e-book has solved that problem in their mind; another abs course won't sell. Sell the next thing they need: "Every time you solve a problem for someone, it creates a new problem" (a new car needs gas; a funnel book creates the need for funnel software). Ask: "What is the next logical product or service they need to reach their goals?" Brunson's first OTO came from Mike Filsaime asking what else he could do to serve buyers after they had his $37 potato-gun DVD. Their biggest complaint was hunting down the pipes, igniters and supplies, so he offered a $197 kit; one in three took it, which added $65 to the average order ($102 a day in revenue against $50 a day in ads), and he stayed profitable even at $3 or more per click.
- **The No Thanks link** sits right under the Order button, big enough to see, or they think the order is finished, leave, and miss the thank-you page, the download and the other offers. Its wording gives no positive action (not "No Thanks, Take Me to the Member's Area Instead"); make them acknowledge what they're turning down: "No Thanks, I Don't Want to Add This Amazing Bonus at a 95% Discount to My Order Right Now."
- **Downsell** (optional): after a no, a cheaper version: remove elements, switch physical to digital, or offer a payment plan. Not essential, but a powerful ACV boost for little effort. His: the One Funnel Away Challenge for $100 → "WAIT! Before You Go / Want To Get Digital-ONLY Access... For Just $47? (That's Less Than Half The Cost!)"
- **Upsell 2** (optional): built the same way with another offer under the same rule. Ask: "What is the next logical thing they need to be more successful? What new opportunities have you created for them that you can now help them solve?"
- A book funnel never has more than two upsells (two upsells, or one upsell and one downsell); more lowers lifetime value.

**Page 3, thank-you page.** Thank them; say how to get the digital products (the members' area) or how and when the physical ones ship; point them to the next funnel, usually a webinar (presentation funnel) or an application (phone funnel). Dean Graziosi's *Millionaire Success Habits* thank-you page: "Register Your Seat Before You Miss Out On This Webclass That You Already Paid For..." The presentation was already part of the book offer, so registering keeps them climbing.

## Cart funnel

Pages: **special offer page** → **bump** → **upsell 1** → **upsell 2** → **thank-you page** with an **offer wall** (more products, each with Add to Cart).

FiberFix (Harmon Brothers, who made the Squatty Potty video) had a great video and weak website sales. The old site was a product grid like an Amazon listing (Single Pack $5.99 plus shipping, Starter Kit $24.99, Medium $49.99, Large $99.99). Most e-commerce sellers live on organic traffic because their ACV is too low to pay for ads, and a page of dozens of products fails because "A confused mind always says no!"

**Choosing the front-end offer**: look at every SKU in your store and pick the sexiest, most interesting one, or simply the best seller. Marcus Lemonis asked about a dozen Camping World employees for the best-selling product, got dissolving toilet paper, and offered four rolls free plus $4.95 shipping and handling. FiberFix's video had an amazing hook and story and a horrible offer; it changed from "buy some FiberFix" to three one-inch wraps with two two-inch wraps free and one four-inch wrap free, bundling the best seller with lesser-known products so "FREE" appears twice in the headline.

**Page 1, sales page.** The best way to sell a physical product is a **demo** (Billy Mays with OxiClean and the Quick Chop); the demo is the hook and the story, then it makes the offer. E-commerce headlines use less curiosity and tell exactly what the offer is. His FiberFix page: a scarcity notice that the product may sell out because of the video's popularity, the headline, the demo video, a two-step form, and "normally retails for $40.95... Starter Kit For Just $24.99 Plus FREE Shipping." Step two has two levers: a **quantity selector** above the form (3+3 FREE $24.99; 6+6 $49.98; 9+9 FREE $74.97 highlighted and preselected as "Best Deal - For True Manly Men!"; 12+12 $99.96; 15+15 $124.95; 30+30 $249.90) and the **bump** ("Yes, Add A One Heat Wrap Now! ONE TIME OFFER ($9.97)," normally $19.95). Good physical-product bumps: rush shipping, complementary products.

**Page 2, upsell pages.** The biggest difference from the book funnel: **for physical products, sell more of what they just bought, at a discount.** FiberFix upsell 1: one or two more starter kits at $19.99 each, for themselves or as gifts ("WAIT! Grab One More 'Starter Kit' For $19.99... Fiber Fix Makes Great Gifts!"; No Thanks: "No Thanks, I Don't Want More FiberFix At This HUGE Discount - I'll Pass At This Time"). It needs no video, because there's no new offer to explain; stating the special one-time offer is enough. Keep the No Thanks link close to the Order button, showing what they'll miss. From the second upsell on, work like a traditional shopping cart: the next logical product (hot dogs → buns, ketchup, mustard; a supplement → something else the dream customer suffers from), taken from your other SKUs in best-seller order. FiberFix upsell 2: the number-two seller, a heat wrap bundle with two large four-inch wraps and three rigid patches for $24.99 ("Save $15 Instantly," two sets $49.98); then a last chance: "Upgrade To The 'MANLY MAN' Kit," a storage bag with rolls, rigid packs, heat wraps and repair pens thrown in free. Cart funnels can run three or four offers after the purchase without hurting lifetime value, because shoppers are used to Amazon showing many SKUs.

**Page 3, thank-you page and offer wall.** Two jobs: thank them and say what to expect (when the order arrives, how to reach customer service); and direct them into the next funnels through an **offer wall** of other offers. Trey Lewellen's flashlight funnel thank-you page ("Please SAVE & PRINT This Page... Tons Of Cool Bonuses"): Step #1 the receipt, a call to claim $20 off, and recharger kits to add while the order processes, under a countdown; Step #2 six free offers (bottle targets, gun-cleaning oil, silhouette targets) behind Tell Me More buttons. The offer wall raises sales from hyperactive buyers.

## Challenge funnel

Pages: **challenge sales page** (video, Add to Cart) → **bump** → **upsell 1** (video) → **upsell 2** → **thank-you page** (access the purchase).

Gyms sell New Year weight-loss challenges; Natasha Hazlett turned the model into a paid front end. Brunson routes all his book funnels into a challenge, to: get dream customers a result quickly; give every customer the same vocabulary and foundation so he can work with them faster and get bigger results higher on the ladder; indoctrinate them in what he does and how; warm up the right people and repel those who aren't a fit. His One Funnel Away Challenge drew more than 5,000 people on its first run and is woven into the upsell flow of every funnel, the email follow-ups and onboarding calls: "the value ladder doorway that everyone would have to go through."

Natasha's numbers: her free-plus-shipping book (*Unstoppable Influence*) wasn't profitable, so she sold a challenge with a copy of the book for $47 to cold Facebook traffic, with a Perfect Webinar during the challenge. 450 joined in the first two weeks. Webinars before the challenge had shown 18 percent show-up and 4 percent conversion to her $997 offer (the same presentation closed live rooms at 24 percent or more: webinar fatigue). Inside the challenge, 75 percent registered, more than 50 percent showed up and stayed, and conversion went from 4 percent to more than 20 percent. The first challenge brought in over $105,000, she hit the Two Comma Club ten months later, and the average $47 challenger is worth more than $420. The magic is in the funnel structure and in how the challenge is run.

**Designing the challenge.**
- **Length**: 14 to 30 days. Shorter is too short to build trust and a tribe; longer loses interest. (Hers ran 21 days, his 30.)
- **Location**: a closed Facebook group where the audience already spends time daily; archive it afterward and open a new group for each challenge.
- **Training format**: the secret sauce is people getting to know you, which builds a loyal, high-vibe tribe. Best is live video: a set "class time" creates urgency, so they show up, act, get results and want more of you, and watching together builds community. Prerecorded saves time but loses the community and the scarcity (tools such as OneStream or LIVEpigeon stream prerecorded video live). Written posts or emails alone miss the deep connection; add video, preferably live. He hosts the content in a members' area that drips daily and uses the Facebook group for support, accountability and connection.
- **Moving up the ladder**: near the end, a Perfect Webinar offering three months of coaching at $997. Because challengers already have the promised result, nearly 70 percent register; Natasha's show-up averages 50 percent (18 percent when the webinar was advertised directly), and conversion rose from 4 to 20 percent. Or, with no webinar, a limited-time discount: Cristy "CodeRed" Nickel's $997 custom nutrition and accountability, taken by about 10 percent.

**Page 1, sales page.** The offer: join the challenge to get result X in Y days, stated in the headline. Success or failure rests on three things: the clarity of the promise, the challenger's belief that they could achieve it, and the expert's credibility. Its parts:
- **A clear hook**: the result promised, simple and believable. "The 21-Day Challenge to Becoming The Fearless Influencer You're Meant to Be!" Cristy's "Lose your first, next, or last 10 pounds!! (With absolutely NO pills, powders, shakes, or exercise required.)" earned three to four times her ad spend. His: "How Would You Like Us To Hold You By The Hand As You Launch Your First (Or Next) Funnel!" with a start-date countdown and a $100 one-time price.
- **The story**: short and compelling, bonding them to the Attractive Character and showing you've walked the journey and can deliver the result.
- **The path**: the topics covered and how each benefits them, showing the transformation step by step.
- **The prizes**: everyone loves prizes; make them seed the offer you'll make at the end. Natasha's first prize was a home study course, a month of coaching, a 30-minute breakthrough session and a summit ticket ($6,744), then a summit ticket ($697), then a gift pack ($197). Cristy's: home study with a coach and a 90-day hard-core group ($997), six months of accountability ($397), three months ($247).
- **A stack**: a recap of everything they get, each with its value (price is the price tag; value is the transformation it creates). Natasha's $47 challenge: 21-Day Virtual Coaching ($2,100), Digital Companion Workbook ($34.95), Challenge Bundle ($49.95), Recorded Coaching Calls ($297), Private FB Group ($500), Completion Pack ($297), total $3,278.90. His: 30 Days of video missions from Russell ($997), 30 Days of coaching from Stephen Larsen and Julie Stoian ($997), a customized 30-day plan kit ($247), a bonus physical workbook ($97), an MP3 player ($297), the *30 Days* hardcover book ($97), unlimited 30 Days interviews ($197), behind-the-scenes Two Comma Club interviews ($197), total $3,126, for $100.
- **Testimonials**: after the first run, ask challengers to post a Facebook testimonial you can share; screenshots of social posts add credibility.
- **The bump**: something the challenger believes they need to succeed in the challenge, never random extra training, because you want them to consume everything, get results and buy more. Natasha's audiobook (the book was already included), positioned for committed challengers to take everywhere, converts at well over 30 percent. Others: templates, workbooks, supplements, swag. Label it like "OUR MOST SUCCESSFUL CHALLENGERS WILL CHOOSE THIS UPGRADE." His bump is a Funnel Audibles workshop for $37.

**Page 2, upsells.** At least one, possibly two; the best are ones the buyer believes will help them finish the challenge: a private accountability coach ($67, "Want To SUPER SIZE Your Challenge RESULTS?"), since finishing what they start is the market's biggest desire; Cristy's Code Red Starter Kit (cookbook, water bottle, success journal, pop-socket; $89.80 retail for $39.95); a physical journal and workbook (print 100+ pages yourself, or we mail it). Simple tools to complete the challenge and get results, never more information to consume.

**Page 3, thank-you page.** Thank them, explain how to reach the challenge materials, and say how and when shipped items arrive.
