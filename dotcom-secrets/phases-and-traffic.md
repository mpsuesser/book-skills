# The seven phases of a funnel, and traffic

People are sensitive to how they feel at every step of a buying journey (the GNC clerk's "Can I help you with anything?" makes most people want to leave). "The better you can make them feel at each step in the process, the more likely they will keep progressing."

Each step asks for **one thing** and carries its own hook, story and offer (one call to action). Asking for more than one thing puts up a brick wall. Start from the goal of each step, then craft the hook, story and offer around that one thing, so the journey is frictionless.

## The pre-frame

"A pre-frame is simply the state you place someone in as they enter the next step in your sales funnel," and each step in a funnel is the pre-frame for the next one. In the MIT study (from *Sway*), 70 economics students read identical bios of a guest lecturer except for "a very warm person" versus "rather cold"; the first group loved him, the second found him self-centered, formal, irritable and ruthless. Mark Joyner: "not all clicks are created equal." The frame someone enters through often matters more than what you say on the page.

Armand Morin's proof: Brunson usually closed about 15 percent of a room on a $1,997 course; with Armand introducing him, 42 percent. "Same presentation, same offer, same price point, different pre-frame." He made an introduction video for event emcees to play, and after that rarely closed below 40 percent.

The failure mode: optimizing 100 percent for short-term conversions, selling so aggressively that you lose the respect that a long-term relationship (worth ten times the first sale) depends on.

## The phases

1. **Traffic temperature.** Hot, warm or cold (below). Each temperature needs a different bridge, and may need its own landing page.
2. **Pre-frame bridge.** The ad, video, email or article that carries them to the landing page. Short for hot traffic (a quick email with a link, a blog post, a podcast); a little longer for warm (a video that starts from where they are: an ad to Tony Robbins's audience opens with "growth and contribution," which they learned from Tony, then shows how a business lets them contribute); longest for cold, which needs a separate **bridge page** that educates them before the landing page. For hot and warm traffic the ad usually serves as the pre-frame. Example, a neuropathy supplement: hot traffic is your own list reordering on a reminder; warm is people on social media who know they have neuropathy; cold is people with "nerve pain" who have never heard the word, so a bridge page says that nerve pain is probably caused by neuropathy, and then the landing page's language makes sense. Reaching cold traffic expands your universe exponentially.
3. **Qualify subscribers.** An opt-in (squeeze) page offering something of value for contact information: a free report or video that shows one thing they'd really want to know. Someone who won't give an email is unlikely to give money later. (1,000 visitors at 30 percent is 300 subscribers.)
4. **Qualify buyers.** Immediately, not a day or a week later: after Submit, they land on a sales page with the first premium offer, very low ticket (free plus shipping and handling, up to $7 to $10). Kennedy: "a buyer is a buyer is a buyer." Buyers deserve more marketing spend (phone calls, postcards or letters, special offers). Keep two lists, subscribers and buyers, and treat them differently.
5. **Identify hyperactive buyers.** Some buyers want to pay more to solve the problem now. In pain from bowling, Brunson bought a ball, shoes, books and videos within minutes; days later, out of pain, he never bought again. Give people the chance to buy when they want to buy, with upsells and cross sells: if you believe you have the best products, you owe it to them. Put hyperactive buyers on their own sublist and spend more to market to the $1,000 spender than to the $10 one.
6. **Age and ascend the relationship.** Phases 1 to 5 happen at the point of sale, within five to ten minutes. From here the Attractive Character takes the customer by the hand up the ladder through the follow-up funnels.
7. **Change the selling environment.** It's hard to sell $15,000 or $100,000 online with a sales letter and an Add to Cart button. Sell high-ticket offers over the phone, by direct mail, or at a live event: back-end funnels sell the idea, then schedule a call, where you can customize the offer, get live feedback and overcome objections.

**Diagnostic**: "If you're stuck in your business, it's probably because there's a problem somewhere inside of these seven phases." Walk the business like a retail consultant walks a store (the ad, the front door, the greeting, why people chose what they chose, point-of-sale items, the cashier's upsell, the follow-up):
1. Do you know the temperature of each traffic source?
2. What bridge pre-frames each one?
3. Who will give their email for your free offer, and is the landing page qualifying them?
4. Who will give a credit card for your first premium offer?
5. Who will spend more right now, and do your upsells find them?
6. How does the Attractive Character age and ascend the relationship, matching each buyer to the offer they need most?
7. How do you take them outside the online funnel for high-ticket offers?
And: is each group (subscribers, buyers, hyperactive buyers) getting its own tailored experience?

## Traffic temperature

Eugene Schwartz: if the prospect knows your product and that it satisfies their desire, the headline starts with the product; if they don't know the product but have the desire, start with the desire; if they don't yet know what they seek and are concerned only with a general problem, start with the problem and crystallize it into a specific need. The awareness continuum: **Unaware → Problem Aware → Solution Aware → Product Aware → Most Aware**, running from cold to warm to hot. "Start your copy wherever your prospect is."

- **Hot**: knows you and your products; on your list, or following your podcast, blog, Facebook, Instagram or YouTube. Talk to them like friends, through the Attractive Character.
- **Warm**: doesn't know you but knows other solutions exist; the subscribers and fans of others in your market (your **Dream 100**, the people who have already congregated your dream customers). Make them aware of your solution.
- **Cold**: has the problem but doesn't know solutions exist, and hasn't congregated anywhere. The hardest; most effort goes to warm traffic first. Converting cold traffic creates customers rather than moving them from another solution.

## Traffic you control, earn and own

"Your one and only goal is to own all the traffic you can."

The channels are offline media in new form, and the old direct-response funnel (an ad with a phone number → a free report that sells a low-ticket product → a high-ticket offer) runs the same way online: Networking → Facebook; Radio → Podcast; Sitcom → YouTube; Reality TV → Instagram; Newspapers → Blogs; Direct Mail → Email.

- **Traffic you own** (the most important): your email lists, Messenger lists and customer lists. Send a message and get instant traffic, with no buying from Google or Facebook and no PR or SEO: your own distribution channel, no new marketing cost, sell to it repeatedly. Mark Joyner's advice to him: focus on building a list. Mike Filsaime's rule of thumb: about $1 per name per month, with a good relationship, often more. Brunson's list: 200 subscribers made about $200 a month, 1,000 made $1,000+, 5,000 made $5,000+; within a year, 100,000; within five years, more than a million. "The bigger your list, the more money you make." The best way to give yourself a raise every day is to spend time every day finding new ways to build your list. Convert controlled and earned traffic into owned traffic as fast as you can.
- **Traffic you control**: you can tell it where to go, but someone else owns it (buy a Google ad, and Google owns the traffic). Paid sources: email ads (solo ads, banners, links, mentions), pay-per-click (Facebook, Google, Yahoo), banner ads, native ads, affiliates and joint ventures. More of it always costs more money, so send all purchased traffic to a squeeze page (it squeezes out the email address) with one goal and no distractions: give your email or leave. His Marketing Secrets Blackbook squeeze: headline "99 Marketing Secrets That Will Change Your Business... And Change Your Life!", an image of the book, "Free Instant Access!", an email field, Get Access Now.
- **Traffic you earn**: you don't control it (a podcast interview, a blog mention or a social post sends people to search your name and land on some page). Sources: social media, SEO, online PR, word of mouth. Guide it to a **funnel hub**: a site that looks more like a traditional website and holds all your funnels, where the only real thing to do is enter a funnel and give an email. Layout: logo and menu; headline, photo and a START HERE button; an About box; products and services as Funnel #1, #2, #3; social follow, watch, connect. His hub's "Start Your Journey": Step 1 read the books, Step 2 start the challenge, Step 3 use the software.
