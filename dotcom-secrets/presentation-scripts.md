# Presentation scripts: the Perfect Webinar and the Product Launch script

## The Perfect Webinar

Brunson's favorite script, used for live and automated webinars, presentations from stage, video sales letters, product launch videos and email sequences. (Jaime Cross's "Five-Minute Perfect Webinar" adapts it to short videos and ads for physical products.)

Three sections. For 90 minutes: **Introduction** 15 minutes, **Content** 45 minutes, **Stack and Close** 30 minutes. Compress it to 60 minutes or 5 and the ratios stay the same.

### Introduction: hook them fast

1. **Big promise.** The One Thing that got them to register, stated up front. It becomes "the ruler they will use to judge your webinar's quality"; if you don't set it, they'll measure you by something outside your control. Ask yourself: what is the big domino you need to knock over for them in the next 60 minutes? ("In the next 60 minutes, you are going to learn my exact strategy on how to ___ without ___.")
2. **Hook to the end.** A reason to stay to the end: a free giveaway, something funny or cool (a secret link to download the transcript, live attendees only).
3. **Command attention.** Close Facebook, turn off phones, give undivided attention, grab a pen and paper: make a commitment to stay focused; can you do that for yourself?
4. **Qualify yourself.** Why you are qualified to teach this ("Here's my story: ___").
5. **Future pace.** Imagination exercises, descriptive, using all five senses. "Imagine what your life will be like after you know how to ___. Can you see ___? Would that make things better for you?"

### Content: break and rebuild their beliefs

About 45 minutes, delivering on the big promise. The goal isn't to teach everything; it's to break the false beliefs that keep them from letting you help. Teach the **what**, never the **how**: the what is your unique system, process or framework (the **vehicle**; his is funnels), and the product in the Stack gives the how (the step-by-step tactics).

**The One Thing.** The whole presentation revolves around one thing they must believe ("Funnels are the number one way to growing their company"). If they believe it, the big domino falls and "they'll have to give me money." Open the content with your origin story: why you came to believe this vehicle is the secret. In High-Ticket Secrets, the headline is the One Thing is the big domino: how to add high-ticket sales to your funnel without personally talking to anyone on the phone, ever (other experts taught phone closing; he hates the phone, and so does his audience). After the origin story, many are ready to buy.

**The three secrets.** What holds them back is false beliefs about three things: **the vehicle**, their **internal beliefs** (about themselves), and **external beliefs** (things outside their control). Give one secret to break and rebuild each. High-Ticket Secrets:
- Secret #1, vehicle. Break: the best model for making money online is selling information or e-commerce products. Rebuild: you can make more in one day selling high-ticket than in a month selling normal products.
- Secret #2, internal. Break: to sell high-ticket, I have to sell on the phone. Rebuild: you don't have to sell anything personally; build a two-person mini call center to close for you.
- Secret #3, external. Break: it must cost a fortune in traffic. Rebuild: you need only a little traffic, about 100 clicks a day.

Break each belief with a story: I used to believe what you believe, then some experience showed me a different, better way. They have the same epiphany you did.

### Stack and Close

1. **"Let me ask you a question..."** The transition is the hardest part, and nervous hesitation shows. Say "Let me ask you a question," then "Is it okay with you if I spend 10 minutes going over a very special offer I created to help you implement ___?" Be confident: "it's your moral obligation to show them you have something that can help them." Show how the product helps with the One Thing, then go into the Stack.
2. **The Stack.** Learned from Armand Morin, who closed nearly half the room with it; it took Brunson from closing 5 to 10 percent of a room to 30 to 50 percent or more, and he never presents without it. The principle: "the only thing your prospect remembers when you sell is the last thing you showed them." The typical presentation shows the core offer, then each bonus on its own slide; the prospect ends up weighing the last bonus against the price of the whole offer, and doesn't buy. Instead, build a Stack slide listing everything. Introduce the first item and talk about it. The second slide restacks ("So what that means is that you're first going to get ___ and also ___."). The third stacks all three, first to last ("...AND also this ___."). Keep restacking through the close, and end on the final Stack slide with everything, recapping each item, before you introduce the price, so the price attaches to the full offer. His One Funnel Away PLATINUM Edition: 30 Days of Coaching ($1,997), OFA Challenge Kit + *30 Days* book ($197), DotCom Secrets LIVE ($2,997), Expert Secrets LIVE ($2,997), Traffic Secrets LIVE ($2,997); total value $11,185.
3. **Total value.** Show it and anchor that number, not the price they'll pay.
4. **If all.** Get them to tell you it's worth it, which anchors and justifies the value and points them toward pleasure and away from pain. "Let's say we did charge you the full $11,185. Would it be worth it?" Toward pleasure: "If all this did was give you the house of your dreams, would it be worth it?" Away from pain: "If all this did was let you fire your boss, would it be worth it?" Get a yes three to five times before the price drop.
5. **Reveal the real price**, far below the stacked value, in steps: not the $11,185 it's worth, not the $4,997 retail, not even the $1,997 most of you were assuming; because you're here today, just ___.
6. **Call to action**: click the link, call the number, or run to the back of the room.

## The Product Launch script

The Perfect Webinar broken into four videos (the **sideways sales letter**): Video #1 carries the Introduction and Secret #1, Video #2 Secret #2, Video #3 Secret #3, and Video #4 the Stack and Close. Each video has four parts.

### Video #1, Wow and How

The first video after registering: hook them and excite them about what's possible, or it may be the only one they watch.
1. **Introduce the new opportunity.** The best offers are **new opportunities** (something completely different), not **improvement offers** (a better version of what they have). The whole series is about a new vehicle to the result they want most, after they've tried a dozen other ways. State it at the start; each video strengthens its position.
2. **Kill false beliefs (origin story).** You had false beliefs before you believed, and they'll have the same ones. Tell the origin story of why you believe this is the best vehicle; it builds rapport and belief.
3. **Let's look at others.** Case studies: not only you, but others you've helped get similar results. The content teaches the what (strategies, frameworks); the product is the how.
4. **Hook to video #2.** Like every TV show: ask them to post questions and comments, and you'll answer some in the next video.

### Video #2, Transformational Education

The goal: they picture themselves doing it.
1. **Over the shoulder.** They watch you do it (his: building in ClickFunnels), which fades the false belief "this vehicle may work for other people, but it won't work for me." A demo, or proof of results.
2. **Your framework.** Walk through your system, giving the what for each step, not the how. "Here's exactly what I do every time: Step 1... Step 2... Step 3..."
3. **Framework proof.** Take one person from the Video #1 results and show their journey through each step.
4. **Hook to video #3.** Ask for comments and questions.

### Video #3, External Forces

By now they believe the vehicle is right (Video #1) and that they can do it (Video #2). Now break the external false beliefs: address the outside roadblocks and show how to get past them.
1. **Future pace.** Imagine what would be different in your life if ___. What possibilities would open up if ___? How would you be different if ___?
2. **What roadblocks?** Name the external roadblocks and false beliefs (my spouse eats the food that would wreck my diet; the funnel would work but I have no idea how to get traffic), and tell stories of others who had them and what helped them through.
3. **Hinting at the offer ("Shhh...").** The offer fills in the blanks: they've learned the what, and the product gives them the missing how.
4. **Hook to the offer video.** They'll soon learn how to get their hands on it.

### Video #4, The Offer

1. **Here's what I've got.** Show the new opportunity again, recap the what, and introduce the offer as the how. Structure it like the Stack, each element added with the total value shown at each step, speaking to the emotional side: the more amazing, the better.
2. **Here's what you'll do with it.** Help them picture owning it, often by walking through the buying process. Here's the order form; after you enter your information you'll land on this page; once you have access, here's the first thing I'd do.
3. **Here's what it'll do for you.** Re-explain the benefits and every hidden benefit, appealing to the logical side: as many logical problems as it solves.
4. **Here's what you need to do right now.** The call to action, exactly what to do and how, with urgency and scarcity: how long it's available and what happens after (the price goes up, or sales stop for a while). This is the last shot; be bold.
