# Building, launching and fixing a funnel

Brunson's consulting order, which is the order of the book: the hook, story and offer; the Attractive Character; the value ladder; which funnel each step needs; then **one** funnel to execute. Then you move from strategist to technician and build it: mastering the strategy *and* doing the work is one of the most profitable skills there is (Noah Lenz, 11 years old, went from charging $10,000 to $25,000 per funnel to building funnels only for equity).

## The build process

Brunson built ClickFunnels because hand-coding each funnel took his team about three months (more than 150 funnels for his own company), and the entrepreneurs he coached almost never got a single page live. The steps below are the book's process from choosing a funnel to launching it; the interface details are ClickFunnels', and its interface changes over time.

### 1. Choose your funnel

Find the step of the value ladder your price fits (the tier table in SKILL.md), then choose the funnel within that step that best fits your audience and offer. The tiers flex (a $1,000 offer can be unboxed; a $100 product can sit on a lead funnel's thank-you or special-offer page). You never know which funnel will work best, so make your strongest guess, build it with the book's strategies, launch, and if it fails, call audibles and keep tweaking until it wins. Building used to cost three to six months per attempt; now a failed attempt costs a week or two, so try another funnel type and repeat until you find the winner.

### 2. Sketch your funnel

Sketch each page and step before building, modeling the book's frameworks but adding or removing pages to fit your offer. Ask:
- What pages will I need?
- Will I use an order form bump?
- How many upsells? Do I want a downsell?
- What is the hook, the story and the offer on each page?
- What is the price of each offer?

His sketch format: Landing Page ("Headline," email, submit) → Offer (video, Add to Cart) → Upsell (OTO, video, Add to Cart), with "Hook? Story? Offer?" blanks under each page, filled in before building.

### 3. Build it

Create the funnel (in ClickFunnels, Add New opens the Funnel Wizard with three paths: **Collect Emails** builds a lead squeeze funnel, **Sell Your Product** builds unboxing funnels, **Host Webinar** builds a live or automated webinar funnel, or Create a Custom Funnel). Check that the funnel's steps match your sketch, and add or delete steps until they do (a squeeze funnel is two pages: opt-in and thank-you). Pick a template for each page; choose one close to what you want and customize it to your brand. The book's 10 core funnels exist as ready-made "share funnel" templates.

Pages are built from **sections, rows and elements** (color-coded green, blue and orange in the editor):
- **Sections**: the containers for the header, body areas and footer, each with its own background.
- **Rows**: inside sections, each with one or more columns; this structure keeps pages responsive and mobile friendly.
- **Elements**: the content inside rows: headlines, text, images, videos (paste a YouTube, Vimeo or Wistia link), order forms and bumps, buttons, countdown timers, progress bars, opt-in forms, surveys and more (over 40 kinds).
Edit text by clicking it, drag elements around, and change sizes, colors and backgrounds; preview and tune the mobile view.

### 4. Plug in your hooks, stories and offers

The real power isn't in the pages; it's the hooks that get attention and the stories that build connection and raise the value of the offer on each page. Good design and branding matter, but the hook, story and offer make the money, and when a page doesn't convert, they're what you change.

### 5. Build the follow-up funnels

Map the follow-up first: who the Attractive Character is, how the emails flesh them out, what each Soap Opera Sequence email says, what kinds of Seinfeld emails you'll send. His sketch: landing page → video page → thank-you page, with the opt-in dropping into a follow-up sequence of six emails grouped Emotion → Logic → Fear. Create one email per day of the sequence with its delay, design each (the email editor also uses sections, rows and elements; edit the pre-header for more opens; add images for higher click-through), then connect the funnel's opt-in to the sequence so every new email address starts receiving it. Track sent, opens and clicks.

### 6. Launch

Drive traffic and track the stats. What took his team two to three months now takes about an hour.

## Funnel audibles

### Expect the first launch to flop

Brunson lost his first match of the season to the returning state runner-up; his father rewatched the tape all night, found the holes, and drilled the adjustments with him every day for four months, and he beat the same opponent in the state finals. Most funnels fail the first time ("Mine all do"). The launch is the first match: it exists to get the numbers that show where the funnel is failing, and you can't know what to fix until traffic goes in. The sooner you see where it's failing, the faster you fix it. Watch each step: How many clicked the ad? What did each click cost? How many joined the list? What percentage bought? Did anyone take the upsell?

### One "properly exploited" funnel

Gary Halbert: "Properly exploited, one good idea that occurs to you while walking on a beach is worth more than ten lifetimes of hard work." Brunson's version: one good funnel, properly exploited, is worth more than ten lifetimes of hard work. Take it from loser to okay to winner, because "you're just one funnel away."

**Keep it simple.** "Complexity is not your friend in the funnel game." A 50-page funnel map with dozens of if/then paths and countless triggered sequences might add a fraction of a percent, maybe; when it underconverts, there are thousands of things that could be broken and no place to start (it's usually a consultant making themselves look valuable). His funnels are three to five pages with one good Soap Opera Sequence, for two reasons:
1. **Speed to market.** Entrepreneurs fail because they run out of time or money before their idea reaches the market, not because the idea is bad. A simple funnel takes a few hours or a weekend. Joe Vitale: "money follows speed."
2. **Fast diagnosis.** With a simple funnel there are only a couple of levers to move, so you need less testing to reach profitability.

### The test

1. Build the best funnel you can and get it out fast.
2. **Test budget**: spend on traffic the amount you'd make if one person bought everything in the funnel. A webinar selling a $1,000 course: $1,000 of ads. A book funnel with two upsells: $0 book + $37 bump + $97 OTO + $197 OTO = $331. The goal is to break even (a **breakeven funnel**); if you don't, write the loss off as market research.
3. Buy the traffic (Facebook, Google or whatever source fits) and wait a few days to a week while it joins the list, goes through the Soap Opera Sequence and sees every page.
4. **Let the market decide.** He refuses to tell people whether their funnel will work, because he isn't their market. The question is: will my dream customers pull out their wallets and vote with their credit cards for this offer? Don't trust your own opinion either; split-testing shows you're wrong most of the time. "The market never lies."
5. Read the numbers, make the changes they point to, spend one sale's worth again, wait, and repeat. It usually takes three or four rounds to get a winner.

### Marketing math

Two numbers drive everything. It's simple arbitrage.
- **Cost Per Acquisition (CPA)**: ad spend ÷ customers. $1,000 of Facebook ads selling 100 books is a $10 CPA; the same spend for 10 customers is $100. (The *Shark Tank* question: how much does it cost you to acquire a customer?) Check the CPA of every ad.
- **Average Cart Value (ACV)**: total funnel sales ÷ customers. 100 book buyers producing $2,500 across the book, bump and upsells is a $25 ACV.

**If CPA > ACV, your funnel is broken. If CPA < ACV, your funnel is working**, and you spend as much as you can, because every ad dollar brings back more than a dollar. Every ad has its own CPA: turn off the ones that run too high and keep the low ones running as long as they stay profitable.

**The evaluation sheet.** After the test, fill it in:
1. The actual CPA (spend ÷ customers), written over the ad.
2. For each page: the offer price, its conversion rate, and price × conversion rate, which is that page's contribution to ACV.
3. The sum of the contributions: the ACV.
4. Your Score: ACV − CPA = profit per customer.

His worked sheet (CPA $20):

| Page | Book | Bump | OTO #1 | OTO #2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Offer price | $7.95 | $37.00 | $97.00 | $197.00 |
| Conversion rate | 5% | 13.6% | 10% | 5% |
| $ added to ACV | $.40 | $5.05 | $9.70 | $9.85 |

ACV $25.00 − CPA $20.00 = profit $5.00.

### Reading the result

- **Lost money**: the market says the funnel isn't ready; find the underperforming step.
- **Broke even**: the funnel generates leads for free, a huge win; now test toward profit.
- **Making money**: "give myself a raise every day." Each day, look at each active funnel's **control** (the page currently winning) and brainstorm ways to beat it, like a video game where you keep the money. A page converting at 3 percent and making $1,000 a week, lifted to 4 percent, adds about $17,362 a year from one test.

Case: Mike Schmidt and AJ Rivera's webinar was costing $24.85 per registration with a 22.4 percent show-up rate, and they thought they needed a new funnel type. Their headline gave away the topic ("...My Secret Method For Helping Companies Get More (Real) 5-Star Reviews..."), and if you know the answer, you don't register, or you don't show up. The fix was to stop saying what the webinar teaches, say what it *isn't* about, and create curiosity. They changed only the headline ("How I Added 6 Figures to My Web Company by Adding This One Easy to Offer Service THAT DOESN'T Require Cold Calling..." with a long "and NO IT'S NOT Facebook ads, SEO, Email Marketing..." list, and a button reading "REGISTER NOW AND FIND OUT WHAT IT IS!"). Cost per registration fell to $5.84, and show-up rose to 31.7 percent.

### ACV audibles: benchmarks

Find the pages that underconvert, then build new pages to beat them. Conversion rates vary by market and traffic source, so set your own baseline and beat it; below these benchmarks, call an audible on that step, and above them, keep testing anyway, because small changes yield big money over time.

| Funnel | Metric | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| Front-end lead | lead funnel opt-ins | > 20% |
| Unboxing | two-step order form opt-ins | > 10–15% |
| | book or cart funnel sales conversion | > 1–5% |
| | order form bump conversion | > 20% |
| | OTO/downsell conversions | > 3–15% |
| Presentation | video sales letter sales conversions | > 1–3% |
| | webinar registration rate | > 20% |
| | webinar show-up rate, live | > 10–20% |
| | webinar show-up rate, automated | > 50–80% |
| | webinar buy rate, hot traffic live | > 10% |
| | webinar buy rate, cold traffic live | > 1–5% |
| | webinar buy rate, automated | > 3–8% |
| | product launch funnel registration rate | > 20% |
| Back-end phone | cost per application | < $100 |

### CPA audibles: ads

Sometimes the funnel performs and the cost per customer is still too high. That can be the landing page, but more often it's the ads. Run many ads with different hooks, each with its own CPA. One of the biggest secrets of online marketing is that you need a lot of ad creative. Dean Graziosi was outselling Brunson's book four to one: his TV infomercials used to last about 18 months before fatigue drove the CPA up, but online ads fatigued in a few weeks at best, so he filmed new ads on his phone, holding his book, wherever he was each day. His team turned off the losers (CPA above ACV) after a day or so and ran the winners for weeks until they fatigued (CPA rising toward ACV), then replaced them. He made four times as many ads. Cut losers fast and scale winners as long as you can; the page-level changes below apply to ads too.

### What should I change? Hook, story, offer

"If your ad doesn't work, it's always because of the hook, the story, or the offer," and the same goes for the landing page, the emails, the sales pages and the upsells. "The hook grabs their attention for the story, and the story increases the perceived value of your offer." Brunson's method with his funnel team:
1. Pull up every active funnel and the stats for each page.
2. List the pages below the benchmarks.
3. For each page, ask: "What's the hook on this page? What could we do to make it better?" Then: "What's the story we're telling on this page, and does it build the perceived value of our offer?" Then: what could we do to make the offer sexier? Brainstorm a dozen tests for each.
4. Split-test new pages against the controls, page by page. (In his first round, every new page won.)

**Split testing.** Create a variation of a page, either from scratch or by cloning the control and changing one thing at a time (a headline or a sales video is easiest; his test pitted "FREE Book!" against "Grow Your Company Online!" over the same page). Set the traffic split (50/50), run it for a few days or weeks, and compare. The winner becomes the new control, and you start again the next day to give yourself another raise.

If you paid him $100,000 for a day of consulting, his answer would always be the same: "It's your hook, your story, or your offer," and then you'd brainstorm ways to make them better.
