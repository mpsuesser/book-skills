# Evolution, memes and culture

Deutsch's account of how knowledge is created in the biosphere and in culture, what keeps societies static, and how creativity evolved. The *who created the knowledge?* test is in `SKILL.md`.

## Evolution and the appearance of design

- ***Evolution*** (Darwinian): "creation of knowledge through alternating variation and selection." In humans, variation is by conjecture and selection by criticism and experiment; in the biosphere, by mutation and natural selection. Biological knowledge is non-explanatory, so its reach is limited. Mutations are random; conjectures are intentional. Each misconception about evolution has a counterpart about human knowledge.
- ***Adapted***: "few, if any, small changes would improve its ability to perform that function". "Good adaptations, like good explanations, are distinguished by being hard to vary while still fulfilling their functions."
- **The argument from design.**
  - Socrates (reported by Xenophon) and Paley (1802, the watch found on a heath) were right that the *appearance of design* needs explanation: it "signals the presence of knowledge".
  - "Evidence of apparent design for a purpose is not only that the parts all serve that purpose, but that if they were slightly altered they would serve it less well, or not at all. A good design is *hard to vary*." The sun keeps time but is not adapted to it; the knowledge is in us and our sundials. "Merely being useful for a purpose, without being hard to vary while still serving that purpose, is not a sign of adaptation or design."
  - Paley's mistake was "There cannot be design without a designer". We now know "there *can* be 'design without a designer': knowledge without a person who created it." His argument, with "ultimate designer" substituted for "watch", rules out an ultimate designer: "none of us can choose what our ideas imply." Paley still sets "a benchmark that explanations of knowledge-laden entities must meet".
- **The three non-explanations of improvement.** "*Any* theory about improvement raises the question: how is the knowledge of how to make that improvement created? Was it already present at the outset? The theory that it was is creationism. Did it 'just happen'? The theory that it did is spontaneous generation." The third is Lamarckism.
  - ***Creationism*** is "the gods did it", no better than "the conjurer did it". It faces a dilemma. A designer who is "just there" with the knowledge explains nothing, since the biosphere could equally have "just happened". A designer whose own origin is explained is no longer supernatural. Suboptimal designs count against it: the vertebrate retina is wired in front, causing a blind spot the squid lacks, and humans carry a vestigial, broken vitamin C gene. "Undiscovered purpose" is a bad explanation, because it could make any design perfect. "Creationism is really *creation denial*".
  - ***Spontaneous generation***: mice from rags, disproved by Pasteur in 1859. The "experiment should never have been needed". Ask "how does the knowledge to construct a mouse get to those rags".
  - ***Lamarckism***: improvements acquired in a lifetime are inherited (use and disuse, giraffes stretching their necks). But muscle-strengthening is itself knowledge-laden genes at work, and nothing "knows" what a tiger's stripes are for. "The fundamental error being made by Lamarck has the same logic as inductivism. Both assume that new knowledge… is somehow already present in experience, or can be derived mechanically from experience. But the truth is always that knowledge must be *first* conjectured and *then* tested."
  - All bad explanations are interconnected and interchangeable: "too easy" as explanations. They all "underrate *creation*". "Before a discovery is made, no predictive process could reveal the content or the consequences of that discovery. For if it could, it would be that discovery."
- ***Neo-Darwinism***: "evolution favours the genes that spread best through the population". Not the good of the species, nor of the individual, nor even of the genes' welfare, but "only for spreading through the population at the expense of rival genes, particularly slight variants of themselves."
  - *The nesting-date thought experiment.* Suppose April is optimal for nesting. A mutant that nests in March takes the best sites, so its gene spreads and drives nesting ever earlier while the population falls. The equilibrium is far from optimal.
  - So "survival of the fittest" is wrong. "Not all evolution constitutes progress, and no (genetic) evolution optimizes progress." Peacock tails are another case.
- ***Replicator***: "an entity that contributes causally to its own copying." "A population of replicators subject to variation (for instance by imperfect copying) will be taken over by those variants that are better than their rivals at causing themselves to be replicated." "The real replicator is abstract: it is the knowledge itself."
- **Explanations replicate differently.** Rules of thumb evolve like genes: a rhyme is remembered better than accurate prose. But "good explanations are hard to vary even without being tested, and hence random errors in the transmission of a good explanation are easier for the receiver to detect and correct. The most important source of variation in explanatory theories is creativity."
- **What would refute Darwinism.** The reply often attributed to Haldane, that "a single fossilized rabbit in a stratum of Cambrian rock" would refute the theory, is misleading: it would refute the prevailing history of life, not evolution. What would refute it is "evidence which, in the light of the best available explanation, implies that knowledge came into existence in a different way". Examples: a run of favourable mutations, complex adaptations without precursors, or an adaptation that was never selected for in the ancestry, such as an animal consulting internet weather forecasts to decide when to hibernate.

## Culture and memes

- ***Culture***: "a set of shared ideas that cause their holders to behave alike in some ways." Ideas are any information storable in brains that can affect behaviour, and many are inexplicit: word meanings, riding a bicycle, freedom. Inexplicit ideas cannot be downloaded, and no two people hold exactly the same one. Hence the schisms after a founder dies.
- ***Meme***: "an idea that is a replicator". A ***memeplex*** is "a group of memes that help to cause each other's replication", such as quantum theory or a religion. Most ideas are short-lived. A long-lived meme is an exceptional idea accurately replicated many times in succession. "2+3=5" is not a meme, since it is copied only with a theory of arithmetic.
- **The two questions** about any long-lived meme: what gives it the ability to resist change through many replications, and under what conditions do memes change for the better?
- **Holders do not know why they act.** We are often "mistaken about the reason for our own behaviour". A joke stays funny when re-heard, so its humour is not mere surprise. Grammar is the clearest case: the British say "in hospital" and "play the piano", Americans "in the hospital" and "play piano", and few can say why. Memes implicitly contain knowledge their holders lack, and people give false explanations even to themselves.
- **Two forms, two selections.** Genes are copied indiscriminately, even when unexpressed. Each meme "has to be expressed as behaviour every time it is replicated", because a recipient cannot see the brain representation: "If it is not enacted, it will not be copied." A meme alternates between a brain form and a behaviour form, and must survive two potentially unrelated selections each generation. The brain form must cause its holder to enact it, and the behaviour must cause the recipient to remember *and* enact it. Intention to transmit is not enough: most new religions fail. "Children are gullible" is no explanation, or every adult would be a proficient algebra teacher.
- **The selfish meme.** A meme competes for expression with other ideas in the same mind, for recipients' attention, and against rival versions of itself. The successful variant is whichever changes its holders' behaviour so as to best displace the others, whether or not it benefits them or their society.
- **Most evolution has happened in brains.** A brain runs thousands of cycles of variation and selection before enacting an idea, and memes pass to non-descendants, so meme evolution is enormously faster than gene evolution. "A substantial proportion of all evolution on our planet to date has occurred in human brains." "The whole of biological evolution was but a preface to the main story of evolution, the evolution of memes."
- **Argument by analogy.** "Arguments by analogy are fallacies." An analogy contains a grain of truth only where there is an independent explanation of what is analogous to what, and why. The biosphere–culture analogy obliterates the distinctions between mindless and creative, determinism and choice, right and wrong, and is often drawn to debunk humans as causal agents. Marx's "evolutionary" history and Nazi "survival of the fittest" misused it. Competition in evolution is between variants of genes within a species, and it can produce cooperation, as between flowers and insects.

## Static societies

***Static society***: one that changes "on a timescale longer than its members can notice". People die under the same values, technology and economy they were born into. Static societies are dominated by anti-rational memes.

- **The puzzle.** Merely assume that people try to improve things and that transmission is imperfect. Then a small improvement, spreading through all minds, becomes an exponentially growing revolutionary force. "But in a static society that beginning of infinity never happens."
- **How stasis is kept.** Taboos enforce enactment, forbid variants and suppress criticism, but they cannot suffice:
  1. No enactment is identical, and it is infeasible to specify all behaviour.
  2. No one can tell which deviations will start a change.
  3. Once a variant spreads to one more person, it is hard to stop.

  So enforcement is a secondary "mopping-up operation". The primary method is "always – and can only be – to disable the source of new ideas, namely human creativity": traditions of child-rearing that disable creativity and the critical faculties. People come to value conformity, and to take pride in subordinating themselves to their society's memes through obedience, piety and duty.
- **Memes know, though no one does.** "Just as genes for the eye implicitly 'know' the laws of optics, so the long-lived memes of a static society implicitly possess knowledge of the human condition, and use it mercilessly to evade the defences and exploit the weaknesses of the human minds that they enslave."
- **Finely tuned compulsion.** There is also pressure for minimal damage, so a meme becomes a narrow inability to refrain from enacting it. Religions instil fear of specific entities, not general fearfulness or gullibility. The damage is confined but deeply entrenched, at a large emotional cost of deviating.
- **Stasis is a struggle.** A static society "consists of… a relentless struggle to prevent knowledge from growing". Its fear of change holds "much truth", since it is constantly endangered by dysfunctional memes. During the Black Death the new ideas included killing Jews and "witches", crowding into churches (which spread fleas) and the Flagellants. "Oppression is what it takes to keep a society static; oppression of a given kind will not last long unless the society is static."
- **Deduce history.** Without historical research one can deduce that every pre-Western society was either static or destroyed within a few generations, as Athens and Florence were. Hunter-gatherer societies must have been static, and their members chronically baulked, since "creating knowledge is itself a natural human need and desire".

## Rational and anti-rational memes

- ***Rational meme***: an idea "that relies on the recipients' critical faculties to cause itself to be replicated." ***Anti-rational meme***: one that relies on disabling them. The two are as different from each other as both are from genes.
- **Dynamic societies.** The West is the only known long-lived ***dynamic society***, one dominated by rational memes, with rapid, peaceful change including consensus about values. When choices vary unpredictably, the best way for a meme to *seem* useful to diverse people is to *be* useful, where useful includes interesting, funny, elegant, memorable and morally right. "The ideas with the best chance of surviving through many generations of change are truths with reach – deep truths." Newton's laws served cathedral builders, bridge builders and gunners. Rational memes *rely* on criticism: they are criticized, but their rivals fare worse.
- **The hobgoblin test.** Ask how a meme about a hobgoblin would survive in each kind of society:
  - *Rationally*: it is criticized to extinction, or becomes an overt fiction that is pleasantly thrilling, or, if the danger is real, a matter of practicalities and optimism.
  - *Anti-rationally*: unpleasant emotions are useful to it. It must exploit genuine vulnerabilities, so its implicit knowledge is true and deep, but its overt content need contain no truth. Non-existence helps it, since a hobgoblin is not constrained by finite attributes that could be combated. It does better still if it undermines the principle of optimism.

  "Just as rational memes evolve towards deep truths, anti-rational memes evolve away from them." Mixing the two strategies does no good. Each is weaker on the other's home ground: dynamic societies for rational memes, static ones for anti-rational memes.
- **The Enlightenment is incomplete.** It began with Galileo and became irreversible with Newton: "Western society would become the beginning of infinity or be destroyed." Elsewhere the old manner persists.
  - Gender-stereotyping memes ("ladylike", boys not crying) prevent ideas from crossing minds.
  - "Because I say so" is "a curious type of meme whose explicit content is true though its holders do not believe it".
  - Clothing and home decoration follow narrow conventions with steep social costs; you would not go shopping in pyjamas. Ask whether careers, relationships, morality, politics and national identity are the same.
  - The coexistence of the two types is unstable, because each impedes the other. Anti-rational memes sometimes evolve towards rationality, as autocratic monarchy did into constitutional monarchy.
  - Anti-rational *subcultures* persist because finely tuned damage lets members function otherwise. Racism and bigotry live almost entirely in criticism-suppressing subcultures, and bigotry harms the bigots.
  - Education is still static-style: rote learning, psychological pressure, faithful transmission of a curriculum. So science is acquired "in an anaemic and instrumental way", and someone can count cells by laser by day and chant to draw supernatural energy by night.
- **Against anti-meme accounts.** Susan Blackmore's *The Meme Machine* downplays creativity and, citing Basalla, denies "the myth of the heroic inventor". That distinction makes sense only in a static society. In a dynamic one, "when evolution takes place largely within an individual mind, it is not meme evolution. It is creativity by a heroic inventor." Denying progress towards anything better is true of static societies, not ours.

**Detecting a meme in yourself.** Respond as to any error: conjecture, criticize, seek good explanations. Like the eye's blind spot, a meme can be detected indirectly, through explanation plus observation. Suspicion escalates:

1. You enact a complex or narrowly defined behaviour that is accurately repeated from holder to holder.
2. Enacting it thwarts your own objectives, or you keep doing it faithfully after its ostensible justifications have disappeared.
3. You explain your own behaviour with bad explanations.

Also suspect the *conditions* for anti-rational evolution: deference to authority, static subcultures, "Because I say so", "It never did me any harm", "Let us suppress criticism of our idea because it is true". Failure to locate a meme need not be permanent, and "locating hang-ups is, in the last analysis, easier."

The Enlightenment is the moment explanatory knowledge becomes the most important determinant of physical events. Sustained knowledge creation has never worked before, so "everything that we shall ever try to achieve from now on will never have worked before."

## How creativity evolved

- **Two puzzles.**
  1. Creativity evolved rapidly from ape-like ancestors, so it had a large selective advantage. Yet before agriculture (about 12,000 years ago) there were thousands of years between noticeable changes. "Their ability to innovate was increasing rapidly, but they were barely innovating." What was it for? Sexual display is unlikely: creativity is sophisticated, evolved only once, and small differences are hard to detect.
  2. How do you replicate a meaning?
- **Imitation is impossible.** Meme replication is often described (as by Blackmore) as imitation, but ideas cannot be observed. Popper opened a lecture by telling students to "observe"; they asked what to observe, since observation needs prior theory. Likewise "imitate Popper": push him off the podium? Face the rear wall? Copy his Austrian accent? Each interpretation is a different theory of the ideas causing the behaviour. "There is no such thing as 'just imitating the behaviour'." Popper also said: "It is impossible to speak in such a way that you cannot be misunderstood." And "people need inexplicit knowledge to understand laws and other explicit statements, not vice versa."
- **Apes ape, parrots parrot.** Their genes know the inexplicit theories in advance. A parrot never chooses what to imitate, resolving ambiguity by making no choices, so it is devoid of creativity and relies on lacking it. A parrot at Popper's lectures transmits like air: "The sound of the meme would be there, but its meaning would not. And it is the meaning – the knowledge – that is the replicator." Apes learning to crack nuts use what Richard Byrne calls ***behaviour parsing***: they split the stream of behaviour into elements they already know how to imitate and link them through statistical patterns over many viewings. It is slow, with few fixed options, and again each action has only one meaning, known in advance. Mirror neurons cannot be the key to the human mind.
- **Humans explain instead.** For humans, meaning is precisely what they strive to discover, and the actions are secondary and forgotten. A student may acquire a lecturer's meme without repeating a single sentence. A restatement makes it easier for a human, harder for a parrot. "Rather than imitating behaviour, a human being tries to explain it – to understand the ideas that caused it – which is a special case of the general human objective of explaining the world." "If we end up behaving like other people, it is because we have rediscovered the same idea."
- **The solution to both puzzles.** "What replicates human memes is creativity; and creativity was used, while it was evolving, to replicate memes". The meme acquirer faces the scientist's logical problem: discovering a hidden explanation from evidence. The imitation theory makes the mistake of empiricism and Lamarckism, assuming the environment can instruct a mind. Popper: "the critical or Darwinian approach only allows instruction from within… We use, rather, the method of trial and the elimination of error. As Ernst Gombrich says, 'making comes before matching'." "Memes, like scientific theories, are not derived from anything. They are created afresh by the recipient. They are conjectural explanations, which are then subjected to criticism and testing before being tentatively adopted."
- **Why there was no innovation.** In a static society noticeable innovation reduces status. The only way to gain status by extra creativity is to "enact that society's memes more faithfully than the norm. To display exceptional conformity and obedience. To refrain exceptionally well from innovation." Commands cannot specify every detail, so one must understand their point. "Paradoxically, it requires creativity to thrive in a static society – creativity that enables one to be less innovative than other people."
- **What it implies.** Creativity is a property of software, and perhaps "the creativity program is not entirely inborn. It is a combination of genes and memes." "We do not know what was being gradually increased in that approach to a universal explainer. If we did, we could program one tomorrow." Creativity is also sufficient: deaf, blind and paralysed people acquire and create ideas fully. "The beginning of creativity was… the beginning of infinity." Static societies were a "hideous practical joke that the universe played on the human species": creativity was prevented from innovating by the very memes it preserved, and the Enlightenment may have made escape "from that eternity into infinity" practical.
