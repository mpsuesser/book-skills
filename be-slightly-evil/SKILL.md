# Be Slightly Evil (Venkatesh Rao)

A playbook for the "dark continent" of adversarial decision-making under competitive conditions, in a world that isn't all win-win and Happy Culture. It deals in the *how*, never the *why*: means-ends justification is the user's business. It gives specific prescriptions, sorted into Boyd's OODA (Observe-Orient-Decide-Act) stages plus moves for disrupting an adversary, and closes with a capstone, *inside the tempo*. Think in Rao's terms, defined below, across the situations he writes for: offices and organizational politics, sales and negotiation, careers and life choices, everyday social rituals, markets and competing companies, politics.

## The stance

- **Slightly Evil** is a do-something, tragic, pragmatic philosophy of **moral minimalism**: every true thing learned shrinks the domain where moral opinions are useful. The universe is not benign; entropy makes it, and us, slightly evil.
- **Amorality heuristic**: "an idea is Slightly Evil only if it is, in principle, equally valuable to both good and evil people." Distrust anything that works *only* if you are good.
- **Tragedian, not idealist**: humans don't change, so you change your environment (the **Unreasonable Man Effect**) and are transformed as a side effect. Tragedians stay neutral between win-win and win-lose framings and pick whichever suits the situation; they seek the *appropriate* time horizon, not the long term.
- **Most situations are only partly adversarial.** In outright conflict "you should prefer a dumb enemy over a smart one". Everywhere else the other party's fully engaged intelligence is worth having. The *Game of Thrones* model of constant backstabbing is as unrealistic as win-win-or-no-deal.
- **The boundary of "slightly"**: "it stops being 'slightly' evil when you start impoverishing your non-adversarial relationships in any way, particularly the one with yourself." *Keep your customers fully human.* Always pick on someone your own size: fair fights are how you grow, and fighting below your weight class shrivels your muscles. Tests: after you "win", did the other person *learn* something valuable at a reasonable price, or were they played for a sucker? Would you want to win *that way* against a spouse, friend, child, pet or parent?
- **No free lunch**: if you can't tell who is paying for a lunch, don't eat it, even if the knowledge is true. "If you don't know who the sucker is, it is probably you."
- **Draw your own lines.** "I am not your priest. Draw your own lines in the sand."

## Working a situation

Run these in order. The work is done when you can name the move, in Rao's terms, with the reading of the situation that justifies it, and the band-aid and strategic parts are both specified.

1. **Reframe from the tactical to the whole situation.** "Isolated tactical problems are very rare." If the question is "how do I respond to this email" or "what do I say in this meeting", it is probably a misframed broader problem headed for "your personal Vietnam". Map the full context: who, their roles and incentives, the manager or judge above them, the default perceptions in play, your own limits. As the challenged party, use the **first rule of dueling**: choose the time, place and manner of combat ("If pistols at dawn work better for you, choose those").
2. **Gauge the field.**
   - How adversarial is it: fully, partly, or not at all?
   - Is the setting *tribal* (the same people meet again and reputations matter, as in a corporate department) or *civilized* (strangers, disinterested adjudicators)? In tribal settings, calibrated vindictive pushback is rational; revenge is never worthwhile.
   - Is there a game in progress, and in which phase: opening, mid-game or end-game?
3. **Observe.** Read the players: what they consume (blatant *archetyping*), their felt-versus-played status pattern, whether they are telling the truth or only *think* they are (candor and cursing versus clarity). Listen cold-bloodedly for the one useful piece of information.
4. **Orient.**
   - Is the interaction **open** (both trading information) or **closed** (someone seeking status, conflict or harmony)? Which of the four attitudes is in play?
   - Which motivations are buried in context: *extrinsic* (role, incentives) or *intrinsic* (ego)?
   - Which delusions must be factored in rather than fought?
   - What status position do you need to hold?
5. **Decide.**
   - Go *through* the obstacle or *around* it?
   - What is your BATNA?
   - Are sunk costs distorting anyone's story?
   - How much of your advantage should you cede, given your moral doubt?
6. **Act and course-correct.** Pair an immediate band-aid with a well-timed strategic intervention, then correct at multiple levels with feedback. Done right, this is **investment rather than spending**: reputation and practiced skills accumulate instead of every interaction starting from square one.

## Core vocabulary

**Status.** *Felt*, *played* and *perceived* status are nearly independent. In **locked-status mode**, what you play is fixed by what you feel. The four felt/played patterns: **LL** (can't take a compliment), **LH** (rude to waiters), **HL** (gracious *noblesse oblige*), **HH** (rarest: emperors, spoilt children, uniformed doormen). "Status matters if it matters": break your locked patterns until felt status decays into a **status vacuum**, and status becomes a control variable you play against those to whom it matters. To move someone, *threaten* their status, *validate* it, or attack then validate ("give people a way to save face"). Pick a credible position at the right distance and sign, and hold it. "Play status or crash."

**The four attitudes.** These combine status with conflict/harmony:

- **Condescension**: better than you and for you.
- **Contempt**: better than you and against you.
- **Supplication**: worse than you and for you.
- **Insolence**: worse than you and against you.

These games waste time: move to an open, information-trading conversation, or disengage.

**Through or around.** Going *through* opposition means open confrontation, or getting a more powerful decision-maker to rule for you. Going *around* means misdirection, distraction, pre-emptive neutralization, sidelining, flattery, stealth, divide-and-conquer, **momentum judo** (accelerating their efforts to failure) and strategic leaks. Go around almost always; it is cheaper and less damaging. The exception is your **first day in prison**: when new, make an example of one well-chosen opponent. Cooperative engagement of real opposition ends in **death by consensus seeking**.

**Conflict without ego.** "You cannot get mad at facts." Foreground extrinsic motivations and quantify them; bracket intrinsic ones and let them go. Conflict still remains, because "the ideas of your friends are not always the friends of your ideas."

**Deception.** By the **principle of conservation of deception**, each level of moral and intellectual development carries a minimum of deception: if you aren't deceiving others, you are deceiving yourself. The **hierarchy of deceptions**, from least to most sophisticated and defensible: outright lying → misdirection → withholding → equivocation → **not-correcting-others**. Give away your advantage in direct proportion to your doubt, turning moral ambiguity into **Russian roulette**.

**Delusions.** The mission: *To Organize the World's Delusions.* The hierarchy runs from getting out of the way, to creating a sandbox, to pouring fuel on the spark (PR), to manufacturing delusions as a last resort. Costanza's law: *it's not a lie if you believe in it.* The aim is the **Yossarian belt**: ironic absurdity, trafficking in delusions without getting attached to them.

**Inside the tempo.** "Being inside an adversary's tempo is all about *wrangling luck to exploit habits.*" Drive **serendipity** towards yourself and **zemblanity** (snowballing misfortune) towards adversaries. Games are precipitated out of **VUCA**. A **habit** is "an efficient pattern of action dissociated from the logic that created it": an OODA loop with *orient* unplugged, leaving **O-DA**. You attack it with **FUD**, artificial VUCA aimed where the adversary feels strong.
