# The Stack and the closes

The last 30 minutes of the Perfect Webinar (slides 16 to 43) move from teaching to selling. Armand Morin closed almost half of a room of about a thousand on a $1,000 course with what he called the Stack; Brunson's first use of it produced his first table rush and first six-figure day, and he has never sold anything without it since.

## Stack psychology

"The only thing prospects remember when you sell is the last thing you show them." The usual pitch shows the core offer, then bonuses, then the price, so people judge the price against the last bonus. In the Stack, you introduce the first element, tell the story of how you created it (learned or earned), and show it on a slide with its value; then the second element, its story, and back to the Stack Slide showing items one and two with a new total; and so on for every element. "The *last* thing you show them before you reveal the price is the full Stack Slide with the entire offer," so the price is weighed against everything. That shift does more to raise sales than almost anything else.

## Slides 16–43

16. **Transition to selling: "Do you think this would work for you?"** Recap the three secrets as one question: if you did Secret #1, then Secret #2, then Secret #3, do you think you could be successful? They connect the dots, say yes, and the Big Domino falls. Live, the people who nod buy and the ones who don't aren't yet convinced; one-to-many, pack the recap with as many of the false beliefs as you can, so it both gauges the room and helps them convince themselves.
17. **The question slide.** The transition is the hardest part. Armand's magic words: "Let me ask you a question . . ." Then: "How many of you are excited about what we just talked about?!" "How many of you are feeling a little overwhelmed because we've covered so much?" Show a fire hose; they laugh; it's impossible to show everything in an hour, so you made a special package for those ready to go. **Ask permission**: "Is it okay with you if I spend 10 minutes going over a very special offer I created to help you implement ___?" Wait for the yes (on video, pause). If the room is silent: "If you don't want to learn this, that's totally okay. I already know all this. I can leave right now." Wait, then: "Who here wants me to spend 10 minutes going over this?" The second time everyone says yes.
18. **What you're gonna get (the core product).** An image of the core product and the story of how and why you created it for them.
19. **"You'll be able to . . ." / "You'll be able to get rid of . . ."** An investment shouldn't cost money, only save it. List what they'll be able to do and what they'll get rid of; when the savings exceed the price, it's irresistible.
20. **The problem this product solved for you.** "When I was first figuring this stuff out, I ran into a big roadblock. I didn't know how to ___. So I had to create ___ for myself."
21. **How much time and money it will save them.** What you spent (a year developing templates, expensive lawyers for contracts) that they won't: I don't want you to reinvent the wheel; I already have proven ___ and I'm giving them to you in this package. Or: save months or years because you'll do it right the first time, with no trial-and-error period.
22. **Break related beliefs about the product.** Quickly break and rebuild false beliefs about the product or their ability to use it.
23. **Stack Slide #1.** The single item: "When you sign up, you're going to get instant access to my product, a total value of $___."
24. **Element #2, the frameworks.** The opportunity-switch or opportunity-stack frameworks (course, workshop, event; for an info product they may be the core product), with the story of why and how you created them.
25. **Quick high-level recap of deliverables.** About 30 seconds. The big mistake is going deep into each module, which overwhelms and confuses: "Week 1, we're going to talk about ___. Week 2... Week 6, we wrap it all up with ___." Then: "Now let me show you some people who've had a chance to go through this process already."
26. **Case studies.** "Let me introduce you to... Then there's... And probably my favorite story is..." Add more over time.
27. **Who this works for (all-inclusive).** After case studies, people think it won't work for them (another country, another industry). Make a blanket statement of who it's for: just starting out or already successful and wanting to scale; a hundred pounds to lose or the last five, or just wanting healthy muscle.
28. **Destroy the #1 reason people don't get started.** Name the elephant in the room and answer it head-on: "You might be thinking you can't get started with this because . . . Here's why that's a mistake that will hold you back from success." (For ClickFunnels, "I don't have a product": sell affiliate products, or learn to make one.)
29. **Stack Slide #2.** Core product on top, frameworks second, new total. "This is where the magic starts happening."
30. **Repeat for every other element.** Introduce it, tell the story of the pain and cost it took you to learn or earn it, show the ease and speed it gives them, and restack (Stack Slide #3, #4...). Each element knocks down a false belief about why they might not succeed (afraid of traffic: a traffic bonus), until the offer removes every remaining one.
31. **The Big Stack Slide.** Every element with its value; the total is ten times the actual price, and if it isn't, add something more valuable. Example: a 12-month ClickFunnels account ($3,564), a six-week masterclass ($1,997), two courses ($997 and $1,997) and copywriting software ($2,997), totaling $11,552.
32. **"If all" statements** (Dave VanHoose): "If all this package did was ___, would it be worth $___?" Transition: "Now obviously, I'm not going to charge you $11,552. But if I DID charge you $11,552, and all it did was ___, would it be worth it to you?" Then one for each secret: if all it did was (the Secret #1 result), would it be worth $___? *Stop* and wait for the yes in their mind. If all it did was (the Secret #2 result)? Stop. And what if all it did was (the Secret #3 result)? Stop. Having said yes three times, the real price is a 90 percent discount from what they've said it's worth.
33. **"I had two choices."** Agreement to charge more to make the program better: I could go as cheap as possible and sell as many as possible, but then I couldn't stack on the value; so I chose a slightly higher investment, so my team can dedicate more time, energy and resources to help guarantee your success.
34. **What would the end result be worth? (price anchor).** "So if you had a successful funnel today that was making you money, what would it be worth to you?" *Stop.* "How much would you pay to have that one successful funnel?" Stop a few seconds. You can see why people pay $___ for a similar result from me: "it's not a cost—it's an INVESTMENT."
35. **Price drop.** You've seen it's worth $___, and even at the $___ I charge the public it's a great deal, but because of ___, I'm making a very special offer . . .
36. **Price reveal.** The first statement of the real price and the first call to action (button, website, phone). Every slide after this carries the call to action.
37. **Price justification.** Never end on the price: what comes after the reveal matters most, while the sticker shock settles. Full price: on my regular website this costs $___, but because you've invested this time and proven you want the result, this is a special offer for this webinar. Apples vs. oranges: hiring a professional to do this would cost $___, but because you're learning to do it yourself with all the tools, you pay only $___.
38. **"You've got two choices."** Do nothing with what you've learned and get nothing; or take a leap of faith and test it to see if it works for you.
39. **Guarantee.** A 30-day money-back guarantee: test-drive it with nothing to lose.
40. **"The real question is this . . ."** Make the choice a no-brainer: is it worth gambling a few minutes of your time to check it out? "Even if it does only HALF of what I've claimed today, it will pay for itself as soon as ___."
41. **Last Stack Slide.** The Big Stack once more, line by line, to cement the offer before the final pitch.
42. **Urgency and scarcity bonus.** "The two most valuable tools in marketing are urgency and scarcity." A bonus or upgrade available only now: to a set number of people, for a set time, or both. "Do not skip this part!" Once they leave, the chance they buy later is almost zero. Offer it only to live attendees, not replay viewers, so people show up live and buy before the end. "The deadline is the key."
43. **Closing call to action / Q&A.** Stays up through the whole Q&A with a recap of the offer, a 30-minute countdown clock, the price and the call to action. Take live questions or pre-write the typical ones, giving the call to action after each, and keep breaking remaining false beliefs: "You're probably thinking ___, right?" Brunson calls that single concept the thing that has made him more money than anything else.

## Trial closes

Ted Thomas, "the Pied Piper of Selling," noticed Brunson nodding through their whole lunch: trial closes, "dozens of little yes-or-no questions where the only answer is yes." Brunson's rooms never rushed the table because "the first time I asked the audience to say yes was when I was asking for their money"; Ted's audiences nod like ocean waves, having said yes hundreds of times before the ask. Adding a few dozen recorded trial closes to an automated webinar raised revenue from $9.45 to $16.50 per registrant.

Examples: Are you ready to get started? Are you all getting this? Is this making sense? Can you imagine if that happened to you? Who here wants a free copy of ___? Would you like to be our next case study? You've heard them talk about this before, right? Isn't that cool? Isn't that exciting? Am I right? Can you see yourself doing ___? I'm sure you've noticed this too, right? The more yeses, the more readily they accept the epiphanies and the offer; trial closes are a large part of telling a story well. They hit hardest right after a case study: pause and drop half a dozen ("Did you hear that? Isn't that amazing? Can you imagine what your life would be like if that happened to you? Are you getting this?!"). Keep them on note cards around the desk.

## The 16 mini closes

Several come from Jason Fladlien. Pick a handful that strengthen the argument and flow naturally; use one right before introducing each new element of the Stack, sometimes two or three between elements.

1. **Money is good.** Detach money from the fear of spending it: money is only a tool for exchange (you can't eat it or stay warm with it), and every exchange happens because what you get is worth more than what you keep. Would you exchange the money for these results? If so, get started now, and if you're afraid, you get your money back.
2. **Disposable income.** What's left after fixed costs is disposable income, usually blown on short-term pleasures (movies, ice cream, travel), and it comes back every two weeks. Invest it in something that helps you; they do have the money.
3. **Money replenishes.** Is it okay to dip into savings or credit? Money replenishes each month; "time does NOT replenish." I've already spent the time for you; the money comes back, the time lost to trial and error away from your family doesn't.
4. **Break old habits.** If they leave without investing, nothing changes: by tomorrow morning they've slipped back into old routines. I could leave, I'm already successful; this is about you, and as your coach I won't let you. Lasting change needs repeated exposure to the full system.
5. **Information alone.** Information isn't enough; people need coaching and accountability. The success rate of people who go it alone is almost zero; of those who work closely with you, far higher. "If information were enough, then you could have just turned to Google. You need a guide who has been there before."
6. **Money or excuses.** There are only two kinds of people, those good at (getting the result) and those good at making excuses; you can't be both, and you get to choose.
7. **Your two choices.** Why you charge what you do: cheap and many leaves no room to pile on value (the bonuses would cost more than the course), so you raised the price a little to give them everything they need to succeed.
8. **Their two choices.** It would be crazy not to: do nothing, or make a tiny investment against the value, with their money back if it doesn't work and "nothing to lose but the stress and headaches."
9. **Us vs. them.** Doers or dabblers. Dabblers sit, listen, learn, rarely act and look for excuses; doers have faith because they've seen it work for others, and doers get ahead.
10. **The handhold.** Walk them through signing up: open a browser, type the address, what they'll see, what to click, the form, the account, where support is.
11. **Say good-bye.** The pain that disappears: say good-bye to the stress of ___; you'll never have to worry about ___ again; what will you do with the extra time, energy and money?
12. **Now and later.** Life before and after: where I was, what I struggled with (sound familiar?), and what I've been able to do since the new opportunity. Can you imagine?
13. **Only excuses.** Call out and defuse the reasons they haven't signed up, answering each with the part of the offer that handles it: the content (the first module shows exactly ___, with templates, so by the end of week one you'll know ___), the setup (week two is step-by-step setup), and the price (if it's too expensive, it's your decision; when I invested $___, I got back ___ within ___).
14. **Reluctant hero.** They can do it: I'm no one special, I have no supernatural gifts, I actually struggle with ___, and that's what I love about this system, I don't have to worry about it anymore.
15. **If you only got.** I could stop right here, and if you only got ___ it would still be worth it; but you're also getting ___ and ___, because I want nothing standing in the way of your success.
16. **Close close.** The final push, repeated through the Q&A: if you're still on the fence, open a new browser window and go to ___; there's no risk with the 100 percent money-back guarantee, and the only way to know if it's right for you is to start now.

## Creating your own closes

"A good close in its more pure form is just an Epiphany Bridge story to break a false belief about why they shouldn't buy." Use the stories from the inventory that the presentation didn't. Brunson's "investing vs. buying" close: as a college wrestler, his coach (an Olympic gold medalist) knocked on his dorm door, handed him a technique tape, and took all the money from his wallet, saying that if he'd been given the tape free he'd never watch it, but because he'd paid, he would; he watched it over and over and became a better wrestler. It breaks the false belief that they "don't have money to buy something that will cost them money": they do have money to invest in something that will make them money. Closes built from your own Epiphany Bridge stories are the most powerful.
