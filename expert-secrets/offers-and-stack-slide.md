# Offers: more money for the same framework

"People will spend more money for the exact same content (or framework) packaged in a different way." A paperback sat unread for six months; the same book as a $97 CD set was finished in a week. A different consumption experience can be worth ten times as much.

## Information products in any company

"While your product may not be a new opportunity, your framework has to be." ClickFunnels alone is an improvement offer (a better way to build a website); the funnel frameworks are the new opportunity and the software is the tool that simplifies them. Wrapping frameworks around a core offer makes complicated sales easy (the information indoctrinates them in why they need the product), positions you as an expert rather than a commodity so price resistance disappears, acquires customers for free through break-even funnels so back-end sales are pure profit, and grows the company faster.

Two kinds of expert business, same tactics, different strategy:

- **Selling information products**: frameworks become books, courses, events, coaching and consulting. No capital needed, only passion and storytelling.
- **Leveraging information as fuel**: a physical product, service or software company sells frameworks to create desire for the core product. In ClickFunnels' first year more than 10,000 people paid $1,000 for a funnel course bundled with a free software trial.

Frameworks become either information or tools.

## Step 1: Increase the value of your frameworks

Change how it is consumed: written word → audio → video → live experience (book, then audiobook upsell, then video home-study course, then workshop). Or change how it is fulfilled: **Do It Yourself** at the bottom of the ladder, then **Do It With You**, then **Do It For You**. The framework stays the same at each level; the experience and delivery change and the value rises.

The eight most popular packages:

| Package | Price | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-funnel content** | Free | A framework's steps as the hook for an ad or social post (a whiteboard list of "How to Set Healthy Boundaries" in five steps); also YouTube, podcast, blog |
| **Lead magnet** | Free | A 2- to 20-minute video teaching one framework, in exchange for an email |
| **Book** | Free plus shipping | Hardest to create and sells for least, but the highest perceived value and positioning |
| **Membership site** | $10–$100 per month | Blends modalities with community; drips frameworks over time; perpetual |
| **Online course** | $100–$1,000 | Finite |
| **Seminar or workshop** | $500–$5,000 | A workshop is a few days teaching your frameworks to a smaller group (about 100 people over three days); a seminar has many speakers on a topic (thousands) |
| **Mastermind** | $10,000–$100,000 | Implementing existing frameworks; members (about 30) each present their business and get help |
| **One-on-one** | $10,000+ | Done for them (services) or with them (coaching); no leverage, time traded for money |

The author, speaker, coach and consultant ladder runs book → home-study course → live event → mastermind, value and price rising together.

## Step 2: Turn your frameworks into tools

Sam Brannan bought gold pans for 20 cents and sold them for $15 to prospectors following his framework: there is gold in the river; you need three tools (picks, shovels, pans); the only place to get them is my store. "How much money you are able to make as a business is in direct relationship to how simple you can make the process for your customers." Look at each step of the framework and ask what tool would simplify it.

- **Software**: the best tool, huge perceived value and cheap to build. Start with small ideas a programmer can build for a few hundred dollars from your sketches; fill-in-the-blank copy software is one example.
- **Supplements**: the ultimate "easy button."
- **Physical products**: physical-product companies start here and build a results framework around the product (a survival flashlight inside a framework for surviving a disaster); experts create products that ease the framework (journals, workbooks).
- **By-products**: things you had to create while using your frameworks: phone scripts, training videos, contracts. "People want tangible assets that make the core training easier to implement. Scripts, templates, cheat sheets, checklists, time lines, and schedules."
- **Information products and services**: every info product is also a tool, and the top tiers of the ladder are services implementing the frameworks.

## Step 3: Turn frameworks into an offer with the Stack Slide

The offer's job is to raise perceived value and make what you sell unique to you, available only inside this special offer, so it can't be commoditized. The simplest way is to wrap the product or service inside your frameworks. The Stack Slide is the first thing to create for any offer and is used in every story-selling script.

**Layout.** On the left, list possible **information** items; on the right, possible **tools**; circle the best and move them to the center slide, "What You're Gonna Get":

1. Core product ($ value)
2. Opportunity switch or stack core framework ($ value)
3. to 6. Further items ($ value each)
- Total value

Item 1 is the core product; item 2 is the framework that makes it a new opportunity, priced by its delivery (the same funnel course was a $9,997 two-day live event, a $1,997 online course, or a $97 smaller course). For a physical product or service, the framework goes in a modality matching the price; for an info product or event, the core product and framework are often the same thing.

**Brainstorm** at a whiteboard with the team.
- Information: what could go in written form, audio, video (membership, course) or live (seminar, workshop, mastermind, one-on-one)? Keep only one or two information items: "Having too much information can decrease the perceived value of an offer because it may seem like too much 'work.'" Often only the core framework belongs.
- Tools carry higher perceived value, so add as many as possible: software, supplements, physical products, by-products that cost you time or money, other products or services to bundle.

**Build each item to break one of the three core false beliefs:**

1. **Vehicle**: "I don't believe this vehicle (your new opportunity) is the right vehicle for me." Ask what tangible thing would convince them; usually case studies and examples (20 to 30 student examples as a booklet or video).
2. **Internal**: "I believe this is the right vehicle for some people, but I don't think that I could be successful with it." Listen to the beta group's "I don't know how to ___" and "I can't ___" and build the training, tool or template that removes it (not technical: how to hire tech people; afraid of copywriting: a copy course with templates and swipe files).
3. **External**: "I believe that I could have success, but I have an external force holding me back from success" (the economy, no time, no traffic). Build something that eliminates or minimizes the excuse (a traffic course).

**10x rule.** Give every item a value; the total should be at least ten times the price ($97 offer, at least $997; $997 offer, at least $9,997). If it isn't, go back to the drawing board. Every step of the value ladder gets its own offer and Stack Slide. Funnel hack other people's launches, in any market, for packaging ideas.
