# The future-based cause

Once Roger Bannister ran a four-minute mile, it was broken again 46 days later and more than 1,400 times since: belief follows proof that something is possible. "Where there is no vision, the people perish." The expert's job is to cast a vision of what is possible and move people to higher ground; every movement's leader paints a picture of the future and of life once the tribe gets there. "Fear of the future causes us to lean against and cling to the present, while faith in the future renders us perceptive to change" (Eric Hoffer). People want to cast their faith into something bigger than themselves.

The cause runs: dream customer → expert (with a slogan) → identity shift → two journeys, the **journey of achievement** (climbed by milestone awards) and the **journey of transformation** (a social mission).

## Step 1: Launch your platform

Campaign as if running for office: be the president of your movement. In US presidential races, the winners offered a vision of the future (a new opportunity) and the losers offered improvement on the present: Reagan's "Let's Make America Great Again" over Carter's "A Tested and Trustworthy Team"; Clinton's "Building a Bridge to the Twenty-First Century" over Dole's "A Better Man for a Better America"; Obama's "Change We Can Believe In" over McCain's "Country First"; "Make America Great Again" over "Stronger Together." Better and stronger are improvement words.

Gary Halbert said, "You are only one sales letter away from striking it rich." Brunson drafted "You're just one funnel away from" being rich, quitting your job, growing your business; each ending reached only some people, so he dropped it: **"You're Just One Funnel Away . . ."** Each listener finishes the sentence for themselves. It closes every video and email and themes events: a constant, consistent callout, backed by stories of failures each saved by a funnel.

Reader examples: a financial firm whose movement is that anyone can be financially free in 10 years or less regardless of age, income or experience (any strategy that doesn't contribute is discarded), slogan "Rise Up. Live Free."; a relationship program built on the claim that it takes only one partner to transform any relationship, slogan "Changing the Way Relationship Is Done®"; a soap maker's "Seed to Skin. Skin to Soul."

Exercise: running for president of your movement, what is your slogan? What do your people really want, and where do they want to go? Capture it in a simple calling that fits on a campaign sign.

## Step 2: Give them an identity shift

After people join, success depends on the identity shift more than almost anything else. Keep selling to them, or they revert or chase someone else's new opportunity: "take them off the market" (Myron Golden).

**Be a drummer.** "I want to learn how to play drums" is not an identity shift; "I am a drummer" is. A drummer with no rhythm who couldn't play the first beat still asked, "What do drummers do? Drummers practice." Once someone takes the identity, they do what that identity does ("that's what Funnel Hackers do"). The leader's job is to design the identity so it's easy to put on.

- **Name the tribe.** Making the movement about yourself makes it hard to join; a company named after its founder is hard to identify with. The company is ClickFunnels; the tribe is Funnel Hackers. When a trainer renamed her weight-loss business from her own name to LadyBoss, churn dropped 10 percent within three months, and customers began calling themselves LadyBosses. "The first step to the identity shift is to create a name for your tribe that will make them want to belong."
- **The "I am" statement.** Let customers self-identify: "I'm a ___" or "I ___" (I'm a Funnel Hacker; I'm a LadyBoss; I build funnels; I see things differently; I'm a biohacker). Put it where they will wear it: a free #funnelhacker T-shirt for every new account cost about $10 to print and ship, the accounting showed no benefit at 10,000 shirts, and members who never logged in kept paying because they loved the shirt and the tribe; more than 250,000 have shipped. Ask what your tribe would wear proudly on their chests.
- **A Title of Liberty, or manifesto.** Captain Moroni tore his coat into a flag naming what his people fought for. A tribe in doubt needs a rallying call that reminds them who you are, who they are, and where you're going together. Write it by asking: Who are my people? What do we stand for? Who are we trying to become? The LadyBoss manifesto (transcribed from the founder talking about what being a LadyBoss means, and hung on walls and mirrors) describes a courageous woman who takes responsibility, isn't a victim, ignores haters, controls her destiny, and ends "YOU'RE A LADYBOSS NOW." The Funnel Hacker manifesto calls them "a new breed of entrepreneur," says business is a calling, scorns venture capital for bootstrapping, and ends "I am a Funnel Hacker and I am just one funnel away!" It calls in the people you want and repels those who don't fit. Offer it as a poster and a phone lock screen.

The name, the shirts and the words matter less than how members feel about the movement and their role in it: they become a new person, connected to you as their guide.

## Step 3: Milestone awards (the journey of achievement)

"A soldier will fight long and hard for a bit of colored ribbon" (Napoleon). The result alone is often too weak a pull: goals are intangible ("grow my company") and growth has no finish line. Milestone awards make the next step tangible, and every winner immediately asks "What's next?" The **Two Comma Club** award for $1 million through one funnel was handed to 73 people onstage the first year; the audience, watching peers break their four-minute mile, transformed. Then came Two Comma Club X ($10 million) and Two Comma Club C ($100 million).

Ask: "What is the four-minute mile for your movement that you could create an award around for your people?" At first it must be something you've already achieved, to show it's possible. As members break it, attention shifts from the expert to the tribe: "people usually come into a community for the expert, but they stay for the community," and every new winner attracts more members.

## Step 4: A social mission (the journey of transformation)

"If the only thing you do with your tribe is help them achieve a result, eventually they will leave you. There is no long-term fulfillment in achievement alone." Achievement is the first journey; transformation is who the hero becomes along the way. Most successful people succeeded because they gave, so give the tribe a cause to give to. ClickFunnels gives $1 per live funnel to build schools in Kenya and backed a child-rescue organization (one premiere raised more than $1 million, one funnel more than $2.5 million), and created the Two Heart Club award for members who donate more than $1 million. Teaching people to be givers is "the most important thing of all" (Tony Robbins). Brunson came to see the achievement mission as the means and transformation as the real goal, and tied the charities and the company together under one mission: "Liberate and Educate."
