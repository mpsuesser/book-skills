# Funnels: the guide's presentation in every funnel

## Your dream customer's two journeys

In the customer's story the brand is not the hero; the customer is, and you are the guide, Yoda to their Luke (Donald Miller, *Building a StoryBrand*). Business means finding congregations of heroes stuck with a challenge in a red ocean, throwing out hooks, and becoming their guide. "The presentation is about telling your story (as a guide), but the funnels are about creating their journey, where they are the hero." Their journey maps onto the funnel:

- **Backstory → advertising.** Hooks thrown into the red oceans where they're frustrated. Your own backstory tells you where they are now.
- **Journey → the free lead magnet page.** The call to leave their ordinary world (Facebook, Google, email) for your funnel, their first real offer: an email for an e-book, video or webinar. "The deliverable matters less than the commitment they make in deciding to start this journey with you."
- **New opportunity → the presentation**, in any funnel type: introduce yourself as their new guide, tell your story to give them an epiphany, present the new opportunity.
- **Framework → achievement and transformation.** As customers they receive the frameworks and ascend the value ladder.

## Testing your presentation live

Brunson's first Funnel Hacks presentation was built Stack first (software, a course not yet created, copy and traffic tools, months free: an offer that felt worth $10,000, priced at $1,000), then the Big Domino, origin story and three secrets. Delivered unpracticed to about 100 low-energy people, it signed up over a third of the room.

**Focus on one funnel until it hits the Two Comma Club.** A marketer who grew a publisher from $11 million to $67 million in a year compared internet marketers to a Broadway producer who opens a show in a small city to a standing ovation and then closes it to write the next one; she takes the show on the road until it stops making money. Online, that means driving more traffic to the same webinar every week. Brunson's coaching-group rule: no second funnel until the first hits the Two Comma Club. "Do your presentation live for a year, or until it hits the Two Comma Club, whichever comes sooner."

**Perfecting it.** One attendee assumed the software was only for supplement companies because every example was a supplement; showing coaching funnels closed three more people, so slides for other industries went in. The first partner webinar drew about 600 live and sold about $30,000; reading the questions and comments exposed four or five confusing spots, and slides answering those questions before they were asked took the next one (about 500 people) to $120,000. He repeated it more than 60 times in 12 months, exporting the questions after each and adjusting. Recording once, untested, means never learning the real objections; even half a dozen live runs reveal the sticking points. "The difference between 10 percent conversion and 15 percent conversion is the difference between a 7 figure-a-year webinar vs. an 8 figure webinar." A perfected presentation lets you predict revenue from attendance, because the close rate holds.

### The live presentation model

Monday to Wednesday promote; Thursday the live webinar; Friday the replay; Saturday and Sunday urgency and scarcity; then start over. Promote from Monday until Thursday (emails, Facebook ads, joint-venture partners) and stop promoting when the webinar starts; the rest of the week is conversion.

- Spend only $3 to $5 per registrant. Above that, the landing page is wrong, the message isn't interesting or the targeting is off; at $7 to $8 it's hard to stay profitable on the front end.
- Weekly stats: 1,000 registrants ($3,000 in ads at $3); 250 attend (25 percent show-up); 25 buy a $997 course on the webinar (10 percent close, $25,000); 25 more buy from the replay follow-ups ($25,000); $47,000 net, plus 1,000 new list members a week, from the same webinar every week.
- Take the offer down at midnight Sunday. To start, test with a smaller group: at least 100 live, so about 300 registered, on a lower ad budget, learning the conversion before scaling. Ads, software and power will fail and people won't show; keep going.

### The live webinar funnel

1. **Send traffic to the registration page.** The key is *curiosity*. A page that doesn't convert is showing too much: people who think they know the answer won't register or show up. Use the "How to ___ without ___" headline and split-test it for more curiosity. Three features of a page that converts:
   - **The picture makes no sense**: a strange, kinda-related image raises conversions; a video on the registration page rarely beats it (test without one).
   - **The headline builds a ton of curiosity**, leaving open questions ("My Weird Niche Funnel That's Currently Making Me $17,947 PER DAY! And How To Ethically Knock It Off In Less Than 10 Minutes!": what niche? is that possible? ethically? 10 minutes?).
   - **Urgency and scarcity** ("Only 200 Seats Available!"; a countdown).
2. **Thank-you page with a self-liquidating offer.** Basic details and a video of you excited: "The registration page is about curiosity. The thank-you page is about your passion and excitement." The **self-liquidating offer** (SLO) liquidates the ad cost so webinar sales are pure profit, makes buyers likelier to show up live, and makes them likelier to buy again: "Buyers in motion tend to stay in motion, unless you do something to offend them." Price it low ($37 to $47) or offer a free or $1 trial (a free software trial on the thank-you page produced more than 15,000 trials in a year).
3. **Indoctrination emails.** Registrants face "about 10 million distractions." Send videos that introduce your philosophy, build excitement and presell: each presells the framework of one of the three secrets by talking about its belief pattern and saying the webinar gives the framework. "Don't answer the questions, just increase the curiosity." The sequence amplifies but isn't essential.
4. **Reminders.** From Wednesday, short emails and texts ("Don't forget we're talking about ___ *live* tomorrow at ___"): a day before, the morning of, about an hour before, about 15 minutes before, and "We're live—join us!"
5. **Present live.** Thursday is Brunson's choice; the day matters less than the indoctrination, the Perfect Webinar and the follow-ups. Set the time by market (entrepreneurs in the day, 9-to-5 workers at night). Expect about 25 percent to show; if fewer, push indoctrination, texts and the last-hour emails. Base close rates on those still on at the pitch, about the 60-minute mark. "5 percent close rate, you have a good webinar and are likely going to be profitable on the front end. When you get it to 10 percent, then (I believe) you have a million-dollar-a-year webinar." At 15 percent, a webinar approaches $10 million in its first year.
6. **Follow-ups and last-minute urgency.** The replay campaign usually *doubles* sales between the end of the webinar and Sunday midnight. Send Friday, Saturday and Sunday emails with the replay link (which disappears when the cart closes); add a PDF cheat sheet or the slides for readers; remind them the offer ends Sunday. "If people think they have all the time in the world to buy, they won't." On the last day send a couple of emails that the cart closes at midnight, recapping the main reasons to buy; many buy a minute before midnight.
7. **Close the cart.** Sunday midnight, buy buttons off.
8. **Repeat.** Monday, back to step 1, better each time; never stop after one try, especially a disappointing one (one presenter had nobody show up to her first webinar, kept refining, and reached the Two Comma Club in her first year). The common mistake is automating too soon: the Funnel Hacks webinar ran live more than 60 times before it was automated, and the automated version is predictable because it was perfected.

"Run it every week for a full year, and watch what happens to your bank account and your expert status."

## Plugging the stories and presentations into the value ladder

The value ladder's funnels, in order: **lead funnels** (Epiphany Bridge stories), **unboxing funnels** (the 5-Minute Perfect Webinar), **presentation funnels** (the Perfect Webinar), **phone funnels**, with follow-up emails built on the Perfect Webinar. In front of them sits the Dream 100, where you either earn attention (a podcast interview) or buy it, using Epiphany Bridge stories, the 5-Minute Perfect Webinar and the Perfect Webinar Shortcut. You hook them in ads, give them the call, present the new opportunity, and guide them to achievement and transformation.

### Epiphany ads

Hooks come from your Epiphany Bridge stories; your frameworks; the red ocean you're throwing rocks into; your new opportunity; the parts of your future-based cause (platform, identity shifts, manifesto, milestone awards); your offers; your presentations. The three most profitable ad types:

1. **Epiphany Bridge story ads**: the story of how you created the thing the ad offers (lead magnet, webinar, book): backstory, journey, new opportunity, framework (what the ad offers), achievement. The call to action always points back to the framework in the story.
2. **5-Minute Perfect Webinar ads**: one for every product, used as both the ad and the landing-page video.
3. **Perfect Webinar Shortcut ads**: for launching a new product; keep buying ads as long as they're profitable.

### Epiphany email follow-up funnels

André Chaperon's **Soap Opera Sequences** end each email with a hook into the next episode. Break the Perfect Webinar into its four core stories and the Stack, one per email: **Email #1 Origin Story Epiphany; #2 New Opportunity Epiphany** (the vehicle); **#3 Internal Belief Epiphany; #4 External Belief Epiphany; #5 The Stack.** Write them or link each to a video: "the way you transmit the story matters less than following the actual story structure." Tease the next email in every one. In a nine-email follow-up the first five emails sell on **emotion**, the next two on **logic**, the last two on **fear**; the five Perfect Webinar emails are the emotional ones (the Stack on email five), and two to four logic and fear emails close the campaign.

### Epiphany lead magnets

Tell the story in the ad and summarize an Epiphany Bridge on the page to show the framework is worth an email, in video or copy: backstory, journey, new opportunity, framework (what they get for becoming a lead), achievement.

### Epiphany unboxing funnels

Lower-priced front-end offers (a free book plus shipping, a $39 lotion bar) don't need 90 minutes: use the 5-Minute Perfect Webinar as the ad and as the sales video.

### Epiphany VSL funnels

Record the Perfect Webinar and put it on the sales page as a video sales letter, either the full 90 minutes or a 30-minute shortcut version; both work. A short one states three secrets and the next step (a trial).

### Epiphany automated webinar funnels

The same full Perfect Webinar, recorded and placed on a page instead of a live webinar platform: register → thank-you page (countdown) → web class → order form. Automate only after it is perfected live.

### Phone funnels

At the top of the ladder: a basic webinar funnel with the Perfect Webinar, where the call to action goes to an application form instead of an order form.

### Epiphany product launch funnels

Jeff Walker's product launch is a "sideways sales letter"; built on these stories it becomes a **sideways Perfect Webinar**: opt-in (free workshop) → **Video 1**, origin story epiphany and new opportunity epiphany (with the Secret #1 framework) → **Video 2**, internal belief epiphany (Secret #2) → **Video 3**, external belief epiphany (Secret #3) → **Video 4**, the Stack and close.
