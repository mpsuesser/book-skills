# Becoming the expert and teaching frameworks

The first part of the movement is the **Expert/Guide**. The Attractive Character (the persona that attracts people to you) becomes the guide who takes them to a result. "We are all in a relationship business, not a product business" (Dan Kennedy).

## The Five Phases to Become an Expert

Two phases of **growth**, then three of **contribution**. "Contribution is the key to continued growth": the only way to keep growing is to shift into helping others. Breaking a move into pieces to teach it makes the teacher better at it.

1. **The Dreamer: it starts with a spark.** Passion is built, not found. Tom Bilyeu's five steps: experiment with a ton of things; notice the ones that spark interest; engage deeply with those; if interest becomes fascination, gain mastery; Fascination + Mastery = Passion. Your **superpower** is what comes naturally to you, the thing so second-nature you dismiss it (cooking, piano, motorcycles, building chicken coops): look at what comes easily and what you love to geek out on. The biggest hurdle is being okay calling yourself an expert; the voice of inadequacy grows louder the more people you help. You have both the ability and the responsibility to serve.
2. **The Reporter.** Your own knowledge isn't enough, so interview the experts a few chapters ahead of you. Read a dozen or more books on the topic to see all sides and form your own opinion. Keep your **teachability index** (how teachable you are at any given time) high; it falls to zero the moment you think you know. "The fastest way to turn your spark into a fire is to get around others who are on fire already." Three ways:
   - **Live events**: hear speakers, notice what resonates, and ask attendees what they struggle with and can't find answers to; that gap is where you design your category.
   - **Your own show or podcast**: a dozen or more interviews is the equivalent of a dozen books, and reveals the gaps and frameworks that become your new opportunity.
   - **A summit funnel**: interview the top experts and build a list at the same time.
   Example: a six-hour interview with an author, run from an outline of the questions his book raised, was sold as recordings, part of the first offer Brunson ever created that sold more than a million dollars.
3. **The Framework Creator.** The end of the growth phase: identify the patterns of success and build your own frameworks (see below).
4. **The Servant: work for free serving your future dream clients.** After, and only after, the framework is proven on yourself, test it on others; most people skip this. Find a beta group. Ask "How can I serve people?" before "How can I sell my product?" Example: with no track record for a $50,000-a-year coaching program, Brunson offered a dream client a free day ("If I am able to make a big impact on your business, then I'll probably charge a lot in the future"), worked with him about a month, built the funnel that grew to tens of millions a year, and the unsolicited testimonial video launched the program with a waiting list.
5. **The Expert/Guide.** Two objections answer themselves. "I'm not certified": "Your results are your certification." "Others know more than me": you only have to be one chapter ahead of the people you're helping.

## Finding your voice: who you need to become as a leader

"The secret to finding your voice is in sharing your message consistently for a long time." Brunson's podcast was recorded on a 10-minute commute; the first 45 or so episodes weren't good, nobody listened, and three years in he found most of his high-end clients had binged it from episode one. Six steps:

1. **Publish daily for at least a year.** Pick the platform that is easy for you (writer: blog; video: vlog; audio: podcast) and decide where, how and when. "If you can publish every day for a year, you'll never have to worry about money problems again." Make great content, then endure long enough to get noticed (Seth Godin only appears on podcasts that have recorded at least 100 episodes); ignore the download stats at first.
2. **Document the journey** (Gary Vaynerchuk's "Document, Don't Create"). Talk about your process ("my intuition says...") rather than prescribing ("you should..."). You publish because you are obsessed with the topic, not because you know everything. Ask: what is the big result you're obsessed with, the thing you're trying to learn anyway, that you can document in real time? State it as **the big question** in your show's intro, a frame inside which anything related fits. A time of failure is the perfect time to start documenting.
3. **Test your material.** Like a comedian who tells ten jokes, keeps the one or two that land and writes eight new ones, keep what connects, gets shared and gets comments, and refine the rest. Brunson "earned" his first book over a decade of testing on his own and clients' businesses and in small seminars.
4. **Learn to be prolific.** Prolific means "someone who has abundant inventiveness": new, unique ideas and frameworks. The **Prolific Index** runs Crazy – Prolific – Mainstream – Prolific – Crazy. "There's no money in the mainstream" (the ideas taught to the masses through traditional channels); the crazy fringe recruits only a few. The money sits in the **Prolific Zone**, unique enough to be noticed but not dismissible (Bulletproof Coffee: a little crazy, never recommended by the government). Polarity attracts attention and people pay for it; "neutrality is boring." Expect about one loud hater per hundred true fans. Exercise: where does your niche sit on the index?
5. **Master persuasion.** Blair Warren's one-sentence persuasion course: "People will do anything for those who encourage their dreams, justify their failures, allay their fears, confirm their suspicions, and help them throw rocks at their enemies."
   - **Encourage their dreams**: learn their dreams and encourage them inside your new opportunity. Strangers who encourage gain the power parents lose by discouraging "for their own good."
   - **Justify their failures**: take the blame off their shoulders and place it on the old opportunities they tried.
   - **Allay their fears**: present evidence, offer support, tell stories and work with them until the fear subsides; telling them not to be afraid does nothing.
   - **Confirm their suspicions**: they are suspicious of you and of the market. Tell the story of how you had the same suspicion and overcame it, so they think "I knew it."
   - **Throw rocks at their enemies**: create "us vs. them." Take a stand for what you believe, why you're different, and who you're collectively fighting. The enemy can be a person, a group, an illness, a setback or a rival philosophy.
6. **Care, a lot.** "Nobody cares how much you know until they know how much you care." Choose clients you would serve for free, then charge them, for two reasons: "those who pay, pay attention" (the more they pay, the closer they listen; free guests at his $50,000 events never launched successful companies), and success shrinks your time, so price is the barrier that protects it and lets you serve more people.

Share your message consistently, find your polarizing point, and be transparent about your backstory and flaws.

## Building your own frameworks

For a service, physical product or software business, ask: "What is the framework that someone must follow to get that result? Your product is likely just a piece of that framework." A dentist's result is straight white teeth; the framework is daily brushing, the right toothpaste, whitening strips, tongue scrapers, enamel supplements and twice-yearly checkups. Owning the framework positions the business differently, makes the process replicable, and sells the other products the framework recommends.

1. **Create your framework hypothesis.** "Research your own experience, absorb what is useful, reject what is useless, and add what is essentially your own" (Bruce Lee). Create your own lingo, a shared language. Procedure: imagine coaching your first client, who is you at the moment you found your spark. Bullet-list everything you would teach that younger self, the more the better, until the list holds everything needed to guarantee the promised result. Organize it into an outline in teaching order, like a table of contents (sections, then chapters). Larger frameworks contain smaller ones.
2. **Test it on yourself.** Be the human guinea pig. Most "experts" hear a good idea and regurgitate it; testing it on your own business finds the holes, and that is how you earn the framework. Stand on the shoulders of giants and always give credit. Update frameworks as the market changes.
3. **Give it a proprietary name**, easy to remember and owned by you (Hook, Story, Offer; the Perfect Webinar; the Value Ladder; the Epiphany Bridge). Add a description: "___ (your framework name): My ___-step framework (or system, process) for ___ (insert result)." Example: "HOOK, STORY, OFFER: My Three-Step System for Grabbing Your Dream Customer's Attention and Getting Them to Pay You What You're Worth!" Name, description and outline make the framework hypothesis.

## The Framework for Teaching Frameworks

"Your framework is your savior": it is the map that puts a presentation back on track after any interruption. The same framework teaches at any depth (two minutes on YouTube, two hours on a webinar, two days at a workshop) and becomes a post, video, podcast episode, lead magnet, book chapter, course, membership site, coaching program or mastermind.

**Introduce it** by name ("This is my Hook, Story, Offer framework") and description ("my ___-step framework for ___"), for example: "This is the five-step framework for creating a webinar that sells any product to a cold audience in less than 90 minutes." Then four steps:

1. **Story: how you learned or earned it.** The big mistake is jumping straight to strategy and tactics: pearls before swine. A story of the pain, money, time, testing and iterations it cost you is the preframe that creates the value of what follows; without it, the strategy and tactics are worthless. **Learned** means from your knowledge and the people you learned from (credit them; it raises respect); **earned** means from your own experience.
2. **Strategy: the what.** The overarching plan, slow to turn like an aircraft carrier. Give the outline, like a table of contents. Example, the Perfect Webinar: grab attention and quickly build rapport; tell your origin story of how you earned or learned the new opportunity; break and rebuild their false belief patterns with the three core stories (vehicle, internal, external); use the Stack to position the offer; finish with closing techniques.
3. **Tactics: the how.** The specific actions; most of the content. Teach step by step as if teaching yourself at the start. Template question: "If I were to suddenly lose ___ (the results I've achieved that others are coming to learn from me), and I only had my framework left and I was starting over with no advantages, what would I do from day 1 to day 30 to get ___ (the big result) back?" The answer is the process from ground zero. Each step can nest its own learned-or-earned story and case study, and each step can be a framework itself: level 1 suits a two-minute video, level 2 a two-hour webinar, level 3 a two-day event.
4. **Social proof: show it working for others.** Case studies prove the framework transfers. Testing on others strips out your unfair advantages (life experience, talents), so adjust the process; and it answers the skeptic who thinks you have special abilities. Gather stories, testimonials and examples from the first minute.
