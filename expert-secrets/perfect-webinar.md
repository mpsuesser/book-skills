# The Perfect Webinar

Brunson's first talk from a stage out-taught every other speaker and nobody bought. He learned the format from people who could sell from platforms (not necessarily good speakers), then spent three years testing which topics, in which order, sent people running to the back of the room. "Teaching the best content hurts sales. But learning how to identify, break, and rebuild false belief patterns gets people to take the action they need in order to change." The result is the perfect sales presentation, nicknamed the Perfect Webinar, used on stages, teleseminars, webinars, VSLs and Facebook Live. One 90-minute keynote with it sold $3.2 million.

Inputs: the Stack Slide (built first) and the story inventory. Build it in slides (Keynote, PowerPoint or Google Slides); for an email sequence or another medium, the slides become the reminder of what to cover. Timing and the three phases are in SKILL.md.

## The Big Domino

"The one Big Domino—the One Thing that, if I could knock *that* down, all the other dominos would either fall down or become irrelevant" (Tim Ferriss). Perry Belcher analyzed ten years of offers: the more things a prospect had to believe, the worse the conversion; a second point of focus cut conversions by half. "Look at how many things a prospect has to believe in order to buy what you're selling. If it's more than one, you need to rework your sales presentation." "Every product has one Big Domino." The presentation is built around "a single point of belief that your message is built around and is emphasized over and over and over again from a variety of different angles" (Jason Fladlien).

The argument is a **modus ponens**: if A, then B; A; therefore B. A belief someone already holds is hard to argue against, because its seed has already knocked down the smaller dominos.

**The Big Domino statement**: "If I can make people believe that (my new opportunity/category) is/are the key to (the result they desire most) and is/are only attainable through (my specific vehicle/frameworks), then all other objections and concerns become irrelevant and they have to give me money." For ClickFunnels: funnels are the key to online business success and are attainable only through ClickFunnels.

If you can't make it a valid argument, you have an improvement offer, not a new opportunity. "cutting calories and exercising is the key to losing weight and is attainable only through my new weight-loss course" fails three ways: it is mainstream rather than in the Prolific Zone, it is no new opportunity (thousands of identical courses), and no blue ocean (they could buy any of a hundred). Fixed: "ketosis is key to losing weight and is attainable only through my proprietary framework that gets your body into ketosis within 10 minutes."

The Three Secrets aren't new beliefs to install; they are the three false beliefs people already hold that keep the Big Domino standing. Knock down all three and it falls.

## Big Domino slides (1–6): the first 15 minutes

Before building, write the who/what statement: "I am going to teach ___ (insert submarket) how to ___ (insert result) through ___ (insert your niche)." For example, real estate investors how to make money by flipping houses on eBay; people sick of dieting how to lose weight by drinking ketones.

1. **Title slide.** An opportunity-switch headline: "How to (result they desire most) without (thing they fear most)." Examples: create a seven-figure funnel in less than 30 minutes without having to hire, or be held hostage by, a tech guy; get your sales letters, scripts and emails written in as little as 10 minutes without hiring an expensive copywriter; make $10,000 flipping your first house on eBay without a bank loan; reconnect with your wife without painful counseling. Open with "Welcome to the webinar. This is ___ and today I'm going to show you how to ___ without ___."
2. **Intro and rapport slides.** Blair Warren's five, right at the start:
   - Justify their failures: if you've failed at ___ before, it's not your fault (information overload, the wrong advice).
   - Allay their fears: if you've worried you just can't succeed with ___, "You can do this. You just need the right person to explain it to you."
   - Throw rocks at their enemies: the big companies want you to think you need venture capital or a fancy degree; they're wrong.
   - Confirm their suspicions: if you've suspected the government and banks want you to fail, you're probably right, and we actually care.
   - Encourage their dreams: you have a dream to change the world, and this webinar shows how.
3. **The ruler slide.** "The measuring stick people will use to judge your webinar." State your goals up front so anyone with a different goal can leave, show that the new opportunity delivers their greatest desires and raises their status, and make it inclusive so nobody wonders "Is this for me?": "My goal for this presentation is to help two types of people. For those who are beginners, you'll get (what the presentation/new opportunity will do for them, or how it will fulfill their desires). For more experienced people, you'll get (alternative)." Segments work too (retail store owners get ___, online store owners get ___).
4. **The Big Domino slide.** Reveal the domino as an extension of the goal: "In the next 90 minutes, my goal is to get you to believe that (new opportunity) is the key to unlock (what you desire the most), and I'm going to show you my proprietary frameworks that will make it simple for you to achieve that result."
5. **Qualify yourself slide.** Talking about how great you are is the fastest way to break rapport, yet they need to know you're qualified. Give one or two highlight-reel items, then go straight to the backstory: in the past few years I've had the chance to ___ and help others ___, "but it wasn't always that way. In fact, just a few short years ago I was just like you . . ."
6. **Epiphany Bridge origin story slides.** At least one slide for each of the 14 steps of the Epiphany Bridge script, with bullet points to talk from. This is the first attempt to knock down the Big Domino; some will have the epiphany here and be ready to buy, and everything afterward strengthens that aha.

## The Three Secrets: 45 minutes

Sometimes one epiphany story is enough. Often, especially with expensive opportunities and big life changes, new concerns pop up, and the three secrets answer them.

### Building the secrets

1. **Identify their three core false beliefs** from the story inventory: about **the vehicle** (the new opportunity and its framework), about **internal beliefs** (their ability to execute), and about **external beliefs** (forces outside their control, like time or the economy). Pick the core one per secret that would keep the Big Domino standing; the rest become supporting stories. For funnels: "Funnels sound cool, but I don't understand how they would work for me"; "I'm sold on funnels, but I'm not technically inclined, so I don't think I could build one"; "I think I could build a funnel, but even if I did, I don't know how to drive traffic into it." For network marketing, the table reads: vehicle, "network marketing doesn't work" (from signing up a mother who got mad), rebuilt as "you can generate leads online"; internal, "I'm scared to talk to people" (from an awkward prospect call), rebuilt as "you can sell through email"; external, "my friends won't sign up" (they made fun of me), rebuilt as "you can get interested leads online."
2. **Write three Epiphany Bridge stories**, one breaking each core belief and leading into a framework taught at the strategy level.
3. **Write three secret titles**: rewrite each false belief and story into a "how to" statement hinting that your framework is the key. Curiosity is the point. "Secret #1—Funnel Hacking: How to Ethically Steal More Than $1,000,000 Worth of Funnel Hacks from Your Competitors for Less Than $100." "Secret #2—Funnel Cloning: How to Clone a *Proven* Funnel (Inside of ClickFunnels) in Less Than 10 Minutes." "Secret #3—My #1 Traffic Hack: How to Get the Exact *Same* Customers Who Are Currently Going to Your Competitors to Start Coming to Your Funnel Instead!"

### Breaking and rebuilding belief patterns

"This is not a teaching presentation; this is a presentation to inspire people to take action and change their lives." Teach each framework with the Framework for Teaching Frameworks minus step 3: the story, the strategy (the what), and social proof, with the tactics (the how) left for after the purchase. Until the false beliefs about the strategy are broken, no tactic helps, and teaching tactics in a sales presentation is the surest way to kill the sale. Brunson taught his best material and was praised for "amazing content" while nobody changed; he was a "failed expert and coach." Milk before meat. "The greatest service you can provide for someone is getting them to buy something. The act of buying creates a commitment that causes someone to take action." After the switch he made hundreds of sales instead of a few, and ten times as many people said the content changed their lives.

### Three Secrets slides (7–15)

7. **Transition to the Three Secrets slide.** "Here's what we're going to cover during the next 45 minutes or so:" and the three secret titles.
8. **State Secret #1.** Quickly state the first secret (the vehicle).
9. **Introduce the framework.** It is the framework alluded to in the origin story, so they've already heard of it and seen your results. Name it and tell them to take notes: "Remember earlier I told you about the framework we developed that helped me to (desired result)? Take out your pen and paper... I call it (proprietary name)." No strategy yet.
10. **Learned-or-earned story.** Without this preframe they won't value the gold that follows. The origin story told how you discovered the new opportunity and turned it into a framework; the vehicle story tells how you actually developed the framework. Example: told to model successful people, he launched a supplement company and struggled, funnel hacked a competitor's funnel, modeled it, and sales rose tenfold overnight; the framework: find a funnel to model, funnel hack it (buy the product to see the structure), blueprint the funnel you need, build it.
11. **Teach the strategy.** The what of each step (find, buy, blueprint, build), adding a mini Epiphany Bridge story for a step when time allows, never the how (where to find funnels, what makes one good, what to look for in the design); that is in the training they buy. "If you spend time in the weeds teaching the tactics here, you will lose them." The only goal is to make them believe the strategy will work for them.
12. **Case study slides.** Others' results with the framework.
13. **Supporting Epiphany Bridge stories.** Short stories from the inventory knocking down related false beliefs about the vehicle, each 30 to 60 seconds: name the belief, then a quick story of why it's wrong and what's true. "You're probably thinking (insert false belief), right? Well, (tell quick Epiphany Bridge story)." For example: "You're probably thinking you need a lot of money to drive traffic, right? Well, actually, you only need 100 clicks a day." Jason Fladlien tracked every objection and spent about 90 minutes busting around 50 of them; he sold three times more in that stretch than in the first 90 minutes, with "no resistance left." Adding supporting stories after each secret more than doubled another team's webinar sales.
14. **Second framework (internal beliefs).** The same six moves: state the secret, name the framework, tell the learned-or-earned story, teach the strategy, show case studies, tell supporting stories. Example: having paid expensive programmers, he heard from a partner that the software could be easy, and built ClickFunnels; the framework "How to Build a Funnel in 10 Minutes": pick a template; drag and drop elements to match the brand; type the copy and add images; check it looks good on mobile; launch.
15. **Third framework (external beliefs).** The same six moves. Example: ads on Google and Facebook gave little; software showing where competitors buy ads did; the framework: find a competitor who already has your dream customers; find the sites they buy ads on; model their banner ads; buy ads on the same sites.

Done right, about an hour in they believe the vehicle is the key, that they can use it, and that nothing outside will stop them, and they must pay to unlock the tactics. The last 30 minutes are the Stack and closes: slides 16 to 43 in [stack-and-closes.md](stack-and-closes.md).

## The Perfect Webinar Shortcut

A full Perfect Webinar takes a week or two the first time. With a few days left in an affiliate contest, Brunson whiteboarded one in 15 minutes, delivered it from two phones on Facebook Live and Periscope at once (26 minutes), promoted it for three days, reached more than 100,000 viewers, sold more than $250,000 and won the contest. Another team modeled it the same day with a whiteboard Stack and three secrets on paper, made more than $100,000 the first time, and later more than $650,000 from one Facebook Live with no slides.

**The Perfect Webinar Cheat Sheet**, answered in order:

1. **What new opportunity am I offering?** (The software itself was nothing new, so: "Increasing webinar sales using my weekly webinar model.")
2. **What special offer can I create for those who purchase?** Spend five minutes stacking what you already have that complements it (a script, a training, a recorded live close, a funnel template), each with a value, and total it.
3. **What is the one Big Domino for this offer?** (That webinars run through his model are the only way to reach seven figures in the next 12 months; the title, "How to Make (at Least) Seven Figures Next Year with This Webinar Model.")
4. **What is my Epiphany Bridge origin story to attempt to knock down the Big Domino?** (Bombing at his first event until a guide taught him the Stack.)
5. **What is the framework I'm teaching and the false belief I'm trying to break?** (Vehicle)
6. The same for **internal beliefs**.
7. The same for **external beliefs**.
8. **How can I structure the Stack and close to increase my sales conversions?**

For each secret, fill five lines: framework, false belief, truth, story, title. His three:
- Vehicle: framework, the Perfect Webinar; false belief, "Perfect Webinars don't work for me"; truth, "you just need the right script"; story, how he developed it; title, "It's All about the Script."
- Internal: the weekly webinar framework; "I did a webinar once and it bombed"; do it consistently every week for a year; how ClickFunnels grew on a weekly webinar; "Understanding the Model."
- External: transitioning to an automated webinar; "I have to do live webinars for the rest of my life"; once the pitch is perfected you can automate it; his own switch after 60 live runs; "You Have to Do This Live Until . . ."

**Live on social video.** Every viewer sees the presentation (on a traditional webinar about 40 percent register and about 20 percent show up), and a version that converts can be run as an ad while it keeps selling. Practice in a live webinar first: live video on Facebook or Instagram is intimidating and the comments can be brutal.

"The Perfect Webinar is perfect." If it doesn't work, it is a bad market, an improvement offer, or a presenter who slipped into teaching tactics before selling strategies. A good market, a truly irresistible new opportunity, and three broken-and-rebuilt belief patterns make it work in VSLs, teleseminars, product launch videos, Google Hangouts, Facebook Live and email sequences.

## The 5-Minute Perfect Webinar

A soap maker who sold at farmers markets tried a 90-minute webinar for a $200 package and it failed. Ecommerce sellers assumed the framework was only for authors, speakers, coaches and consultants; she asked how to make it work for soap, cut it to five minutes for a $39 product, made more than $130,000 in six weeks and almost $2 million the first year. Her ad opened with a misconception ("Did you know that the most powerful ingredient for your skin does not come from plants?").

Use it for live videos that become ads, ad videos, landing pages and sales pages for products low on the value ladder, under about $100. The higher the price, the longer the presentation. The script, developed with Jim Edwards, in order (fill the parentheses):

1. **Misconception hook**: "Hey, did you know that (big misconception)?"
2. **Reframe and promise**: it sounds crazy because we're used to hearing about (the usual thing), and while that matters, I'm going to share three secrets that will revolutionize the way you (area of their life).
3. **Introduce yourself**: I'm (name), a (role) who has helped (accomplishment).
4. **The big idea**: "So what is (the big idea or thing you'll reveal)? It's (the thing). It's (more details about the thing)."
5. **Promise the payoff**: three secrets about (the thing) and how you can (big payoff).
6. **First objection**: "And I know what you're probably thinking: (objection)." But I'll show you a great way to (action) and get amazing results.
7. **Secret 1**: the secret, the big idea behind it, why it matters.
8. **Secret 2**: the secret, the main thing to understand, what it means.
9. **Secret 3**: the secret, the main thing to understand, why it's key.
10. **Next objection**: "Now, I know what you're thinking. It's (next objection). Well the tricky thing is that (real truth behind the objection they don't realize)!"
11. **The offer**: I've (created/developed/found) this (thing) so you can (what they can do with it); what makes it special; what it will do for them.
12. **Your superpower**: as a (role) who has (accomplishment), what I do is (the secret of your success), so I've (what you've created for them).
13. **Call to action**: "If you click the link, you can (what they can do)."
14. **Scarcity**: I don't know how much longer this will be available, and prices will probably go up because (the real reason); click the link to get it today.
15. **Reassurance**: we're here to help, with amazing results for (target audience) just like you struggling with (problem).
16. **Next offer**: mention (the next thing you'll offer as a one-time offer), but today it's about (the thing).
17. **Sign-off**: "I can't wait to see you on the inside. Have a great day."
