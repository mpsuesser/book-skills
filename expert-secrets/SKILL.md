# Expert Secrets

A funnel can be built correctly and still not convert. What converts online visitors into lifelong customers is the expert: the story of why they created the offer, and the movement they lead. As an expert grows they move from selling a product, to creating offers, to leading a movement. Brunson treats business as a calling: you have been called to serve a group of people, and getting them to buy is the greatest service you can give them, because buying creates the commitment that makes them act.

The book's one-line statement of a movement: **"The Expert Offers Someone a New Opportunity and Then Guides Them to a Result with a Future-Based Cause."** Every mass movement Brunson studied (companies, religions, political movements, cults) has all three parts: a charismatic leader, a new opportunity, and a future-based cause that unites the tribe.

## The system

The book builds in four sections, each resting on the one before.

1. **Creating your movement.** The three parts of the **Your Movement** triangle:
   - **The Expert/Guide.** The **Attractive Character** at the front of the value ladder attracts people; the expert then guides them up the ladder to a result. People come for the product and stay for the relationship. "People are silently begging to be led" (Jay Abraham).
   - **The New Opportunity.** A new vehicle to the result the customer wants, in a category you create.
   - **The Future-Based Cause.** The vision of where the tribe is going together, the identity they take on, and the awards and mission that keep them moving.
   - The value ladder mission statement ties them: "We Help ___ [Dream Customer] to Achieve ___ [Result] Through ___ [New Opportunity]."
2. **Creating belief.** **Hook, Story, Offer**: the hook grabs the dream customer's attention, the story builds the perceived value, the offer is what you sell. Every asset (ad, email, post, podcast episode, landing page, sales page, upsell page) carries all three, and "if something wasn't working in your funnel, it was always the hook, the story, or the offer." When perceived value exceeds price people say yes; when it doesn't, they don't. Story is the preframe that raises perceived value: **story selling** means telling the story of how you learned or earned what is in your offer.
3. **One-to-Many Selling.** Face to face you can ask questions and resolve objections as they come up; from a stage, a webinar or a funnel you can't, so the presentation must resolve in advance every objection that will come up for as many people as possible. Your platform and your funnels are your **virtual stage**: ads, landing pages, live and automated webinars, Facebook and Instagram lives, YouTube, podcasts all take the same structure, the **Perfect Webinar**.
4. **Becoming your dream customer's guide.** You are the guide in the customer's story and they are the hero. Test the presentation live every week until it is perfected, then plug its stories and structure into every funnel on the value ladder.

## Leading terms

- **New opportunity vs. improvement offer.** An improvement offer promises something better, faster, stronger (almost any word ending in "er") inside a vehicle the customer already knows: you become a shark in someone else's red ocean, fighting over scraps. A new opportunity replaces what isn't working with something altogether different. "Improvement offers sell through the pain, whereas new opportunities sell away from the pain." Even when the product is an improvement, the framework wrapped around it has to be a new opportunity.
- **Status.** The only thing that causes people to move or not move (Perry Belcher). The buyer's subconscious asks: "Is this thing I'm considering going to increase my status or decrease it?" Status is how you perceive yourself, not how others see you. Load the increase side and remove the decrease side; "the key to making a sale is 100 percent tied to this concept of status."
- **Opportunity switch / opportunity stack.** The switch moves customers out of their old vehicle into your new one. "Every business should have one and only one opportunity switch." Every other offer on the ladder **stacks**: the same opportunity packaged differently to serve them at a higher level.
- **Framework.** The process someone must follow to get the result. "Your product is likely just a piece of that framework." Frameworks get proprietary names and are the raw material of every info product, tool, story and presentation.
- **Epiphany Bridge.** "A story that takes people through the emotional experience that got you excited about the new opportunity you're presenting." People buy on emotion and justify with logic; the story carries them across from the emotional side to the logical side so they reach the epiphany themselves and sell themselves. **Technobabble** (facts, jargon, industry numbers, "groundbreaking") delivered before the epiphany is the number one sales killer.
- **False beliefs.** Every belief comes from an experience that created a story (**Experience → Story → Belief**). Selling means identifying the false stories that keep customers from success and rewriting them with Epiphany Bridge stories. Three core false beliefs recur through the whole book: about the **vehicle** (this new opportunity isn't right for me), **internal** beliefs (I couldn't succeed with it), and **external** beliefs (something outside my control will stop me).
- **Story inventory.** A stock of Epiphany Bridge stories, each built to break one false belief, so the expert always has the story that answers the objection in front of them.
- **The Big Domino.** The one thing that, if the prospect believes it, makes every other objection fall or become irrelevant. A presentation is built to make them believe only that.
- **The Stack / Stack Slide.** The offer presented as a growing list of elements, each with its value, restacked after every new element, so the last thing they see before the price is the whole offer.
- **Trial closes.** Dozens of small yes-or-no questions whose only answer is yes, so the request for money is never the first yes.
- **The Frustrated.** Of the three groups in any red ocean (Diehard, Satisfied, Frustrated), the only one to sell to.

## The Epiphany Bridge script

Five phases, fourteen questions; it scales from a 30-second version to two hours. It is the shape of origin stories, belief-breaking stories, closes, ads, emails, lead magnets and product launches.

1. **The Backstory.** (1) What is your BACKSTORY that gives us a vested interest in your journey? (2) What is the DESIRE or result you want to achieve, external and internal? (3) What are the OLD VEHICLES you tried in the past that didn't work?
2. **The Journey.** (4) What was THE CALL that made you start on this journey? (5) Who or what is THE VILLAIN keeping you from success? (6) WHAT will happen if you don't have success?
3. **New Opportunity.** (7) Who was the GUIDE who gave you the epiphany? (8) What was the EPIPHANY? (9) What is the NEW OPPORTUNITY you created from it?
4. **The Framework.** (10) What is the STRATEGY or frameworks you developed? (11) What were THE RESULTS you got following them? (12) What were OTHERS' RESULTS?
5. **Achievement and Transformation.** (13) WHAT end result did you achieve (external desires)? (14) HOW did you transform during your journey (internal desires)?

How each part is told, the 30-second version, and the craft rules (oversimplification, the "kinda like" bridge, making them feel) are in [story-selling.md](story-selling.md).

## The Perfect Webinar

The perfect sales presentation, in any medium. Three phases, timed for 90 minutes (30-minute version in brackets):

1. **The Big Domino** with introduction and origin story: first 15 minutes [5]. Big Domino statement: "If I can make people believe that (my new opportunity/category) is/are the key to (the result they desire most) and is/are only attainable through (my specific vehicle/frameworks), then all other objections and concerns become irrelevant and they have to give me money." The origin story is the first attempt to knock it down.
2. **The Three Secrets**, 15 minutes each [5 each]: Secret #1 the **Vehicle**, Secret #2 **Internal Beliefs**, Secret #3 **External Beliefs**. Each gets a framework, its learned-or-earned story, its strategy, social proof and quick supporting stories. Teach the **strategy (what)**, never the **tactics (how)**: "Your audience's desire for the how is the reason they are going to give you money at the end."
3. **The Stack and Closes**: last 30 minutes [10]. Transition question, the Stack, price, closes, urgency, Q&A.

All 43 slides with their scripts, the 15-minute **Perfect Webinar Shortcut**, and the **5-Minute Perfect Webinar** for low-priced products and ads are in [perfect-webinar.md](perfect-webinar.md). Slides 16 to 43 (the Stack, price and closing sequence), the trial closes and the 16 mini closes are in [stack-and-closes.md](stack-and-closes.md).

## Applying the book

1. **Find the stage.** For a diagnosis, decide which of hook, story or offer is failing, then which belief the prospect still lacks. For a build, find where the user is in the build order below. Done when the problem is named in the book's terms (for example, "improvement offer", "no Big Domino", "selling to Diehards", "teaching tactics").
2. **Build in the book's order**, each stage feeding the next: dream customer and the market where they already congregate → new opportunity and category → Big Domino statement → named frameworks → Stack Slide offer (built before the presentation) → story inventory → Perfect Webinar → live weekly testing → plugged into the funnels on the value ladder. Done when every stage the task touches rests on a finished earlier stage, or the gap is named.
3. **Fill the author's script, part by part.** Every script in the book (the Epiphany Bridge, the Big Domino statement, the slides, the closes) is filled in completely, every part answered, in the user's own material and the customer's words. Done when no part is blank.
4. **Run the diagnostics** below on the result.

## Diagnostics

- **One core desire.** The message speaks to one of health, wealth or relationships. Asking someone to believe in two things usually cuts conversions in half, most times by 90 percent or more.
- **New opportunity test.** If the Big Domino statement won't form a valid argument (if A, then B; A; therefore B), the offer is an improvement offer in a red ocean, not a new opportunity.
- **Prolific Zone.** Mainstream ideas carry no money; crazy ones recruit only a few. The message sits in between, polarizing enough to be noticed.
- **Who you are fishing for.** Sell only to the Frustrated. A feature war means you are trying to convert a Diehard; price shopping means the Satisfied.
- **10x value.** The Stack's total value is at least ten times the price ($97 offer, $997 or more in value). If not, back to the drawing board.
- **Strategy, not tactics.** A selling presentation that teaches the how kills sales.
- **Emotion before logic.** Story first, at about a third-grade reading level, every hard concept bridged with "kinda like".
- **When the Perfect Webinar doesn't work**, it is one of three things: a bad market (no one wants to hear), an improvement offer (no one wants to buy), or the presenter slipped into teaching tactics before selling strategies.
- **Webinar numbers.** $3 to $5 per registrant (above that, the page, the message or the targeting is wrong); about 25 percent show up; a 5 percent close is a good webinar, 10 percent is a million-dollar-a-year webinar.
