# Team assessment

A straightforward diagnostic of a team's susceptibility to the five dysfunctions. Have every member complete it if possible. Then review the results together, discuss where answers differ, and identify any clear implications for the team.

**Instructions.** Rate how each statement applies to the team, honestly and without over-thinking: **3 = Usually, 2 = Sometimes, 1 = Rarely.** The statements below are given in paraphrase.

1. Members discuss issues passionately and without guarding what they say.
2. Members call out one another's deficiencies or unproductive behaviors.
3. Members know what their peers are working on and how it contributes to the team's collective good.
4. Members apologize quickly and genuinely when they say or do something inappropriate or possibly damaging to the team.
5. Members willingly sacrifice in their own departments or areas of expertise (budget, turf, head count) for the good of the team.
6. Members openly admit their weaknesses and mistakes.
7. Team meetings are compelling, not boring.
8. Members leave meetings confident that their peers are completely committed to the decisions agreed on, even after initial disagreement.
9. Failing to achieve team goals significantly affects morale.
10. The most important and difficult issues are put on the table in team meetings to be resolved.
11. Members are deeply concerned about letting down their peers.
12. Members know about one another's personal lives and are comfortable discussing them.
13. Members end discussions with clear, specific resolutions and calls to action.
14. Members challenge one another about their plans and approaches.
15. Members are slow to seek credit for their own contributions and quick to point out others'.

## Scoring

Add the three statements for each dysfunction:

| Dysfunction | Statements |
|---|---|
| 1. Absence of trust | 4, 6, 12 |
| 2. Fear of conflict | 1, 7, 10 |
| 3. Lack of commitment | 3, 8, 13 |
| 4. Avoidance of accountability | 2, 11, 14 |
| 5. Inattention to results | 5, 9, 15 |

- **8 or 9**: the dysfunction is probably not a problem for the team.
- **6 or 7**: the dysfunction could be a problem.
- **3 to 5**: the dysfunction probably needs to be addressed.

Whatever the scores, every team needs constant work; without it, even the best deviate toward dysfunction. ## Reading the scores

- Score each member's answers separately before totaling the team. The discussion of discrepancies is where the diagnosis happens: a leader who rates conflict 9 while members rate it 4 has found the problem.
- Interpret low scores through the model's chain. A low commitment score with a low conflict score points down to conflict, and a low conflict score points down to trust. The behavior lists in each dysfunction's file ([trust.md](trust.md), [conflict.md](conflict.md), [commitment.md](commitment.md), [accountability.md](accountability.md), [results.md](results.md)) confirm or refine what the numbers suggest.
- Kathryn's own team review asked the same thing in plain words: redraw the triangle, ask "How are we doing?", and go level by level. Members' answers then ("We certainly trust each other more... still too early to say," "doing better with conflict, although I can't say I'm used to it yet," "accountability... worries me the most") are the qualitative version of this scoring.
