# Other therapies through the TRP lens

Map any system by asking which TRP step each move fulfills, whether it fulfills it explicitly or implicitly, and which step is missing when markers don't appear. Systems don't *always* fulfill the core process, but when they do, transformational change occurs. Systems that assume the schema's content theoretically and do Step C without Step B succeed only when the schema happens to match the theory (chapters on AEDP, EMDR, ISTDP and psychedelic-assisted therapy show Step C without B). The TRP map lets a therapist switch between systems' methods mid-session without disruption. Schools should adjust to the TRP rather than tamper with it.

Parentheses mark steps that occur implicitly. What matters is the *concurrence* of Steps 1 and 2, not their order. Other systems shown elsewhere to produce transformational change: Alexander Technique, NLP, Progressive Counting, Social-Cognitive Transactional Analysis, Tapping; also Hakomi, Sensorimotor Psychotherapy, Ideal Parent Figure, Presence Psychotherapy, Deep Brain Reorienting and Traumatic Incident Reduction. Schema Therapy is being integrated with the TRP.

## Hindsight inquiry

When markers appear without visible Steps 1–2–3, ask in hindsight. The question uses the disconfirming knowledge as a portal to the schema it disconfirmed (reverse engineering): "If it was new for you to hear that ending it with him could be OK even though you had an emotional investment in it from long ago, what were you previously believing or expecting about how it would *not* be OK to end it?" Every such inquiry has revealed an implicit 1–2–3. It shows the client their capacity to unlearn, and shows the therapist how JEs happen. In that case, an offhand counteractive remark ("when an investment goes badly, sometimes it's best to cut your losses") had disconfirmed "I'd be losing so much of myself that I would die or just be an empty shell or ghost forever."

## AEDP (Accelerated Experiential Dynamic Psychotherapy, Fosha)

A psychodynamic, experiential reparative-attachment therapy built on full attention to the present moment of experiencing the therapist's attunement, "undoing of aloneness," and a non-pathologizing stance. It assumes schemas are insecure attachment disconfirmable by "corrective relational experiences," so Step C is moot or done before therapy begins; it starts with Step 2, which induces Step 1.
- *Moves*: tender attunement plus benign challenge of affect avoidance ("what are you feeling inside right now *as you share this with me*?"). The invitation to violate the implicit relational schema reactivates it ("Recognizing and integrating a new, positive relationship in the here and now organically evokes its historical contrast"). "So what's it like to have me inviting you to be sad with me?" brought "like a forbidden action," the schema made explicit alongside acceptance. Then repeated recognition of the relational experience: "Do you feel alone right now?"; "Do you feel my presence with you?"; "What's it like to have another man not abandoning you and also not having an ulterior motive with you?"
- *Markers*: deep sobbing received ("I'm right here, Daniel") disconfirmed the oceanic-grief schema; "So this is what it's like, huh?"; cessation of affect avoidance and depersonalization; a deep breath; "not being hunted"; better self-care.
- *Reading*: AEDP's **metacognitive processing** holds that secure attachment "in and of itself, is necessary but in fact often insufficient" and that "both experiential shifts and intentional reflection upon those shifts are necessary in equal measure." In TRP terms the needed reflection is a sustained JE. When AEDP focuses on emerging well-being, the new positive states must disconfirm the old ones, not be installed as separate competing learnings. Its attachment assumption raises the question of what to do when schemas are not of that type.

## EFT (Emotion-Focused Therapy, Greenberg)

Humanistic, drawing on Gestalt, person-centered, systemic and attachment work; emotions express needs. Its slogan is "changing emotion with emotion," and it recognizes MR.
- *Moves*: the client's own words held B and C together ("I believe I'm a bad person, but deep down inside I don't think I'm a bad person… I'm grieving for what I probably didn't have"). Empty-chair dialogue with the father ("Tell him what it was like to be called a 'devil'…") produced the JE: I was bad / I wasn't bad. Expressing unmet needs was tolerable because the therapist accompanied; aloneness amplifies distress to unbearable and forces dissociation. An inventory of ordeals supplied more C and repetitions. Two-chair work with the inner critic softened it ("it wasn't because I was unlovable; it was just because they were incapable of those emotions").
- *Markers*: "I needed to be hugged once in a while as a child or told that I was OK. I think that's normal."; no collapse into helplessness; compassion for the father while holding him accountable; "relief that I don't have this anger sitting on my chest anymore."
- *Reading*: shame ended when the self-model was juxtaposed with a different *meaning* of the father's behavior (his failure to be loving, though she is lovable). So the process is **changing mental model with mental model**, and "changing emotion with emotion" misrepresents the phenomenology. Facing shame and receiving acceptance "rather than the expected disgust or denigration" works because the learned *expectation* is the target. Two conflicting *solutions* (cut off feelings vs keep hoping) are preferences, not contradictory models, so holding both is no JE.

## EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing, Shapiro)

**Dual focus**: attention on an internal component of the memory plus concurrent bilateral stimulation (eye movements, tones, taps). Its model is Adaptive Information Processing, worked through an 8-phase protocol with SUDS ratings. Safe-place imagery is for stability, not part of the change process.
- *Moves*: inventory the symptoms and when they occur (A). Select the target image. For the negative cognition, persist past denial: normalize ("some people might feel guilty"), then "Check deep inside. Let your gut answer, not your mind." This completed B ("It was my fault"). The positive cognition ("What would you like to believe instead?") often supplies C as an idea, to be vivified into real experience. Each set begins with what emerged from the last; "Stay with that" plus another set gives Step 3.
- *Signature*: new contradicting knowing emerges suddenly and unpredictably, as whole-body lucid truth ("It was a choice he made. It had nothing to do with me."), sometimes known all along in a separate context, sometimes new: a "hidden store of intuitive knowledge." The process is nonlinear; five areas (self-blame, grief, abandonment, guilt, identity) surfaced, and none addressed the panic directly.
- *Markers*: SUDS 0; positive cognition "completely true"; "I'm not the person I thought I was"; body symptoms gone. A later panic in a store resolved when she recognized the cues (tall shelves = basement, the PA din = the tornado's roar) as present reminders of the past ordeal. The unlearning sequence can occur in daily life.
- *Why dual focus works*: reactivation normally happens outside awareness and takes over the state of mind. Dual focus anchors the viewpoint in the present, safe environment while the client attends mindfully to the material without its "spell." This **unmerged attending** lets the built-in mismatch detector evaluate each recognized element, so contradictory knowledge emerges spontaneously. A subtler dual focus occurs whenever a client interacts with the therapist while attending to revealed material. A traumatic memory carries both a neocortical construal (responsible for the death → guilt) and a subcortical one (tall shelves = tornado → panic).
- *Research*: a 72-study review was mixed; RCT formats cannot detect transformational change, while close scrutiny of individual cases shows it. Within Coherence Therapy, bilateral sets serve as a discovery tool.

## IFS (Internal Family Systems, Schwartz)

Exiles carry the burden of overwhelming original suffering; protectors are managers (proactive: perfectionism, workaholism, people-pleasing) and firefighters (emergency re-suppression: substances, sexual arousal, raging). The Self is an undamaged essence, not a part. "Protective parts can always be identified because they have a strategy."
- *TRP reading*: the exile is the ego state focused on the schema's *problem* (the suffering), and the protector the ego state focused on its *solution* (the tactic). Tightly linked, they form one schema. Discerning whether a response is problem or solution is a critical skill.
- *Moves*: come to the part; check for concerns about knowing it; "Does it know you're there?"; "How are you feeling towards it?" (a Self-energy check); "make sure it knows that you're the adult now" (updating); ask it not to flood; "How come it's holding all this fear?" heads straight to the exile's distress (B). "See what she needs you to know" reveals more. Ask reporting or storyteller parts to soften back, and keep questions in the present tense when a shift to past tense signals distancing. Make explicit what was learned from the suffering: "What's it like for her to be alone?"
- *The key move*: "it sounds like she's got that at the moment with you. She's got that heart-connection." → "Oh, that surprises her!" Prompting an inner child to recognize the present moment's connection with the adult self appears in many experiential systems and often produces the cascade C–1–2–3. "Just stay with it… This is true… She has it now" maintains the JE.
- *Markers*: amazement; relaxing, curiosity (thorough freedom from distress); imaginally revisiting the trigger brings no fear; years without recurrence. Partial relief can occur at Step B through recognition, but transformational change requires 1–2–3. A recommended daily practice helps integration, but a nullified schema needs none.
- Remote video sessions suit deep experiential work. A transpersonal experience in an altered state is not integrated automatically; reflecting it back gives it metacognitive recognition.

## IPNB (Interpersonal Neurobiology, Siegel; case by Badenoch)

Positive dyadic relational experience brings reparative attachment and integrated brain functioning. The client feels attunement; attends to and verbalizes neglected right-brain and subcortical activity; identifies how current triggers resemble the past; and receives de-pathologizing brain education. Published cases are qualitative narrative, so mapping is inferential.
- *Moves*: ask the deeper mind to guide toward the source. Ask each inner parent figure in turn what was hurting or scaring them; the figures are portals to implicit knowledge. Staying with the father figure's pull-away impulse revealed the schema: danger is emotional abandonment, so leave before being left. Offering the figure what was missing (understanding, comfort, continuous connection) disconfirmed it. The mother figure needed many experiences, meaning several schemas. Trust the process even when slow.
- *Markers*: the figure's tension releasing; the father figure curious rather than backing away; fear of closeness "a distant memory," a brief impulse she no longer needs to follow.

## ISTDP (Intensive Short-Term Dynamic Psychotherapy, Davanloo)

"Unlocking the unconscious": the visceral emergence of nonconscious feelings and memories, aiming at total resolution. Defenses are tactics keeping disturbance out of awareness; the therapist detects and comments on anxiety and avoidance from the first session, assesses tolerance, and challenges avoidance until anger at the therapist, invited openly, surfaces the core childhood distress. Its core model (thwarted love → pain, grief, rage → guilt over murderous rage) holds in some subset of cases. The authors note that schemas producing symptoms adaptively involve no conflict in themselves, and prefer "client" to "patient."
- *Moves*: "what's the smile?"; "How do you experience this irritation inside?"; "let's not go to why yet"; "Even this you do nicely"; dealing with a fantasy "as it's occurring here with me." When the therapist's demeanor was too different for the projection to hold, the mother appeared and the schema surfaced: "all this 'Control yourself!'—I can't stand it!", her mother's terms of attachment. Conscious awareness of the demand, her lifelong suffering, her obedience, her protest and her freedom to stop complying formed the JE.
- *Reading*: ISTDP guides tolerable suspension of avoidance while conveying that feelings are valid (C), and the accepting therapist provides interpersonal JEs. But it lacks **specificity of disconfirmation**: a schema such as "If I feel and express negative feelings… I will be utterly disgusting and unlovable to Mom and Dad" isn't disconfirmed by a *therapist's* welcome. Rage at one ex plucked "this particular visible dandelion flower" while "the dandelion root could remain intact." Hence the double sequence A–C–1–2–3–B–C–1–2–3–V.
- *Markers*: disinterest in justifying herself (authentic autonomy, not rebellion); grief and spontaneous compassion for the mother; freedom from affect avoidance, depression and ulcerative colitis, confirmed by colonoscopy and maintained at follow-ups up to 14 years.

## Psychedelic-assisted therapy (ayahuasca)

Potent when the program provides mindset preparation, accompaniment and safety during the session, and review, reflection and integration after. In the case, 14 weekly therapy sessions and 3 ayahuasca sessions were each followed by **Restorative Retelling**: narrative reconstruction and meaning-making (close the eyes, identify the most emotionally charged fragments, organize them into a coherent narrative). The client's complicated grief after her mother's concealed suicide scored 51 on the ICG (above 30 = complicated grief), then 34 before the third session, 7 one week after, and 13 three weeks later.
- *Moves*: questions that turned panic imagery into metacognitive meaning ("What do these figures suggest to you?… what role?"); then "Do you think that this experience has changed the impression you had about your mother in any way?"
- *Reading*: the mother appearing with all-loving presence was contradictory knowledge to models of an unloving, abandoning, unreachable mother. Her begging forgiveness and accepting responsibility met the ideal elements of repair: anguish, remorse, contrition, a request for forgiveness, total acceptance of responsibility. That repair disconfirmed the hopeless expectation that reconnection was impossible for life, which had driven depression and "why bother?" collapsed motivation. The change came from specific disconfirmation, not from how positive the experiences were. There is no mechanism unique to psychedelics; their special value is lucid access to deeply felt knowings with self-evident higher-order truth.
- *Recommendations*: do Step B *before* the psychedelic session, so the mindset holds the schema as the conscious target. Integration sessions should elicit, into mindful recognition and explicit words, the old schemas now disconfirmed, and arrive at an explicit juxtaposition for each, sustained and refreshed several times. First run A and B, then ask whether contradictory material can be found without psychedelics. Psychedelics are most clearly warranted when it cannot (a refractory schema, or no skilled TRP practitioner), and only in a safe setting with trained, experienced guidance, preparation, care and follow-up; effects vary, risks exist, and screening for contraindications is necessary. The absence of a deceased person does not as a rule prevent fulfilling the TRP without psychedelics.

## Somatic Experiencing (Levine)

Bottom-up: completing thwarted self-protective responses and discharging excess autonomic arousal, approaching charged memories indirectly and gradually.
- *Moves*: recall a recent triggering encounter, which reactivates the schema; return to sensations rather than analysis; stay through the urge to leave; follow an arm's urge to thrust back, with the therapist's hand as support and resistance; shaking and leg movements. Fragmentary, nonlinear retrieval linked into a coherent memory of an attempted rape, including molding herself to the attacker "to make him think I was his… Then my body knew what to do." Recognizing the survival tactic completed B.
- *Reading*: "I got away" is not contradictory knowledge. The unspoken C: an over-generalized schema becomes false once conscious ("only that one time with that one person"), which rewrote it: someone with a dominating style is not life-threatening, so submissiveness is not the necessary response. The explosiveness was coherent too: self-hatred over compulsive submissiveness that violated her values. Spontaneous implicit JEs after awareness of a generalized schema are familiar; ask "What just happened inside, what just now became clear to you?" to make them explicit.
- *Marker*: submissive and volatile responses ceased; "back to even more of my [former] self."
