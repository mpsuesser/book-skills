# The neuroscience of memory reconsolidation, and evaluating claims

## How the brain unlearns

- **Consolidation**: a fragile initial encoding becomes a stable long-term one. For a century, extinction studies produced only temporary suppression. **Extinction** forms a separate learning in a physically separate memory system that competes against the target learning without replacing it. Consolidation was believed one-way, so emotional memories were thought "indelible."
- **Discovery of MR, 1997–2000.** Nader and colleagues (2000) conditioned rats to fear a tone, reactivated the fear with the tone, then gave an agent blocking consolidation: the fear was erased. Six hours after reactivation the agent had no effect, which defines a **reconsolidation window** of about five hours, during which the destabilized memory is *labile*. "Reconsolidation" names either the re-locking at the end of the labile period or the whole destabilize-and-restabilize process.
- **Prediction error (PE).** Pedreira and colleagues (2004) found that reactivation alone does *not* destabilize a memory. It takes reactivation *plus* a concurrent *mismatch* between what the reactivated learning expects and what is perceived. At least 30 further studies confirmed this. "PE was found to be paramount for memory reconsolidation." Early studies, Nader's included, contained an unnoticed mismatch. The belief that every reactivation induces MR persisted for over a decade, and studies without mismatch failed.
- **Procedures vs experiences.** The brain responds to the subjective experience of a violated expectation, so therapists are not constrained to particular techniques for creating the key experiences. Many lab procedures induced *the same series of subjective experiences*, as many procedures can induce laughing.
- **Boundary conditions.** A mismatch can be *too weak* (too subtle to be unambiguous, or unnoticed) or *too strong*: so different that it registers as irrelevant to the target learning, not as an *error*, and is attributed to a different **latent cause**. This explains why 20 human studies succeeded and 14 failed to eliminate an acquired emotional reaction. The lab's delicacy comes from the artificially precise structure of Pavlovian target learnings and does not carry over to clinical work.
- **Behavioral updating**: without drugs, the target memory is altered by relevant new learning during the window. The first full unlearning of fear came in animals (Monfils 2009) and in humans (Schiller 2010, which ran Steps 1–2–3 in 15 minutes). Behavioral updating can strengthen, weaken, nullify or modify content, or "integrate" the memory with a salient new experience.
- **Contradiction, a specialization of PE.** In the lab, absence of the expected shock contradicts the learning ("nonreinforcement" misses its subjective quality); a shock at a different body part is a PE but no contradiction. Sources of contradictory knowings: observations of the world, personal behavioral experience, and internal construals of new meaning (Speer 2021 showed construed meaning updating a reactivated episodic memory).
- **The unlearning sequence**, as the book states it: (1) *Reactivation*: present salient cues or contexts to re-evoke the target learning's expectations and knowings. (2) *Concurrent contradictory experience*: while the reactivation is maintained, create an experience that contradicts it; the juxtaposition is the PE that makes the memory labile. (3) *Disconfirmation and unlearning*: soon repeat the same juxtaposition several times, which creates a new learning that disconfirms and revises the encoding. Full-strength contradiction in Step 2 is efficient because Step 3 repeats the same pairing. The sequence is "learning that drives unlearning."
- **Pharmacological blockade vs behavioral updating.** Blocking protein synthesis disrupts reconsolidation itself, so the target is nonresponsive after 24 hours *without any unlearning*; the schema stays intact and only its affective expression is blocked, which, like emotional dissociation, may produce somatic symptoms. Behavioral updating disrupts only the *content*, and the revised encoding restabilizes and functions. Propranolol is the only consolidation blocker considered safe for humans, and both approaches have had replication failures. "Obviously, a behavioral procedure will be preferred over pharmacological manipulations provided that similar effects can be obtained." Behavioral updating also clears the threat memory from both subcortical implicit and neocortical declarative systems, which drugs do not, and is available to every clinician, counselor and coach.
- **MR is a mechanism, not a change.** It *allows* updating; change is driven by the learning that occurs during the window. With no new learning, the target is slightly strengthened and made more precise.
- **MR is not Hebbian.** Counteractive learning needs many repetitions ("neurons that fire together, wire together"); MR is swift.

## The FAQs the book answers

- **Specificity.** Annulment is restricted to precisely the reactivated target learning; closely related non-reactivated learnings are unaffected. Autobiographical memory stays intact: the person remembers having had the fear, but the fear is not re-evoked.
- **Emotional arousal.** *Yes* operationally, since reactivating an emotion-generating memory involves arousal; *no* fundamentally, since MR also works on non-emotional memories (syllable pairs, object recognition, motor tasks). "It is the model that is being disconfirmed and unlearned, not the emotion. … A transformational change in the model immediately produces profound change in the emotion generated." Blocked emotion must be made tolerable so the schema can be accessed; experiencing it alleviates some symptoms and correlates with outcome, but is not transformational unless the schema is disconfirmed. Accounts that give arousal a fundamental role in MR (Lane 2015, Stevens 2019, Welling 2012) omit the PE requirement and the unlearning sequence, and are incorrect.
- **Corrective emotional experience (CEE)**, Alexander and French's term, depends on disconfirming negative expectations, yet richly affective "corrective" experiences often produce no lasting shift. The reason: disconfirmation requires the new experience *concurrent* with foreground reactivation of the problem learning, *and* a contradiction of high specificity, with both sides felt as real and true. Unprompted, the client disengages from the negative learning during the positive experience. The book defines CEE as JE and names "the operational necessity of prompting the client into explicit foreground awareness of both sides of the juxtaposition, affectively felt." "An experience that *could* effectively serve to create disconfirmation and transformational change fails to do so if the target learning is not concurrently reactivated."
- **Why not extinction.** A standard extinction series creates no PE, because the large mismatch reads as a different latent cause. A *single* unreinforced presentation does destabilize; after a rest, an extended series drives true unlearning (misnamed "post-retrieval extinction"). "Reconsolidation cannot be reduced down to facilitated extinction." This timing delicacy is irrelevant clinically, because clients' learnings rarely have such precise structures.
- **What happens to the encoding.** The evidence points to a *storage* effect, not a retrieval deficit or a competing suppression: effective trauma therapy requires "the engagement, rather than the suppression, of the original trauma." "Erasure" is justified but may be incomplete, leaving residual traces too weak to cue. The book prefers **annulment**, **nullification**, **depotentiation** and **dissolution**; episodic memory is not erased.
- **Why the lab studies only fears and addictions.** Neuroscientists are limited to measurable phenomena such as threat and addiction-cue memories; anger, sexual response, grief, attachment and attributed meanings are out of reach in the lab.
- **Ecological validity.** Complex preferences and meanings have been revised by complex contradictory experience; a *mental image* of the cue without shock reactivated the fear and created PE; *vicarious* counter-learning (a video of a person calmly viewing the cue) unlearned it. This grounds imaginal and vicarious work.
- **Lab vs therapy.** The lab knows its target, having created it on Day 1. The therapist faces *unknown content* and *complexity*, hence Steps A and B. Complexity is an advantage: a learning has several components, and succeeding with any one is enough.

## Varieties of mnemonic change: the MTAU framework

The **Mnemonic Therapeutic Action Unification (MTAU) framework** identifies, atheoretically and from the brain's point of view, the mnemonic nature of any method's therapeutic action. Three modes:
1. **Counteractive**: the new learning is a separate, competing memory that suppresses the old; partial, variable, relapse-prone.
2. **Transformational**: Steps 1–2–3; the old learning is unlearned and rewritten.
3. **MR-mediated "integrate"**: a positive experience is conjoined with the destabilized distress memory, so they co-reactivate permanently and the positive partially mutes the distress ("introducing new emotional experiences in therapy enabling new emotional elements to be incorporated into that memory trace via reconsolidation"). This is a counteractive strategy, unpredictable in degree but stable and permanent, and inherently less effective than unlearning.
The modes are binary in experience, but procedures and effects are analog, so discerning them takes care. Picture each therapy as a three-bar graph of how often it produces each mode, and rank therapies on a **TRP fulfillment spectrum** by their frequency of transformational change. Counteractive methods remain necessary in the contraindicated situations.

Polyvagal-based methods create perceptual safety experiences that bring openness and well-being, but the effects persist only with ongoing environmental reinforcement, so they are counteractive; they can be used adjunctively to induce receptivity before a transformational process.

## Evaluating a claim that a method uses MR

- **Minimum requirement**: any protocol claiming MR must fulfill at least Steps 1–2–3. Special protocols for single-event PTSD and addictions (alcohol, cocaine, heroin, nicotine, internet gaming) usually fulfill Step 1, but not all fulfill Step 2, and not all as a decisive contradiction.
- **Methodology evidence** (mapping the protocol's steps onto the TRP) vs **erasure evidence** (the degree of change at Step V). The markers are the strongest evidence. A partial reduction can validly be ascribed to MR only if it persists long-term and effortlessly with no preventive effort.
- **Inference from markers.** "According to current neuroscience, only the MR mechanism can produce the markers of transformational change." Markers that appear promptly after a distinctive procedure are evidence of causation, not correlation. Any system that regularly produces them can validly claim MR, and whenever they appear, Steps 1–3 must have occurred.
- **Prediction**: any therapy produces deep, lasting change only if Steps 1–2–3 occur, whether anyone is aware of them or not.
- Compatibility with MR knowledge is now a validity criterion for every model of lasting change; the TRP is an "empirically confirmed, atheoretical, transtheoretical, transdiagnostic" framework. Steps 1–3 replicate the experiences in each of the first twenty controlled human studies. Client reports of complete, effortless cessation need no questionnaires.
- One protocol (Högberg and colleagues, 2011) fully implemented the TRP and eliminated the post-traumatic symptoms of suicidal adolescents in a few sessions.
- **CBT as counteractive**: in Brewin's words, CBT "does not directly modify negative information in memory, but produces changes in the relative activation of positive and negative representations such that the positive ones are assisted to win the retrieval competition." Positive psychology and exposure therapy (relapse-prone) are counteractive too.
- **Proposed research**: time-resolved process analysis, testing whether Steps 1–3 occurred just before the markers in sessions that produce them.

## The evidence numbers

- RCTs across 15 therapy types and over 40 years show change of about one standard deviation, usually a reduction of merely 20–25% in symptom strength, slightly better than placebo; outliers of transformational change are invisible in RCT averages. RCTs measure the net *efficacy* of cohorts, an average of proficiencies.
- Okiishi 2003: 1841 clients, 91 therapists, 2.5 years. The most effective therapists' clients improved at 10 times the cohort mean, and the least effective therapists' clients worsened. Therapist effectiveness doesn't correlate with age, gender, experience or type of therapy, and manualization hasn't improved outcomes.
- Common factors theory attributes about 15% of the effect to specific factors and 85% to common factors. Yet Weinberger's 1995 meta-analysis found that guiding clients to face what they had avoided accounted for 40% of outcome variance, against 11% for the alliance. Bringing awareness to suppressed feelings is inherent in Step B and Step 1.
- The unlearning sequence is both a common factor (required across therapies for transformational change) and a specific factor (a well-defined process, not a technique or manual). Common factors alone produce transformational change only in the small subset of cases where the client's experience of the therapist specifically disconfirms the schema; otherwise they are catalysts. RCT data may show that about 15% of therapists applied the effective specific factors: "in the most effective psychotherapy, an environment of good non-specific common factors supports facilitation of the specific factors of emotional accessing and memory reconsolidation." Observation: after a schema is retrieved into awareness but before a JE, its grip continues from session to session despite all the common factors; only the JE dissolves it.
- The book's 26 case examples, 25 without psychedelics, each meet two criteria: unambiguous markers and sufficient moment-to-moment detail. Each was the first case found meeting both for its system (no cherry-picking), and their configuration is scientifically valid causal evidence.

## Wider claims

- MR reverses the field's fragmentation, since confirmed empirical knowledge now constrains models, and its drift toward biochemical and genetic causation (the evidence for SSRIs correcting a neurotransmitter imbalance is invalid): psychological causation will speak for itself, even in psychosis. The authors add that some depression may be neurobiological.
- The TRP as unification: it covers all learning domains, demystifies the mechanism, gives a cross-platform template and shared language, serves as a meta-map for choosing methods flexibly (switching systems' methods mid-session without disruption), discerns transformational from counteractive change, synthesizes specific and common factors, and joins psychotherapy to brain science, letting it "graduate" into a mature science. Humans are deeply coherent, adaptive beings, which challenges the view of symptoms as maladaptive.
