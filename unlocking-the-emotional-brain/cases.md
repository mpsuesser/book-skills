# The book's cases, step by step

Each case gives the symptom, the schema, how each step was done and what marked it complete, and the authors' reading. The techniques named here are defined in discovery-and-integration.md and juxtaposition-and-verification.md. Use the case nearest the client's symptom as a template, then follow the client's own material.

Coherence Therapy (developed 1986–1993, first called Depth Oriented Brief Therapy) took its steps from outlier ultra-effective sessions culled from thousands that showed the markers. The symptoms it has dispelled (Table 3.2): aggressive behavior, agoraphobia, alcohol abuse, anger and rage, anxiety, attachment-pattern behaviors and distress, attention deficit problems, codependency, complex trauma symptomology, compulsive behaviors, couple conflict, communication and closeness, depression, family and child problems, fidgeting, food, eating and weight, grief and bereavement, guilt, hallucinations and delusions, inaction, indecision, low self-worth, panic attacks, perfectionism, post-traumatic symptoms, procrastination, psychogenic and psychosomatic pain, sexual problems, shame, underachieving, voice and speaking problems. It works with individuals, couples, families and children, across cultures and sexual orientations.

## Richard: chronic self-doubt at work (about 6 sessions)

- **A**: self-doubting self-talk despite consistent success.
- **B**: symptom deprivation (imagine being confident in the meeting) brought "really uncomfortable, but—it's in a different way," then "Now they hate me," then "an arrogant asshole—like my father," then "It's what I've always vowed not to be!" The overt statement drew "My body is *buzzing* with how true it feels." A card followed, then connecting up (the self-talk is so I'll keep quiet and won't be hated like Dad), with "more solid" as the response.
- **C**: opposite current experience: a colleague confidently voiced the same idea and everyone was glad ("weird, how differently it went").
- **1–2–3**: re-evoke that meeting while holding "expressing *any* confidence makes you resemble Dad"; the JE repeated four times. "It's like there's this part of the world that I didn't notice before."
- **V**: gleeful laughter: "How could I think *that*?" A juxtaposition card was read before work and during meetings. Later: "When I have something to say… I just say it, and it's no big deal"; "what was my *problem*?"
- **Reading**: the target was the learning that made self-doubt *necessary*, not the self-negative thoughts.

## Charlotte: obsessive attachment to an ex (10 sessions)

- **B**: "primal connection… in utero" suggested merging. A sentence stem, "If I let this end—," brought "I lose *me*." Her overt statement met resistance from her values and was softened to "An important *part* of me wants to be you." Finding her own purpose was the milestone, though only the solution had surfaced so far.
- **C**: in the week, the card triggered mismatch detection: "*no boundaries equals death*," the solution's unacceptable costs.
- **1–2–3**: elicited more completely ("giving up the possibility of ever being seen for who *I* am… that's what the 'death' is"), then the two-knowings JE: "how could I possibly have thought it could work to leave myself out like that?" A juxtaposition card followed.
- **More schemas**: the symptom continued, so discovery resumed. An age-12 divorce schema ("love isn't strong enough to keep together people who love each other… So I won't let go!"), with the master construct that the world is horrible if love can't hold people, met opposite current experience: friends who still loved each other after separating.
- **V**: ceased contact, calm acceptance; no recurrence a year later.
- **Reading**: no counteracting (no thought-stopping, no support-building, no self-worth work, no reparative attachment). The relationship was catalytic, not curative.

## Ted: pervasive underachieving, complex trauma

- **B**: symptom deprivation situated in the relationship (imagine succeeding and telling Dad) brought "edgy." The stem "If you think I'm doing well—" brought "would prove he's been successful as a parent." Asked "What do you want him to feel…?", he answered "like shit." Problem: no accountability for his father's lovelessness. Solution: shape his own life as glaring evidence of the injury. The stem "The way I can make Dad realize… is—" brought "me being a mess," and the shock of recognizing his own agency. Resistance to integration was met with a reframe and "I hate to admit this, but—". Card instruction: "don't try to change anything, for now."
- **C**: at the next session, "Sounds as though you believe he *could* get it" revealed the master construct of possibility. The magnet overt statement "My father is a man who's willing to recognize his own big mistake" brought "Fuck!… What a joke."
- **Resistance to transformation**: "I just want to fight it!" He made an overt statement of the refusal, took a grief stem, and the cards held the juxtaposition plus his ownership of the refusal. Ten sessions over six months worked through the themes of aloneness, wasted life, connection-through-struggle and self-responsibility, then returned to the primary JE with natural sorrow: "my father will go to his grave never getting it."
- **V**: imagining finishing the course and re-saying the old card sentences brought no grip. Two years later he was engaged, had finished training and held a job; "Those cards still come in handy."
- **Reading**: a happy-ending solution; the attachment pattern changed with no reparative attachment and no transference.

## Brenda: stage fright, acute trauma

- **A–B**: dreams of runaway vehicles; Gestalt dreamwork brought an intruding image of herself at 8 in the back seat with her drunk father careening toward a bridge. Target: "I'm helplessly trapped in this car that is carrying me to my death."
- **1–2–3**: empowered re-enactment, under four minutes. The therapist coached ("Stop the car right now!"), pushed, and supplied what needed saying to her mother, plus adults and police; her voice became emotionally real when she opened the door. Then explicit recognition of the contrast with her former powerlessness, with 20-second pauses.
- **V**: "I'm *somewhat* afraid; I think I'm *normally* afraid"; the performance went well; a dream of saying goodbye to her family marked separation.
- **Reading**: the stage fright was an affective flashback; the new knowing of her power disconfirmed expected helplessness. Re-enactment is only for situations that offered early signs of danger.

## Raoul, Travis, Regina, Carol, Tomás: attachment or not

- **Raoul** (hair-trigger anger): instances shared a broken agreement; "What's coming up?" led to a partner's betrayal five years earlier. The anger was a solution to three problems: powerlessness, grief, and the despair of an unjust world. The schema was social or existential, not attachment.
- **Travis** (no relationship reaching closeness): guided visualization at 6 brought "I don't matter, and I'm all on my own," generalized to all people. The present-moment JE with the therapist's empathy brought deeper crying and "amazed and bittersweet relief," then "why did I get parents who couldn't do it?" It was extended to the boss through two-sided questions, then into experiments in choosing to matter.
- **Regina** (social anxiety, panic, perfectionism; insecure-disorganized): comparing her first moment in a social setting with Mom's first moment of displeasure brought "felt the same," so "crazy" fell away. Her card stated terms of attachment, generalization and perfectionism as the solution. The Uncle Theo JE brought "my Mom looks smaller" and "what's wrong is in her, not in *me*," then a newly found purpose (believing Mom's blame gave a sense of control). The card was kept in front of her during calls with Mom; I'm in memory took her from 8 to 2 out of 10 ("It has *never* faded out so fast"). She was largely free by session 16 and ended at 18.
- **Carol** (sexual aversion in marriage): at about 15 her mother found her masturbating and broadcast it. Card: "experiencing sexual pleasure with my husband makes me more like my mother… better to sacrifice pleasure and intimacy than to risk doing to Dana what my mother did to me." The decision path ruled out reparative attachment. The next session the card felt "almost silly": mismatch detection had brought "my sexuality is *not* the same as my mother's."
- **Tomás**: reparative attachment proved infeasible with a well-developed adult identity.

## John: a father's guilt over his daughter's amputation (7 sessions)

- **B**: symptom deprivation (try on life without guilt) brought "My daughter's life stopped, so now mine has to!" and imagined others' judgment ("beneath contempt"). Overt statement: "I agree with you all. I am to blame." Integration surfaced perfectionism. Symptom deprivation on it (imagine the feared mistake discovered) brought "humiliated and inadequate," and his idiosyncratic meaning of "inadequate" was sought.
- A stem on accepting that he couldn't stop it brought, past the positive endings, "I'd feel helpless at how unfair it all is." Self-blame protected him from existential powerlessness and preserved the construct that saving her was *possible*. A stem from within the resistance brought "you can do everything right and bad things still happen," which revealed the master construct "If you do everything right, bad things don't happen," inherited from a harsh father. It is a fourth-order ontological construct.
- **C–1–2–3**: a two-sided card; at the seaside he generated examples (Olympic runners, golf shots, the Titanic). "It wasn't my fault, was it, Paul?" brought grief and relief.
- **V**: a "reparative" week of apologies and repairs; his wife and daughter had never blamed him, which was a further JE. Follow-up at 8 weeks confirmed the change.

## Cliff: compulsive drinking

- The clinic protocol (craving medication plus CBT) failed; "I can't change" meant "I *mustn't* change."
- **B**: symptom deprivation with Focusing brought a hot knot saying "Run, avoid this. Don't talk to Melissa"; "Does that sound familiar?" led to his mother. "What would have happened if you had talked…?" brought "Then she would have won." Silence had been "the only real power I had." The therapist's speculative pro-symptom statement brought "If you help me, I'm going to be really angry." Two cards in his own words; he shared one with his wife; "I'm treating the medication as if it were my mother."
- **1–2–3**: the therapist stayed neutral, "non-helping" and noncounteractive, even through a 20%+ rise in drinking; any arm-wrestling let the client "win" from below. Being simply present ("Hey, I'm right here") brought "now I'm just the guy who's right here… and that feels like a lot and enough." An accepting presence, offered exactly when he was in touch with the need not to conform, disconfirmed the belief that power comes only through enfeeblement.
- **V**: he all but stopped drinking and took courses and work. The governing pro-symptom position was the master key; the symptoms fell away with no direct work on them.

## Debbie: compulsive eating, lifelong obesity

- **B**: in a guided mental experiment of losing the weight, "is that the *actual* experience you're having…?" brought a "yucky feeling." The stem "If I stop snacking…" brought "I won't be safe!" Curiosity about "a part of you that knows there's something unsafe" led to age 6 and sexual interest from an uncle and his friends: fat meant safety. Voiced together: "I've got to stay big—it's the only way to stay safe!" She relaxed and grew bigger in her chair. The card ended: "it's more important to stay fat and safe!"
- **C–1–2**: from the card alone, after a few days: "I'm at a different point in my life… and even if I did, I could do things about it now!"
- **V**: steady weight loss over years; no surgery needed.
- **Reading**: years of abuse therapy had never connected the trauma to *current* eating. Assume a breakthrough may be a few coherence-focused steps away despite a long, severe history.

## Emma: psychotic voices (diagnosed schizophrenia)

- After counteractive mindfulness and CBT helped little, the therapist focused on the *content* of the voices and what disability meant to her. EMDR served as a discovery tool through serial accessing: "I hate myself for being in this chair!", then a pro-symptom position (her worth = caring for others, so she deserves scorn). Cards followed. Resistance even to imagining being without depression was used as contact; the laugh of recognition marked the onset of change.
- **Error**: the voices stopped, and the therapist assumed the pro-symptom position was dissolved without checking her agency and purpose. They returned about four months later, as her father declined and the accident's anniversary neared.
- **Second round**: proceeding *as if* the voices were symptoms. The experiment of agreeing with them ("Yes, I can walk, I'm just faking") "kind of shut them up." Then symptom deprivation on "I can walk" brought "It's like it's all my fault": denial protected her from knowing she had carelessly caused her disablement. JEs with her compassionate "Emma the nurse" knowing ended the schema's encapsulation. An origin emerged later: at 9, she learned that displaying distress was weakness.
- **V**: no voices for a year, including the feared anniversary ("I think I'd know I was making them"); she never relapsed.

## A writer and painter: blocked self-expression, systemic racism (one demonstration session)

- **A–B**: guilt when painting, resentment when writing, so he did neither. "How would you put it?" offered autonomy. The memory of a stepmother's "Shut up, you have nothing important to say" surfaced; the "extra quality" question brought "it's *me* that has nothing important to say." The overt statement was calibrated so the therapist spoke it, with "Did your stepmother really pay attention to the substance…?" (No: devaluation without listening, as racism does). Connecting up then brought "A release, quite a release."
- **Painting**: hitting the wall on skin tones. As a child, "I could not find images of black people!" in museums. Connecting up: "I don't want to experience that pain." "What's making that difference?" showed his hope to be contradictory knowing ("I've done portraits and I was able to get through"), not positive thinking. The JE was repeated, framed with an athletic-conditioning analogy, and carried on emailed cards ("…I'll struggle and succeed").
- **V**: at 8 months, writing full steam; at 8 years, painting fluid, without guilt.

## Ross: lost voice, homophobic oppression (Coherent Narrative Therapy)

- **B**: "the earliest thing that most defined you" brought the kindergarten dress and "Don't be that way!", which he wept over within five minutes. Compassion blocked for himself came through the image of his own son ("I would be furious" / "That is what happened to you"). The schema generalized through the Deep South, a narcissistic father, a homophobic grandfather and a double life. Co-constructed: "If I'm truly myself, I'll lose everyone I love, and also my job! I'm safe only if I stay hidden."
- **C–1–2–3**: "…while also knowing that in fact you came out… married a man… have *not* lost your family" brought "I never before put those things together that way!" The same JE recurred in many contexts (a patronizing CFO: "*I* am the taller man!"; a new CEO who sounded like his father and grandfather; the "Leadership Monster"), with returns to kindergarten on slippage, free-writing prompts, and repair with his mother rehearsed by role reversal.
- **Reading**: "That *happened to* him, but that is not *who he is*." The marijuana was later addressed as another solution, after a "sacred pause."

## Jane: bossy inner rules, night panic (online sessions)

- **B**: the stem "If I can follow some rules, that's good, because then—" brought "That's *only* how I got praise." Asked how it felt in her body to have only that channel and "not the hug channel," she grew panicky; "we're not prepared to do *what*?" brought "To connect." She might never find real connection, so she must keep following rules.
- **C–1–2–3**: Realization Process heart-center attunement, offered to her younger part ("Whoa, this actually exists?"), plus recalled accepting neighbors, brought confusion and "mind-blowing." Resistance revealed a punishment schema; she grounded in the lower body, asked about present danger, and reframed "If there's anything nice or good, that's not my area" via sensitivity. The young self was "colored in."
- **V**: punishment "just not an issue anymore," but the connection schema still peaked at 8–9. Follow-up uncovered adoption and "I'm going to return you." Revisiting with the adult knowing that "it is Mom who is wrong," and saying "I will never return you" three times, brought "the charge has been taken out." No night panic at 13 months.

## Carlos: teen misdiagnosed with ADHD (four years, no medication)

- **B**: he sat still and attuned for a full hour. Symptom deprivation (imagine sitting like this in class) brought "No one would talk or listen to me" / "blend into the walls": acting up was the solution to invisibility, learned with an overtaxed aunt. Coherence empathy, depathologizing and a card he never read but remembered verbatim followed.
- **C–1–2–3**: repeated attention to his capacity for focus and connection; receiving quality attention just for being himself; a hard teacher's public praise; "I feel like I'm growing up." The therapist supported a coherent phase of not speaking at school, then the de-suppression of the original terror of separation.
- **V**: typical behavior, improved grades, college medication-free.

## Andy, 6: plunging moods (5 sessions)

- Skills work (counteractive) came first. Scaling revealed "when I think Mom died." Symptom deprivation failed even in imagination, so an overt statement to the imagined Mom: "I can't be happy because I'm worried you're going to die." "Well, that's true." He declined a card and repeated the sentence in his mind instead.
- **C**: "Well, but Mom always comes back and lives." An explicit JE, then real-time JE homework.
- **V**: "I'm 8-out-of-10 happy!"; "Just like that"; then 10/10, "Because I already know that she will not die"; at 9 months, "Did I think that?"

## Fourth-graders: self-blame for divorce (school group)

- Puppets. The Divorce Dragon: "No matter what they tell you, it IS your fault!" The therapist's puppet asked why: no kids, no money problems, no arguments. "do you think that people without kids ever get divorced?" "Of course they don't." The softly confided fact that lots do brought "They *do*???" The next meeting it was a non-issue: "my parents' divorce is not my fault."
