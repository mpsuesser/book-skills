# Humanize data

"Humans aren't good at understanding exponential growth." In 1994, at D. E. Shaw, Bezos read that web usage was growing 2,300 percent a year. Others shrugged; he saw that "things just don't grow that fast" and understood what it implied. Most people suffer from *exponential growth bias*, the habit of reading compounding growth as if it were linear. Tomatoes are an example. A plant that adds three tomatoes a day yields forty-two in two weeks. A plant on which each tomato becomes three the next day yields 1,594,323. "Data only leads to action if people understand the story the numbers tell."

The brain evolved to handle quantities of about one to seven, not zettabytes. "People have difficulty reasoning about magnitudes outside of human perception." It is an analogy machine, so rescale large numbers into things people already know.

## The procedure

1. **Hold your fire.** Adding more numbers overwhelms the audience; it doesn't persuade them.
2. **Choose your target.** Identify the single most important number your audience needs to know.
3. **Make it relatable.** Compare it to something familiar. The three most common comparisons are *size*, *distance* and *time*.

**Size (and weight).**

- Bezos's first catalog of books would have been "the size of more than forty New York City phone books."
- A physical store with that selection would cover six football fields.
- Forty-five thousand electronics items was about seven times a big-box store's selection.
- At Blue Origin he explained 3 percent compounding energy growth as doubling every twenty-five years. Today we could cover Nevada in solar cells to meet demand; within a couple of hundred years we would need to cover the entire Earth.
- Steve Jobs turned a five-gigabyte hard drive into "1,000 songs in your pocket" and pulled the iPod from his jeans.
- At a fusion laboratory, everyone was trained to use the same comparisons: 192 laser beams in a building the size of three football fields, aimed at a target the size of a pencil eraser.

**Distance.**

- An AWS executive said the Snowball appliances that carry customers' data to the cloud had traveled the equivalent of circling the world 250 times.
- Carl Sagan Planet Walk models the solar system at one to five billion. The sun and Earth are nine yards apart, Pluto is a fifteen-minute walk, and the nearest star, Alpha Centauri, sits 5,000 miles away in Hawaii. Those are distances people can *feel*.

**Time**, especially the time a customer saves. Bezos's 2020 letter works through it:

- 28 percent of Amazon purchases take three minutes or less, while a trip to a physical store takes about an hour.
- If each online purchase saves about fifteen minutes, and a customer skips a couple of trips a week, the savings come to more than seventy-five hours a year.
- Valued at $10 an hour, that is about $630 per Prime member each year.
- Across 200 million members, that is $126 billion in value created.

**Drill 19: put a number in context.** A Grande Mocha Frappuccino has about fifty-five grams of sugar. That number means little until you say it is about twelve teaspoons, or three fun-size packages of M&M's, which is how a nutritionist might persuade a client to cut back. Take your key number and translate it the same way.

## Wrap data in story

Choose a few key statistics, build stories around them, and give concrete examples that nonexperts can grasp. Reframe events as opportunities, because "if you don't tell your story, someone else will."

Bezos's 2020 letter made the case for value creation this way. Amazon had created $1.6 trillion of wealth for shareholders, and more than seven-eighths of it, $1.4 trillion, belonged to others: pension funds, universities and 401(k)s. He then quoted a letter from Mary and Larry, who had bought their son Ryan two shares in 1997. Through stock splits those shares became twenty-four, and they helped him buy a house. The aphorism followed: "create more than you consume." "By wrapping data in story, you make it far easier" for people to understand why the numbers matter.

**Show the value**, and sometimes show your work:

- **To investors:** how much they will make, when you will reach your goals, and when they will see a return.
- **To a new employer:** you raised sales 25 percent; say how, and how you will do it again.
- **To customers and employees:** the time, money or sales they gain.
