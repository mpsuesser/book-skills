# Transitions by category

Every entry of the book's 34 categories, each written in italics exactly as the book heads it, grouped within its category by the job it does, with how to use it wherever the phrase alone does not show that. The number after a category's name is its entry count. Category definitions and the tests that separate neighboring categories are in `SKILL.md`.

How to read an entry:

- `...` marks a slot in a **paired or multi-part frame**; each slot takes a word, a clause or a whole sentence (*Both ... and ...*; *Not only ... but also ...*; *First, ... next, ... finally, ...*).
- An entry that stops mid-phrase is a **fragment**: it opens a phrase completed by the next word (*In a similar* way / display; *On further* examination; *If at any* time).
- `/` gives interchangeable forms (*Seeing as/that*); `(archaic)` is the book's own marking.

## 1. Addition (62)

- Openers and links that add the next item: *Additionally*, *Also*, *And*, *And then*, *Furthermore*, *In addition*, *Moreover*, *Plus*, *To add to that*, *What is more*, *For another thing*, *Finally*, *Equally important*, *Even more*, *More broadly*, *At the same time*, *On top of that*, *Not to mention* (a clinching extra).
- Join a second item to the first: *Along with*, *As well as*, *Coupled with*, *In conjunction with*, *Together with*; *In addition to*, *Aside from*, *Aside from this* and *Besides* name the first item and let "also" carry the second.
- Repeat a pattern: *Again*, *Once again*, *Once more*. *And ultimately* carries a gradual change to its end. *More rarely* adds to a negative frequency clause (I seldom X, and even more rarely Y).
- Paired frames: *Both ... and ...*, *Not only ... but ...*, *Not only ... but also ...*. *But also among* completes a clause that opened "not only among X".
- Fragments that take the next noun: *A further* and *Further* (evidence, example), *On further* (examination), *Other* (other X include...), *Another* (reason, point).
- *Additions include* lists the newcomers to a set just announced. *Including* opens a list of members inside the sentence.
- Clause-final: *As well*, *Too*, *To boot* (informal). *Or* adds a choice inside the list (black or blue).
- *Nor* follows a negative clause and inverts: ..., nor do we pay X. *So too* inverts the same way after a claim: X may be up, but so too is Y.
- *Still another* is the third member of a one ..., another ..., still another ... series.
- *More importantly*, *More tellingly*, *More ominously*, *More to the point*, *More than anything*, *More than anything else*, *More than that* add a point and rank or color it above the last one.
- *Also on* and *But with* repeat a preposition from the clause before: on a television program, and also on a magazine cover; not like a coward, but with a scream.
- *Next* presents the next item (Next we have X); *Next to that* means besides that.
- *Incidentally* adds a by-the-way fact.

## 2. Alternative (25)

- Paired frames: *Either ... or ...*, *Neither ... nor ...*, *Whether ... or ...*, *On the one hand ... on the other hand ...* (the second half often opens "but on the other hand"), *While ... is ...* (grant one option, prefer the other: while A is good, B is better).
- Choice inside one clause: *Between* (two endpoints), *In either* (my right hand or left), *Either or both* (+ plural), *Instead of* and *In lieu of* (one in place of the other), *Rather* (would you rather X).
- Tags that append a second option after the first: *Nor* (after a negative: hadn't been eating nor sleeping), *Or*, *Or already*, *Or another*, *Or else*, *Or even*, *Or instead*. In the book's entry *Or so* joins a second "so"-phrase (so big or so complicated), not "approximately".
- Sentence openers that offer the substitute: *Alternatively*, *Another* (option is), *Instead*, *However* (one option allowed, the preferred one follows), *Otherwise* (do this, otherwise that: an ultimatum), *In other respects* (an alternative view of the same person or thing).

## 3. Apposition (14)

- The precise renamers: *Namely* names the thing just described; *That is to say* restates more exactly (next Tuesday, that is to say, the date); *In other words* restates in plainer terms.
- The rest attach a statement that modifies the one before: *And* (+ participial aside), *Because*, *For*, *In order to* (reason or purpose attached), *Furthermore*, *Likewise* (= also, inside the clause), *Similarly*, *On the other hand*, *In any event*, *To sum up*, *Yet*.

## 4. Cause or Reason (46)

- Paired frame: *If ... then ...*.
- Graded causes, by how much the cause explains: *In part because*, *Partly because*, *Partly due to*, *Part of the reason for*, *Perhaps because* (partial or tentative); *Mostly caused by*; *Because*, *Because of*, *Due to*, *Owing to*, *On account of*, *Since*, *As* (full).
- Formal or heavy: *Due to the fact that*, *For the reason that*, *Inasmuch as*, *By cause of*, *Being that* (the reason being that...); archaic: *Forasmuch as (archaic)*. *Seeing as/that* takes either form.
- Circumstance as cause: *In light of*, *In view of*, *In view of this*, *Given that*, *In response to*, *Now that* (the changed situation is the reason).
- *In that* specifies the respect in which a claim holds (dangerous in that he was X). *As for* and *As for those* turn to a group and give its reason or outcome.
- Result-facing entries filed here: *As a result*, *Resulting in* (+ noun, tacked onto the cause), *Thus* (+ -ing: thus avoiding X), *Thus both* (+ X and Y), *Hence*, *Therefore*, *So*, *So that*, *So as a result*, *That is why*, *It follows that*, *And so*, *For that* (= for that reason).
- *For* also appears as the bare preposition (responsible for X); *Because such* takes a noun; *In all likelihood* marks the cause as probable.

## 5. Chain (35)

- Every entry is a frame whose pieces carry the series through clauses or sentences. Choose by the shape of the series:
- Counting points: *First and foremost, ... second, ... third, ...*; *First of all, ... secondly, ...*; *First, ... next, ... finally, ...* (the steps of a process); *First, ... second, ... finally, ...*; *Firstly, ... secondly, ... lastly, ...*; *Generally, ... furthermore, ... finally, ...*; *In the first place ... in the second place ...*; *On the first point ... on the second point ...*; *Pursuing this further ... finally ...*.
- Weighing two sides: *On one hand ... on the other hand ...*, *On the one hand ... on the other hand ...*, *On one level ... on the other level ...*, *On one side ... on the other side ...*, *The former ... the latter ...* (refers back to two named items in order), *While there ... there ...* (there is little X, there is still Y).
- Two causes or means: *As much because of ... as because ...*, *Either because ... or ...*, *Either through ... or ...*, *Not just because ... but also because ...*, *Because ... therefore ...* (cause in one sentence, its consequence in the next).
- Piling on: *Aside from ... also ...*, *In addition to ... also ...*, *Not just ... but also ...*, *Not only ... but (pronoun) also ...* (the pronoun sits between: but I'm also), *Basically ... similarly ... as well ...* (three sentences), *Only ... and also ...* (only by X, and also Y, will you...; inverts after "only").
- Correcting or limiting: *Not ... but ...* (not X but Y), *Sometimes ... but not always ...*, *Nothing ... nor ...* (the second sentence opens "Nor will..."), *Without either ... or ...* (both absent).
- Change and span: *Before ... now* (the earlier state, then the present one), *From ... to ...*, *From that ... in that ...* (from that point ..., in that ...).
- Options: *Whether ... or ...* (two options), *Whether ... or whether ...* (runs through instances: whether it's X, or whether it's Y).

## 6. Clarification (27)

- The definition names two moves: add details, or restate in a different way.
- Narrow to specifics: *Especially*, *In particular*, *Mainly*, *Namely*, *Particularly*, *Specifically* (all follow a general claim and single out part of it), *In this case* (general term, then the instance: a court of law; in this case the Supreme Court), *i.e.* (parenthetical).
- Give a case: *For example*, *For instance*, *One case that* (springs to mind is X), *To illustrate the point*.
- Restate: *In other words*, *That is*, *That is to say*, *Stated differently*, *To put it in another way*, *To rephrase it*, *Another way* (of understanding it is that...), *Put very simply*, *Simply stated*, *I mean* (conversational).
- Correct the record: *Actually*, *In actuality* (the truer or stronger version of what was just said).
- *For clarity* sends the reader to a fuller source. *Seems clear from this* draws the clarifying inference (it seems clear from this that...). *Up to a point* limits how far a claim holds (it works up to a point, but...).

## 7. Comparison (37)

- The category covers likeness and difference alike.
- Likeness: *Alike* (clause-final: children and adults alike), *Also*, *Analogous to*, *Another ... like* (another X, just like the last one), *As well as*, *By the same token*, *In a similar* (+ noun), *In conjunction with this*, *In similar fashion*, *In the same manner* (as), *In the same way*, *Just as ... so too* (inverts: so too can humans), *Likewise*, *Similarly*.
- Measuring against a benchmark: *By comparison*, *By way of comparison*, *As a way of comparison*, *For comparison* (each sets a second figure beside one already given), *Compared to* (as compared to), *More than*, *Less than*, *More than any other*, *To a lesser extent*, *Even* and *Even against* (the strongest case in the comparison).
- Difference: *But*, *But another*, *Conversely*, *On the other hand*, *Whereas* (whereas A holds X, B holds Y), *Rather*, *Rather than*, *Meanwhile* (cuts to another place or party: meanwhile in X), *Against* and *Up against* (one measured against the other), *Balanced against* (a benefit weighed against its cost).
- *Accordingly* is filed here too: a consequence that matches its premise.

## 8. Concession (29)

- Shape: yield point A, then hold point B.
- A, then B: *Admittedly*, *Granted*, *Naturally* and *It may be true that* open the conceded point, with "but" bringing the held one. *Be that as it may* follows a conceded merit and reasserts the fault. *After all* gives the reason for yielding (I forgave him; after all, nobody is perfect).
- Subordinators that carry the conceded clause: *Although*, *Although after*, *Even though*, *Though*, *Despite the fact that*, *In spite of*, *Notwithstanding* (can trail its noun: notwithstanding his illness), *Regardless* (of), *Albeit* (+ a phrase: albeit with apprehension); archaic: *Despite of (archaic)*.
- Openers for the held point after the concession: *All the same*, *And yet*, *But even so*, *Even so*, *However*, *Nevertheless*, *Nonetheless* (both often clause-final), *Still*, *Yet*, *On the other hand*, *In any case* (the fallback that holds regardless).
- *Granted that* works as a condition (= provided that); *Granted* alone concedes. *In turn* (a knock-on consequence) is filed here.

## 9. Conclusion (40)

- Wrap up the series: *All in all*, *In closing*, *In conclusion*, *In short*, *In summary*, *In the end*, *In the last analysis*, *Overall*, *Simply put*, *To conclude*, *To sum up*, *Ultimately*.
- Close on the last item: *As a final comment*, *As a final point*, *Finally*, *Last but not least*, *Lastly*.
- Draw the conclusion the statements support: *Accordingly*, *As a result*, *Consequently*, *Due to* (this), *Hence*, *Implies that* (all of this implies that...), *It follows that*, *It is obvious that*, *Logical conclusion is* (the only logical conclusion is that...), *That being the case*, *Therefore*, *Thus*, *Under those circumstances* (can open a rhetorical question), *So perhaps* (tentative), *Then*.
- Close on a condition: *If so*, *If not*, *In that case*, *Otherwise* (follow X, otherwise suffer Y).
- Look back: *At last*, *In retrospect*.
- Archaic: *Whence (Archaic)* (from where: whence comes X?), *Wherefore (Archaic)* (= therefore, joining two clauses).

## 10. Condition (25)

- The restrictive "only" set, usually after "but": *Only after*, *Only by*, *Only if*, *Only until*; *Only then* inverts (only then does X need to...).
- Stipulations: *On condition that*, *On the condition that*, *Provided that*, *So long as*, *As long as*, *Given that*.
- Contingencies: *If*, *If however*, *If indeed* (if the premise really holds, then...), *In case* (a precaution against X), *In the event* (of), *In that event*, *Unless*.
- Fragments: *If anything* (if anything happens), *If as* (if as a species we...), *If at any* (time).
- Conditions that leave the outcome unchanged: *Even if*, *Even under* (+ threat or pressure).
- Filed here too: *As if* (manner, as though X were true) and *Just so* (= just so that, purpose).

## 11. Conflict (21)

- Two parties holding opposite positions: *Whereas*, *While*, *Conversely*, *In contrast*, *By way of contrast* (can set a second figure against the first).
- Claim against fact: *To the contrary* (rejects a negated claim and asserts its opposite), *When in fact* (pretense, then reality), *On the other hand*.
- Tone-bearing: *But alas* (regret at the failure), *Ironically* (the outcome inverts what the first fact led you to expect), *Paradoxically* (contradictory yet true), *But then* (deflates a claim as unremarkable: X is pretty, but then so are many others).
- Conflict carried in a clause: *Although*, *Even though*, *Though* (these three often trail as afterthoughts), *But*, *However*, *Nonetheless*, *Still*, *Yet*.
- *Lest* = for fear that, + subjunctive (lest it be confiscated).

## 12. Continuation (28)

- The definition covers carrying on and bringing a topic to a close.
- The next event: *After* (that), *Afterwards*, *Immediately thereafter*, *Next*, *Not long after*, *Subsequently*, *Then*, *Consequently*, *The next step* (procedures).
- From here on: *Henceforward*, *Hereafter*.
- Push the argument further: *Following this further*, *Pursuing this further*, *In furtherance of*, *In the same respect*, *In the same way*, *Together with* (+ an earlier item), *With that in mind* (turns to action), *So again* (returns to the central question).
- Take stock: *So far*, *Thus far*, *Up to now* (so far we've covered X; now Y).
- Pick up a topic: *As* (as you've seen), *As far as* (+ a question word: as far as why you stayed...), *As long as* (= while we're on the subject: as long as we're talking about X...).
- *In the meanwhile* runs another strand alongside; *In the next* (+ noun: frame, step) moves to the next unit.
- Close: *Last of all*.

## 13. Contrast (38)

- The definition's mark is "the goal of bringing them together": contrast sets two conflicting ideas in one view, usually both true at once.
- Paired frames: *Before ... now ...* (the former state, then the present), *On the one hand ... on the other hand ...*, *Some ... others ...* (some do X, while others Y).
- A single exception to a general claim: *Apart from the fact* (that), *Aside from*.
- Hold both despite the conflict: *All the same*, *Although*, *Despite*, *Despite the contrary* (+ being proven), *Even so*, *Even though*, *However*, *In spite of*, *Nevertheless*, *Nonetheless*, *Notwithstanding* (can follow its noun: the late hailstorm notwithstanding), *Still*, *While this may be true* (limit a claim to a minority, then assert the majority case), *Yet* (so near, and yet so far).
- Set side by side: *As opposed to*, *By contrast*, *Contrarily*, *Conversely*, *Counter to* (+ claims), *Different from*, *In contrast*, *In the meantime* (two situations at once), *Whereas*, *Whilst*, *In another sense* (in one way X, but in another sense Y), *Instead* (of).
- Reversal: *On the contrary* (rebuts a stated view), *But on the contrary* (not X, but on the contrary Y).
- Others: *Alas*, *But*, *But at the same time*, *But even* (+ subject: but even they won't...), *Otherwise* (adjectival: an otherwise dry weekend).

## 14. Effect or Result (24)

- Consequence openers: *Accordingly*, *As a consequence*, *As a result*, *Consequently*, *For this reason*, *Hence*, *In consequence*, *Therefore*, *Thus*, *So*.
- Result tacked onto the cause with -ing: *Finally* (finally putting an end to X), *Thereby perhaps* (tentative), *Thuswise*.
- Result named as such: *The consequence of* (X was the consequence of Y), *In effect* (what it amounted to), *It turns out that* (the outcome discovered), *In the wake of* (the aftermath), *So much so that* (the degree reached; can open a fragment after the claim).
- Cause-facing: *Because of*, *Due in part to*.
- Intended result: *So as to*, *So that*.
- *If and only if* ties the result to exactly that condition. *So despite* gives a result that holds in spite of a setback.

## 15. Emphasis (43)

- Many sit inside the clause rather than opening it: *Absolutely*, *Always*, *Certainly*, *Definitely*, *Emphatically*, *Eternally*, *Forever* (clause-final), *Specifically*, *Undeniably*, *Undoubtedly*, *Unequivocally*, *Unquestionably*, *Without a doubt* (parenthetical). *Indeed* and *Whatsoever* follow what they stress (rare indeed; no fear whatsoever).
- Negative emphasis: *By no means*, *Never* (fronted, it inverts: never could I...), *Whatsoever* (after a negative).
- Fact-asserting openers: *As a matter of fact*, *In fact*, *In point of fact*, *Of course*, *Obviously*, *Naturally*, *Surely*, *For sure*, *Strictly speaking*, *Surprisingly*, *Peculiarly*, *Without reservation*.
- Singling out: *Above all*, *Above all else*, *Mainly*, *More importantly*, *Particularly*, *More specifically*, *Even more specifically*, *Even more* (+ adjective).
- *As ... as ...* is the simile intensifier (as X as a Y). *By all means* is emphatic permission. *Even that* stresses the last thing left standing. *Besides* can open a dismissive question (besides, how would you...?). *In any case* and *Nevertheless* are filed here too.

## 16. Evidence, Proof, and Facts (34)

- Cite the source: *As noted by*, *In accordance with*, *In agreement* (with), *This coincides with*.
- Name the method: *Empirically*, *By similar argumentation*, *Analogously*.
- Infer from the facts: *Given these facts*, *On this basis*, *In view of*, *Since*, *This implies that*, *Evidently*, *As can be expected*, *For this reason*, *For the same reason*, *As a consequence* (of that).
- Weigh the whole case: *All things considered*, *At the end of the day*, *In the final analysis*, *In the overall analysis*, *In hindsight*.
- Point to the strongest evidence: *Most importantly*, *Of greatest significance* (inverts: of greatest significance are the bank statements), *Another significant* (+ noun), *One such* (+ noun that bears this out).
- Scope the claim: *In all instances*, *In such cases*, *Such a case* (in such a case), *In terms of*.
- Others: *Another possibility would be*, *In fact*, *In simple terms*, *That is* (to say).

## 17. Illustration and Description (29)

- Introduce an example: *A case in point* (is that of X), *An example of*, *Another example of*, *As an illustration*, *By way of example*, *For example*, *For instance* (also mid-sentence after the subject), *In another case*, *On this occasion*, *Take the case of* (+ a named case), *The following example*, *To illustrate*.
- Point at support the reader can see: *As can be seen* (in the following graph), *As demonstrated by*, *As illustrated by*, *As shown* (by), *As you can see*.
- Single out members of a set: *Especially*, *In particular*, *Namely*, *Notably* (most notably X and Y), *Particularly*, *Specifically*, *Such as*.
- *For one thing* pairs with "For another". *In this case* and *In this situation* fix the circumstances the detail applies to. *Thus* can open a verbless fragment (thus, the need for X). *In fact* adds the telling detail.

## 18. Exception (12)

- Exclude: *Except for*, *Other than*.
- An occasional exception to a general picture: *Once in a while*, *Sometimes*.
- Object to what precedes: *Besides* (a further objection), *Despite*, *In spite of*, *However* (+ adjective: however bad the result), *Nevertheless*, *Nonetheless*, *Still*, *Yet*.

## 19. Generalization (21)

- Rule statements: *As a general rule*, *As a rule*, *As a rule of thumb* (practical advice), *In principle* (as a matter of principle).
- Proportion hedges: *For the most part*, *Generally*, *In many cases*, *In most cases*, *In most instances*, *On the whole*, *Ordinarily*, *Usually*.
- Widen the frame: *Broadly speaking*, *Generally speaking*, *In general*, *More generally*, *On a more general* (level), *As a whole* (the group taken together), *At all* (levels).
- *As usual* is habitual and clause-final. *Generally through* takes a time span (generally through the early evening hours).

## 20. Importance (19)

- The ranking runs from *Of least importance*, through *No less importantly*, *More important*, *More importantly* and *Of greater importance*, to *Most important*, *Most importantly*, *Most of all*, *Above all* and *Foremost*. The book lists the adjective and adverb forms (*More important* / *More importantly*, *Most important* / *Most importantly*) as separate entries.
- A last item that still carries weight: *Last but not least*, *Last but by no means least*.
- The main share of a group: *Chiefly*, *Largely*, *Mainly*, *Predominantly*, *Primarily*, *Principally* (after a group, name its biggest part: opposition, chiefly from X).
- *Foremost* also works as an adjective (foremost authority in X). *Foremost among* and *Of greater importance* invert (foremost among these is X).

## 21. Introduction (27)

- Set the topic: *As for*, *Concerning the ...*, *With regard to*, *With respect to*, *In this* (+ noun: in this course).
- Open on the first point: *First and foremost*, *First of all*, *For one thing*, *To begin with*.
- A first impression to be overturned: *At a glance*, *At first glance* (followed by "but" or "you may be forgiven for thinking"), *First and yet* (scared at first and yet went ahead).
- Frame the scope or level: *At this level*, *Generally speaking*, *In general*, *In a nutshell*.
- Frame the stance or confidence: *Admittedly*, *Assuredly*, *Certainly*, *In my opinion*, *In all likelihood*.
- Open on a precedent: *As in the case of* (+ an earlier case).
- Open on a time: *At the beginning* (of), *On occasion*.
- Open on an added item: *Likewise*, *Yet another* (+ noun), *To illustrate* (how to...).

## 22. Logical (21)

- Infer: *It follows that*, *It must be that* (if X, it must be that Y), *Implies that*, *This being so*, *For this reason*, *And so*, *Such that* (the outcome reached), *Thereby* (+ -ing).
- Branch on a condition: *If so* (after a yes/no question), *If not*, *In that case*, *Otherwise* (an ultimatum), *Provided that*, *Whether ... or ...* (the conclusion holds either way).
- Test a truth claim: *It is true that*, *While this is true* (concede, then counter), *When in fact*.
- What follows from now on: *From now* (on), *Henceforth*, *Henceforward*.
- *And then*.

## 23. Purpose (16)

- Openers that point back at the aim just stated: *To that end*, *To this end*, *To this aim*, *Toward this end*, *Towards this goal*, *For this purpose*, *With this in mind*, *For this reason*, *Because of this*.
- Purpose attached inside the sentence: *In order to* (+ verb), *In order that* (+ clause), *So that*, *In the hope that*, *With the goal of* (+ -ing), *The furtherance of* (for the furtherance of X).
- Negative purpose: *For fear that* (hide for fear that X would find us).

## 24. Qualifiers (30)

- Limit: *Almost*, *Almost always*, *Almost as* (+ adjective + as), *Almost exclusively*, *Nearly*, *Just about*, *Just over* (+ an amount), *Partly*, *Some*, *For some*, *In some respects*, *Insofar as* (... is concerned), *To a large extent*, *Less commonly* (+ adjective), *Rarely* (very rarely do X: inverts), *Too seldom*.
- Probability: *May be* (two words, the modal: may be released), *Might*, *Perhaps*, *As perhaps* (described as perhaps the greatest), *Probably*, *Very possibly*.
- Absolute frequency: *Always*, *Never*, *Frequently*.
- Enhance: *But more importantly*, *So much* (of), *So much so* (that), *Somehow* (an unexplained success), *In somewhat* (+ adjective + fashion).

## 25. Quantifiers (44)

- Small amounts: *A bit of*, *A couple of*, *A few*, *A little*, *A lack of*, *Not much* (+ comparative), *None*, *Little by little* (gradual).
- Large amounts: *A good deal of*, *A great deal of* (with mass nouns: patience, strength), *A lot of*, *Lots of* (informal), *Plenty of*, *Many*, *Several*, *Most*, *Much more*, *Far more than*.
- The whole: *All*, *All of the*, *Almost all*, *For all* (+ a number), *For any*, *For each*, *For every*.
- Part of a domain: *For most of*, *For much of*, *In most of*, *In much of*.
- Bounds: *As much as*, *At least*, *At most*, *Enough*.
- Comparatives: *Even fewer*, *Even lower*, *Increasingly* (+ comparative), *Less*, *Lesser*, *Lower*, *More* (than half), *Much less*.
- *Evenly* (distributed), *Minus* (everyone, minus one), *Some*.

## 26. Reference (30)

- Point within the document: *As described above*, *As described below*, *As detailed above*, *As noted above*, *Set forth* (as set forth in), *At one point* (in the article), *In a subsequent* (+ report), *In the latter case*.
- Attribute to a source: *According to*, *As acknowledged* (in), *As told by*, *Considering this* (+ a figure).
- Name the topic: *About*, *As for*, *As regards*, *Concerning this*, *In respect to*, *On the subject of*, *Regarding*, *Speaking about*, *With regard to*, *With respect to*, *In the case of*.
- Tie to an event: *After* (news broke), *Amid* (claims).
- Tie to a class of cases: *As with* (all previous X), *In such* (cases as), *In some* (+ noun), *In reference* (to), *In relation to*.

## 27. Reformulation (17)

- Recall the writer's own earlier words: *As I have noted*, *As I stated*, *As indicated above*, *As noted earlier*, *As previously stated*.
- The same point in other words: *In other words*, *Put differently*, *To put it differently*, *Let me rephrase it/that* (a colon follows).
- Simpler: *Put simply*, *Put more simply*, *To put it simply*, *Or simply put*.
- Shorter or sharper: *In brief*, *In short*, *On the whole*, *In point of fact*.

## 28. Reservation (9)

- The writer voices a doubt or a cost and goes on anyway: *Admittedly* (the salary was not great), *Even so* (I'm a little afraid; even so, I'd like to try), *Despite*, *Even though*, *Nevertheless* (it may seem extreme, but nevertheless...), *Notwithstanding*, *Regardless* (of), *Regardless of whether* (... or not), *Indeed* (a guarded affirmation).

## 29. Resumption (21)

- Return after a digression: *As I was saying*, *Back to* (what I said), *To continue* (what I was saying), *To get back to the point*, *To resume* (what X said), *To return to the subject* (of).
- Set the digression aside and carry on: *Anyhow*, *Anyway*, *At any rate*, *All the same*, *But in any case*, *In any* (event), *Either way*, *In either case*, *Whatever* (the outcome), *Whichever* (way you look at it).
- Side remark: *By the way*, *Incidentally*. Also filed here: *Needless to say*, *In retrospect*, *To put it in another way*.
- Register: *Anyhow*, *Anyway* and *By the way* are conversational; *To resume* and *To return to the subject* are formal.

## 30. Sequence or Order (31)

- Ordinals: *First of all*, *Firstly*, *Secondly*, *Thirdly*, *Lastly*, *Finally*, *In the first place*, *To begin with*, *For a start* (informal).
- Narrative order: *After* (that), *Afterward*, *And* (then), *At first*, *At the outset*, *At this point*, *Before this*, *Concurrently*, *In the beginning*, *In the end*, *Initially*, *Later on*, *Next*, *Previously*, *Subsequently*, *Then*.
- Logical order: *Consequently*, *Hence*, *Therefore*.
- Added in order: *Also*, *Still further*, *On the other hand*.

## 31. Similarity (29)
